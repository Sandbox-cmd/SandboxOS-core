# notes 3 of 8 · surahs 7 to 10 (al-a'raf, al-anfal, at-tawbah, yunus) · abdel haleem

## the ten axioms in this chunk

none of the ten anchor verses (54:17, 96:6-7, 33:72, 21:22, 55:7-9, 2:222, 47:24, 61:2-3, 4:135, 21:47) falls inside surahs 7 to 10. but five of them have close kin here that the maker has not used yet:

- the scales (21:47) have a twin in 7:8-9: "On that Day the weighing of deeds will be true and just: those whose good deeds are heavy on the scales will be the ones to prosper". 21:47 [haleem] reads "We will set up scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection so that no one can be wronged in the least, and if there should be even the weight of a mustard seed, We shall bring it out". 7:8 adds the words "true and just" to the weighing itself.
- the balance (55:7-9, haleem: "weigh with justice and do not fall short in the balance") is made concrete in the market by 7:85: "Give full measure and weight and do not undervalue people’s goods". the cosmic balance of surah 55 becomes a shopkeeper's scale here, and the same verse ties it to not corrupting what has been set right.
- the trust (33:72, haleem: "We offered the Trust to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains, yet they refused to undertake it") has its practical command in 8:27: "Believers, do not betray God and the Messenger, or knowingly betray [other people’s] trust in you". 33:72 says what the trust is; 8:27 says what betraying it looks like in daily life, and the word "knowingly" makes it a matter of conscience rather than accident.
- locks on the hearts (47:24, haleem: "Will they not contemplate the Quran? Do they have locks on their hearts") is echoed as a refrain in this chunk: 7:169 "‘Why do you not use your reason?’", 8:22 "the worst creatures in God’s eyes are those who are [wilfully] deaf and dumb, who do not reason", 10:16 "How can you not use your reason?", 10:100 "He brings disgrace on those who do not use their reason".
- saying without doing (61:2-3, haleem: "why do you say things and then do not do them") sits behind the whole hypocrite sequence of surah 9, and its positive form is 9:105: "Take action! God will see your actions".
- 13:11 (haleem: "God does not change the condition of a people [for the worse] unless they change what is in themselves") has a sibling in 8:53 that the maker has not used: "God would never change a favour He had conferred on a people unless they changed what was within themselves". the two verses say the same law from both sides.

## 1. knowledge and reading

- 7:28 "they say, ‘We found our forefathers doing this,’ and, ‘God has commanded us to do this.’ Say [Prophet], ‘God does not command disgraceful deeds. How can you say about God things that you do not know [to be true]?’" · inherited custom is dressed up as divine command, and the reply is not a counter-doctrine but a question about warrant: you do not know that. a manifesto about defaults can use this exact move.
- 7:33 "My Lord only forbids disgraceful deeds- whether they be open or hidden- and sin and unjustified aggression, and that you, without His sanction, associate things with Him, and that you say things about Him without knowledge." · speaking without knowledge is listed in the same breath as aggression; asserting what you cannot back is treated as a harm, not a slip.
- 7:52 "We have brought people a Scripture- We have explained it on the basis of true knowledge- as guidance and mercy" · the book presents itself as explanation grounded in knowledge, which is the posture sandbox wants for its publications.
- 7:71 "Are you arguing with me about mere names you and your forefathers invented, names for which God has given no sanction? Just wait; I too am waiting." · the argument is about names with no warrant behind them; a tool for testing a default is to ask who sanctioned it.
- 7:146 "I will keep distracted from My signs those who behave arrogantly on Earth without any right, and who, even if they see every sign, will not believe in them" · arrogance is named as the thing that blocks seeing; the failure to learn is moral before it is intellectual.
- 7:169 "Was a pledge not taken from them, writ-ten in the Scripture, to say nothing but the truth about God? And they have studied its contents well. For those who are mindful of God, the Hereafter is better. ‘Why do you not use your reason?’" · people who inherited the book and studied it well still took "the fleeting gains of this lower world"; study without reason and without honesty is possible, and this verse names it.
- 7:172-173 "So you cannot say on the Day of Resurrection, ‘We were not aware of this,’ ... or, ‘It was our forefathers who, before us, ascribed partners to God, and we are only the descendants who came after them" · the forefather excuse is refused in advance; each person is treated as a witness in their own right.
- 7:179 "with hearts they do not use for comprehension, eyes they do not use for sight, ears they do not use for hearing. They are like cattle, no, even further astray: these are the ones who are entirely heedless" · the faculties are there; the failure is not using them. the manifesto can say the tools are already in people's hands.
- 7:185 [itani, via verse.py, because the haleem and sahih lines carry a word the house forbids] "Have they not observed the government of the heavens and the earth, and all the things that God created, and that their time may have drawn near? Which message, besides this, will they believe in" · contemplation of the world and awareness of a short span are put in one sentence.
- 8:21-22 "do not be like those who say, ‘We heard,’ though in fact they were not listening ... the worst creatures in God’s eyes are those who are [wilfully] deaf and dumb, who do not reason" · the strongest condemnation in the chunk is aimed at wilful non-listening, not at ignorance.
- 8:29 "Believers, if you remain mindful of God, He will give you a criterion [to tell right from wrong]" · the criterion (furqan) is given as a result of conduct, not of cleverness.
- 9:6 "grant it to him so that he may hear the word of God, then take him to a place safe for him, for they are people with no knowledge [of it]" · even an enemy in wartime is to be given safe passage to hear, and then escorted to safety, because he lacks knowledge; access to the message is owed to those who do not have it.
- 9:11 "We make the messages clear for people who are willing to learn" · clarity is promised to willingness.
- 9:122 "out of each community, a group should go out to gain understanding of the religion, so that they can teach their people when they return and so that they can guard themselves against evil" · a charter for a research group: some go out to learn, come back, and teach. this is the closest thing in the chunk to a description of what sandbox says it does.
- 9:127 "God has turned their hearts away because they are people who do not use their reason"
- 10:5 "determining phases for it so that you might know the number of years and how to calculate time. God did not create all these without a true purpose; He explains His signs to those who understand" · astronomy and arithmetic are named as the reason the moon has phases; calculation is a permitted, intended reading of nature.
- 10:16 "I lived a whole lifetime among you before it came to me. How can you not use your reason?" · the messenger's argument is his known track record; reason is asked to weigh a life, not a claim.
- 10:36 "Most of them follow nothing but assumptions, but assumptions can be of no value at all against the Truth" · conjecture versus knowledge, stated flat.
- 10:39 "But they are denying what they cannot comprehend- its prophecy has yet to be fulfilled for them." · denial of what one does not understand is named as a pattern.
- 10:42-43 "can you make the deaf hear if they will not use their minds ... can you guide the blind if they will not see" · the limit of any publisher: you cannot make anyone read.
- 10:66 "Those who call upon others beside God are not really following partner-gods; they are only following assumptions and telling lies" · false gods are reduced to assumptions plus lies.
- 10:68 "You have no authority to say this. How dare you say things about God without any knowledge"
- 10:89 "stay on the right course, and do not follow the path of those who do not know"
- 10:93 "It was only after knowledge had come to them that they began to differ among themselves." · a warning inside the knowledge theme: knowledge is the occasion of division, not a cure for it.
- 10:94 "ask those who have been reading the scriptures before you" · even the prophet is told to check with prior readers; verification by consulting those who have read.
- 10:101 "Say, ‘Look at what is in the heavens and on the earth.’ But what use are signs and warnings to people who will not believe" · the command is to look; the honest caveat is that looking does not compel.

## 2. proof and truth

- 7:105-106 "duty-bound to say nothing about God but the truth, and I have brought you a clear sign from your Lord. Let the Children of Israel go with me.’ ... He said, ‘Produce this sign you have brought, if you are telling the truth.’" · the claim, the demand for proof, and the demonstration follow in order; even pharaoh's demand for evidence is honoured.
- 7:116-118 "casting a spell on people’s eyes, striking fear into them, and bringing about great sorcery ... it devoured their fakery ... The truth was confirmed and what they had produced came to nothing" · fakery works on eyes and through fear; the true thing does not argue with it, it consumes it. a working demonstration beats a narrative.
- 8:8 "to prove the Truth to be true, and the false to be false, much as the guilty might dislike it" · proof is meant to land on both sides: confirm the true and expose the false, regardless of who dislikes it.
- 8:42 "so that those who were to die might die after seeing a clear proof, and so that those who were to live might live after seeing a clear proof" · the outcome of the day is arranged so that everyone acts after seeing evidence.
- 9:43 "Why did you give them permission to stay at home before it had become clear to you which of them spoke the truth and which were liars" · the prophet himself is reproached for accepting excuses before verifying them; a leader who trusts a report unchecked is corrected in the text.
- 9:94 "Say, ‘Do not make excuses. We do not believe you: God has told us about you. God and His Messenger will watch your actions now" · the response to unverifiable talk is to watch conduct from here on.
- 9:107 "they swear, ‘Our intentions were nothing but good,’ but God bears witness that they are liars" · stated intention is not accepted as evidence; the building's purpose is judged by what it does.
- 9:119 "You who believe, be mindful of God: stand with those who are true"
- 10:21 "record all your scheming" and 10:61 "Not even the weight of a speck of dust in the earth or sky escapes your Lord, nor anything lesser or greater: it is all written in a clear record" · a written record of everything down to a speck; the ledger is the model of accountability.
- 10:32 "That is God, your Lord, the Truth. Apart from the Truth, what is there except error?" · the binary is stated: truth or error, no third thing.
- 10:38 "Say, ‘Then produce a sura like it, and call on anyone you can beside God if you are telling the truth.’" · the challenge to produce a comparable artefact is the proof offered; make one, then talk.
- 10:81 "Everything you have brought is sorcery and God will show it to be false. God does not make the work of mischief-makers right" · the work of mischief will not be made to hold; time is on the side of the thing that is true.
- 9:16 "without God identifying which of you will strive for His cause" · testing is how claims are sorted.
- 8:6 "argued with you about the truth after it had been made clear, as if they were being driven towards a death they could see with their own eyes" · arguing after the evidence is in is named as a failure mode.

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship, custody

- 7:10 "We established you [people] on the earth and provided you with a means of livelihood there––small thanks you give" · placement plus provision is the frame for stewardship; thanks is the expected return.
- 7:69 and 7:74 "Remember how He made you heirs after Noah’s people" and "Remember how He made you heirs after 'Ad and settled you in the land to build yourselves castles on its plains and carve houses out of the mountains: remember God’s blessings and do not spread corruption in the land" · each people inherits the land from a fallen predecessor; the inheritance comes with permission to build and a ban on corrupting.
- 7:128-129 "the earth belongs to God- He gives it as their own to whichever of His servants He chooses ... make you successors to the land to see how you behave" · succession is conditional and observed; you hold the land to be watched in how you hold it.
- 10:14 "Later We made you their successors in the land, to see how you would behave" · repeated in surah 10; the whole of history is a series of trial custodies.
- 7:142 "Take my place among my people: act rightly and do not follow the way of those who spread corruption." · moses delegates to aaron with two instructions; a template for handing a project to someone.
- 7:102 "We found that most of them did not honour their commitments; We found that most of them were defiant" · the failure of most peoples is summed up as broken commitment.
- 8:27 "Believers, do not betray God and the Messenger, or knowingly betray [other people’s] trust in you" · the direct command; other people's trust is set alongside God's.
- 8:28 "Be aware that your possessions and your children are only a test" · what you hold is a test, not a possession in the full sense.
- 8:34 "although they are not its [rightful] guardians? Only those mindful of God are its rightful guardians" · custody of a sacred place belongs to conduct, not to occupation.
- 8:58 "if you learn of treachery on the part of any people, throw their treaty back at them, for God does not love the treacherous" · a broken trust is answered openly, not quietly.
- 9:4 and 9:7 "fulfil your agreement with them to the end of their term" and "so long as they remain true to you, be true to them" · agreements are honoured to term even with opponents; reciprocity is the rule.
- 9:75-77 "‘If God gives us some of His bounty, we shall certainly give alms and be righteous,’ ... yet when He did give them some of His bounty, they became mean and turned obstinately away ... Because they broke their promise to God, because of all the lies they told, He made hypocrisy settle in their hearts" · a pledge made in scarcity and broken in plenty is the specific betrayal named; a warning for any organisation that becomes funded.
- 9:17-18 "The only ones who should tend God’s places of worship are those who believe in God and the Last Day, who keep up the prayer, who pay the prescribed alms, and who fear no one but God" · who may maintain what is held in common is defined by their fearing no one else.

## 4. craft and excellence

- 9:105 "Say [Prophet], ‘Take action! God will see your actions- as will His Messenger and the believers- and then you will be returned to Him who knows what is seen and unseen, and He will tell you what you have been doing" · the command is to act; the work is seen by God and also by the community, and the account given is of what you have been doing, not saying.
- 10:61 "In whatever matter you [Prophet] may be engaged and whatever part of the Quran you are reciting, whatever work you [people] are doing, We witness you when you are engaged in it." · the witness is present during the work, in the act itself, not only at the result; this is "craft is worship" in the text's own words.
- 9:120-121 "a good deed is recorded in their favour on account of it- God never wastes the reward of those who do good ... if they spend a little or a lot for God’s cause, if they traverse a mountain pass, all this is recorded to their credit so that God can reward them in accordance with the best of their deeds" · every small effort is logged, and reward is set by the best of the deeds, not the average.
- 9:108-109 "a mosque founded from its first day on consciousness of God: in this mosque there are men who desire to grow in purity ... Which is better, the person who founds his building on consciousness of God and desire for His good pleasure, or the person who founds his building on the brink of a crumbling precipice" · a building is judged by its foundation from day one; what a thing is founded on decides what it becomes.
- 7:56 "The mercy of God is close to those who do good" · doing good (ihsan in the arabic) is what brings mercy near.
- 10:26 "Those who did well will have the best reward and more besides." · excellence receives the best and then more.
- 9:91 "there is no reason to reproach those who do good"
- 7:31-32 "dress well whenever you are at worship, and eat and drink [as We have permitted] but do not be extravagant ... Say [Prophet], ‘Who has forbidden the adornment and the nourishment God has provided for His servants?’" · beauty and adornment are defended against those who would forbid them; craft that is beautiful has warrant, with extravagance as its one limit.
- 7:145 "We inscribed everything for him in the Tablets which taught and explained everything, saying, ‘Hold on to them firmly and urge your people to hold fast to their excellent teachings." · the tablets are described as teaching and explaining; a made object whose excellence is that it explains.
- 7:137 "We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people were making and what they were building" · building without right does not stand; scale and effort are not the same as excellence.
- 7:64 and 10:73 "We saved him, and those who were with him, on the Ark" · noah's ark is here only as the saving vessel; the building-under-mockery detail belongs to other surahs, but the outcome is recorded twice in this chunk.
- 7:22 "they began to put together leaves from the Garden to cover themselves" · the first human act after error is making something with the hands; craft begins as repair.

## 5. justice and balance

- 7:8-9 "On that Day the weighing of deeds will be true and just: those whose good deeds are heavy on the scales will be the ones to prosper ... and those whose good deeds are light will be the ones who have lost their souls" · the scales are true and just; weight of deeds, not of numbers or wealth.
- 7:85 "Give full measure and weight and do not undervalue people’s goods; do not cause corruption in the land after it has been set in order: this is better for you, if you are believers" · full measure, not undervaluing what others bring, and not corrupting what has been set right are one instruction in one breath.
- 7:86 "Do not sit in every pathway, threatening and barring those who believe in God from His way, trying to make it crooked. Remember how you used to be few and He made you multiply. Think about the fate of those who used to spread corruption" · shuayb's second charge after short measure is toll-keeping on the road: sitting on every path to threaten and bar. this is gatekeeping named as corruption.
- 8:36 "They use their wealth to bar people from the path of God, and they will go on doing so." · wealth used as a barrier is the specific abuse.
- 9:9 "They have sold God’s message for a trifling gain, and barred others from His path. How evil their actions are" · selling the message cheaply and barring access are paired.
- 9:34-35 "many rabbis and monks wrongfully consume people’s possessions and turn people away from God’s path. [Prophet], tell those who hoard gold and silver instead of giving in God’s cause that they will have a grievous punishment ... ‘This is what you hoarded up for yourselves! Now feel the pain of what you hoarded!’" · the religious class that consumes others' wealth and the hoarders of gold are one sentence; hoarding is punished with the hoard itself.
- 9:60 "Alms are meant only for the poor, the needy, those who administer them, those whose hearts need winning over, to free slaves and help those in debt, for God’s cause, and for travellers in need." · a fixed list of who alms are for, including debt relief and administrators; a distribution rule written down.
- 8:41 "one-fifth of your battle gains belongs to God and the Messenger, to close relatives and orphans, to the needy and travellers" · a fixed share of gains is routed to those without.
- 9:79 "It is they who criticize the believers who give freely and those who can only give a little with great effort: they scoff at such people, but it is God who scoffs at them" · mocking the small giver is a named wrong; contribution is measured against means.
- 7:159 and 7:181 "a group ... who guide with truth, and who act justly according to it" · guidance and justice are paired twice; truth is not enough without acting justly by it.
- 10:44 "God does not wrong people at all- it is they who wrong themselves" and 10:47 "they will be judged justly; they will not be wronged" · justice is promised as the ground rule of judgement.
- 10:27 "each evil deed will be requited by its equal" · proportion: the penalty matches the deed, no more.
- 9:29 "who do not obey the rule of justice" · the phrase itself, as a mark of what the opponents lack.
- 8:1 "make things right between you" · the first instruction on distributing gains is to settle relations, before the shares.

## 6. agency and self-change

- 8:53 "God would never change a favour He had conferred on a people unless they changed what was within themselves." · the law of 13:11 from the other side: favour is kept while the inner state is kept; change within precedes change without.
- 10:41 "I act for myself, and you for yourselves. You are not responsible for my actions nor am I responsible for yours." · a clean statement of separate accounts; each party owns their own doing.
- 10:108 "Whoever follows the right path follows it for his own good, and whoever strays does so to his own loss: I am not your guardian." · the benefit and the loss of choice return to the chooser.
- 10:23 "People! Your outrageous behaviour only works against yourselves." · harm done returns to the doer.
- 8:51 "This is caused by what your own hands have stored up for you: God is never unjust to His creatures." · your own hands stored it; nothing arbitrary about it.
- 7:147 and 10:52 "why should they be repaid for anything other than what they have done?" and "Why should you be rewarded for anything but what you did?" · the same question twice, ruling out reward or blame for anything but action.
- 9:70 "God would not deceive them; they deceived themselves" · self-deception is named as the mechanism of ruin.
- 7:23 "‘Our Lord, we have wronged our souls: if You do not forgive us and have mercy, we shall be lost.’" · adam owns the error in the first person; repair begins with owning it.
- 7:39 "‘You were no better than us: taste the punishment you have earned.’" · the followers cannot pass blame to the leaders; 7:38 gives both double punishment. no one hides behind the crowd.
- 7:42 "We do not burden any soul with more than it can bear" · agency is bounded to capacity; the demand is never impossible.
- 9:46 "If they had really wanted to go out [to battle] with you, they would have made preparations" · intent shows as preparation; a will that leaves no trace in preparation is not a will.
- 9:92 "they turned away with their eyes overflowing with tears of grief that they had nothing they could contribute" · those with no means but the will are counted with the doers; contribution is measured by what you had.
- 9:118 "when they realized that the only refuge from God was with Him, He turned to them in mercy in order for them to return [to Him]" · the three who stayed behind are turned to after they turn; the return is mutual.
- 7:176 "he clung to the earth and followed his own desires- he was like a dog that pants with a lolling tongue whether you drive it away or leave it alone" · the man given signs who did nothing with them; the picture of an agency surrendered.
- 9:41 "So go out, no matter whether you are lightly or heavily armed" · start with what you have.

## 7. time and mortality

- 7:34 "There is a time set for every people: they cannot hasten it, nor, when it comes, will they be able to delay it for a single moment" · a fixed term that cannot be moved; the ground of urgency without panic.
- 7:24 "On earth you will have a place to stay and livelihood- for a time." · the whole of earthly life is qualified with "for a time".
- 7:187 "Say, ‘My Lord alone has knowledge of it: He alone will reveal when its time will come, a time that is momentous in both the heavens and earth. All too suddenly it will come upon you.’" · the hour is unknown and sudden; this is the maker's "the exact amount unknown only to God" in the text's words.
- 7:97-98 "Do the people of these towns feel secure that Our punishment will not come upon them by night, while they are asleep ... Do the people of these towns feel secure that Our punishment will not come upon them by day, while they are at play" · night or day, sleep or play; false security is the target.
- 7:53 "Or can we be sent back to behave differently from the way we behaved before?" · the wish to be sent back is asked and not granted; there is one pass.
- 10:45 "it will be as if they have stayed [in the world] no longer than a single hour" · a whole life, seen from its end, is an hour.
- 10:49 "There is an appointed term for every community, and when it is reached they can neither delay nor hasten it, even for a moment." · terms apply to communities and organisations, not only to persons.
- 10:24 "when the earth has taken on its finest appearance, and adorns itself, and its people think they have power over it, then the fate We commanded comes to it, by night or by day, and We reduce it to stubble, as if it had not flourished just the day before" · the moment of feeling in control is the moment before stubble.
- 10:5 "determining phases for it so that you might know the number of years and how to calculate time" and 9:36 "twelve months- ordained in God’s Book on the Day when He created the heavens and earth- four months of which are sacred: this is the correct calculation" · time is meant to be counted correctly; the calendar is a sacred instrument.
- 7:142 "We appointed thirty nights for Moses, then added ten more: the term set by his Lord was completed in forty nights." · a deadline, extended once, then held; the work of receiving the tablets had a term.
- 7:183 "I will give them respite, but My plan is sure" · respite is real, not indefinite.
- 10:90-91 "as he was drowning he cried, ‘I believe there is no God except the one the Children of Israel believe in. I submit to Him.’ ... ‘Now? When you had always been a rebel, and a troublemaker" · the late turn is answered with "Now?"; a change made at the last moment is not the same as a change made in time.
- 9:126 "Can they not see that they are afflicted once or twice a year? Yet they neither repent nor take heed" · recurring warnings, yearly, ignored.
- 10:11 "If God were to hasten on for people the harm [they have earned] as they wish to hasten on the good, their time would already be up." · the delay itself is mercy.

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- 7:12-13 "‘I am better than him: You created me from fire and him from clay.’ ... God said, ‘Get down from here! This is no place for your arrogance." · the first corruption is a comparison: I am better, by material. arrogance is the root sin of the chunk.
- 7:56 "do not corrupt the earth after it has been set right- call on Him fearing and hoping. The mercy of God is close to those who do good" · the core line: the earth was set right and corruption is what undoes it; the alternative is doing good.
- 7:74 "remember God’s blessings and do not spread corruption in the land" · to thamud, heirs and builders.
- 7:85-86 "do not cause corruption in the land after it has been set in order ... Think about the fate of those who used to spread corruption" · to midian, alongside short measure and gatekeeping.
- 7:103 "See the fate of those who used to spread corruption" · pharaoh's people, after all the towns.
- 7:127 "‘But are you going to leave Moses and his people to spread corruption in the land and forsake you and your gods?’" · pharaoh's court calls the reformer the corrupter; the word is used by both sides, and the manifesto should expect to be called what it fights.
- 7:142 "do not follow the way of those who spread corruption" · moses to aaron, as a standing instruction to a deputy.
- 7:31 "do not be extravagant: God does not like extravagant people" · extravagance is the household form of corruption.
- 7:40 "even if a thick rope were to pass through the eye of a needle they would not enter the Garden" · the image of arrogant rejection.
- 7:48 "‘What use were your great numbers and your false pride" · numbers and pride, dismissed together.
- 7:60, 7:66, 7:75, 7:88, 7:90 "the prominent leaders of his people said", "the disbelieving leaders", "the arrogant leaders among his people said to the believers they thought to be of no account", "His people’s arrogant leaders said, ‘Shuayb, we will expel you", "The disbelieving leaders among his people said, ‘You will certainly be losers if you follow Shuayb’" · in every story it is the leading class that rejects, and they look down on the believers as nobodies and threaten expulsion. the pattern of an elite defending its defaults is the shape of every story in surah 7.
- 7:95 "they said, ‘Hardship and affluence also befell our forefathers,’ and so We took them suddenly, unawares" · normalising the cycle ("it has always been like this") is the last step before the fall.
- 7:100-101 "And seal up their hearts so that they cannot hear ... they would not believe in what they had already rejected" · the sealed heart is one that refuses what it has already refused; decay as a habit of refusal.
- 7:131 "when something good came their way, they said, ‘This is our due!’. When something bad came, they ascribed it to the evil omen of Moses" · taking credit for good and blaming the reformer for bad.
- 7:148 "a mere shape that made sounds like a cow- a calf made from their jewellery. Could they not see that it did not speak to them or guide them in any way?" · a made object that makes noises but neither speaks nor guides, fashioned from what people had of value; the closest image in the chunk to generated slop.
- 7:169 "took the fleeting gains of this lower world, saying, ‘We shall be forgiven,’ and indeed taking them again if other such gains came their way" · the inheritor of the book who takes the gains and presumes forgiveness, repeatedly.
- 8:47 "Do not be like those who came out of their homes full of conceit, showing off to people, and barring others from the way of God" · conceit, performance, and barring others as one posture.
- 8:73 "The disbelievers support one another. If you do not do the same, there will be persecution in the land and great corruption" · corruption grows where the good do not organise; solidarity is the counter.
- 9:25 "You were well pleased with your large numbers, but they were of no use to you: the earth seemed to close in on you despite its spaciousness" · the believers' own decay is named too: pleasure in numbers.
- 9:37 "they will allow it one year and forbid it in another in order outwardly to conform with the number of God’s sacred months, but in doing so they permit what God has forbidden" · gaming a rule to conform outwardly while breaking it; the calendar as an example of a rigged default.
- 9:67 "they order what is wrong and forbid what is right; they are tight-fisted" · the hypocrite's marks: inverted commands and a closed hand.
- 9:69 "like them, you have indulged in idle talk.’ Their deeds go to waste in this world and the next" · idle talk is the decay of those with wealth and children.

## 9. community and mutual aid

- 7:160 "twelve springs gushed out. Each tribe knew its own drinking place" · one rock, twelve outlets, each group with its own known source; a picture of shared infrastructure with local ownership.

- 9:128 "Your suffering distresses him: he is deeply concerned for you and full of kindness and mercy towards the believers" · the leader's mark is being pained by the group's pain.
- 10:19 "All people were originally one single community, but later they differed." · the whole precedes the parts.

## 11. wonder, play and nature

- 7:32 "‘Who has forbidden the adornment and the nourishment God has provided for His servants?’" · adornment is a provision, not a sin.
- a note on play: in this chunk play appears only on the wrong side. 7:51 "those who took their religion for distraction, a mere game", 7:98 "by day, while they are at play", 9:65 "‘We were just chatting, just amusing ourselves.’" the maker's playfulness has warrant in 7:32 (adornment defended) but the chunk's own word "game" is a warning; the manifesto should say what kind of play it means.

## 12. building and repairing stories

- 7:74 "settled you in the land to build yourselves castles on its plains and carve houses out of the mountains" · thamud were builders and carvers; the gift of building was theirs before they corrupted.
- 7:117-118 "‘Throw your staff,’ and- lo and behold!––it devoured their fakery ... The truth was confirmed and what they had produced came to nothing" · the demonstration that eats the fake.
- 7:137 "We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people were making and what they were building" · the tyrant's works do not outlast him.
- 7:142 "‘Take my place among my people: act rightly and do not follow the way of those who spread corruption.’" · delegation during absence.
- 7:145, 7:150, 7:154 "We inscribed everything for him in the Tablets which taught and explained everything" ... "He threw the tablets down and seized his brother by the hair" ... - 7:148 "a calf made from their jewellery" · the counterfeit made object, in moses's absence, from the people's own valuables.
- 7:157 "who makes good things lawful to them and bad things unlawful, and relieves them of their burdens, and the iron collars that were on them" · the messenger's work is described as removing burdens and collars: repair as unshackling.

- 9:107-110 "those who built a mosque––in an attempt to cause harm ... a mosque founded from its first day on consciousness of God ... the person who founds his building on the brink of a crumbling precipice" · two buildings of the same type, told apart by foundation and intent; the honest one is where "men who desire to grow in purity" gather.

- 10:87 "‘House your people in Egypt and make these houses places of worship; keep up the prayer; give good news to the believers!’" · under a hostile regime, the instruction is to turn homes into places of worship: build within your own walls first. this is the closest thing in the chunk to "your own machine".
- 10:98 "If only a single town had believed and benefited from its belief! Only Jonah’s people did so, and when they believed, We relieved them of the punishment of disgrace" · the one town that turned in time; repair at the scale of a town is possible and happened once.
- 7:64 and 10:73 "We saved him, and those who were with him, on the Ark" · the ark as the vessel that carried the few.

## 13. freedom and no compulsion

- 10:99 "Had your Lord willed, all the people on earth would have believed. So can you [Prophet] compel people to believe" · the question is rhetorical; compulsion is not the prophet's to use.
- 10:108 "‘People, the Truth has come to you from your Lord. Whoever follows the right path follows it for his own good, and whoever strays does so to his own loss: I am not your guardian.’" · the message is delivered, the outcome belongs to the hearer, and the messenger disclaims guardianship.
- 10:41 "‘I act for myself, and you for yourselves. You are not responsible for my actions nor am I responsible for yours.’"
- 7:188 "I am no more than a bearer of warning, and good news to those who believe.’" · the role is bearer, not controller.
- 7:87 "If some of you believe the message I bring and others do not, then be patient till God judges between us." · shuayb accepts a mixed response and waits.
- 7:123 "Pharaoh said, ‘How dare you believe in Him before I have given you permission?" · the tyrant's model is that belief itself needs his permission; the sorcerers believed without it.
- 7:88-89 "‘Shuayb, we will expel you and your fellow believers from our town unless you return to our religion.’ He said, ‘What! Even if we detest it ... If we were to return to your religion after God has saved us from it, we would be inventing lies about Him" · the demand to return to the default is refused even under threat of exile.
- 7:193 and 7:198 "it makes no difference whether you call them or remain silent" and "they do not hear" · the limits of persuasion are stated without bitterness.
- 8:61 "if they incline towards peace, you [Prophet] must also incline towards it" · the door to peace is kept open by obligation.
- 9:6 "grant it to him so that he may hear the word of God, then take him to a place safe for him" · the opponent is guaranteed a hearing and safe return.
- 10:15 "‘It is not for me to change it of my own accord; I only follow what is revealed to me" · the messenger refuses to alter the text under pressure to make it more agreeable.
- 10:72 "I have asked no reward from you; my reward is with God alone" · no fee for the message.
- 7:157 "relieves them of their burdens, and the iron collars that were on them" · freedom as the removal of collars.
- a note for honesty: this chunk also holds the martial verses of surahs 8 and 9 (9:5, 9:12-14, 9:29, 9:73, 9:123). they are tied in the text to broken oaths and first aggression (9:13 "who have broken their oaths, who tried to drive the Messenger out, who attacked you first") and bounded by 8:61 and 9:4-7. a manifesto that leans on 2:256 (haleem: "There is no compulsion in religion") should know these verses sit in the same book and not pretend they do not.

- 7:203 "this revelation brings you insights from your Lord, and guidance and mercy" · insights (the arabic is basa'ir, sight-givers) as what the text brings.
- 9:11 "We make the messages clear for people who are willing to learn"
- 9:115 "God would not condemn for going astray those He has already guided [to the faith] before making entirely clear to them what they should avoid." · no one is held to a rule not made entirely clear first; clarity is a precondition of accountability.
- 10:5 "He explains His signs to those who understand" and 10:24 "This is the way We explain the revelations for those who reflect" · explanation is addressed to understanding and reflection.

- 10:61 "it is all written in a clear record" · the record is clear.

## 16. warnings for the manifesto's own posture

- 7:12 "‘I am better than him: You created me from fire and him from clay.’" · the founding sin is a comparison of materials; any manifesto that says "we are better than them" has iblis's grammar.
- 7:21 "he swore to them, ‘I am giving you sincere advice’" · satan uses the exact phrase the prophets use (7:62 "giving you sincere advice", 7:68 "I am your sincere and honest adviser", 7:79 "gave you sincere advice"). the phrase proves nothing; only the fruit does.
- 7:127 "‘But are you going to leave Moses and his people to spread corruption in the land" · the reformer will be called the corrupter; the only defence is to not be one.
- 7:150 "He threw the tablets down and seized his brother by the hair, pulling him towards him." · the reformer's rage lands on his own brother and on the tablets themselves; anger at the crowd damages the guidance and the ally.
- 7:155 "so will You now destroy us for what the foolish among us have done?" · moses pleads not to be judged for the foolish among his own; a collective must reckon with its own fools without disowning them.
- 7:169 "saying, ‘We shall be forgiven,’ and indeed taking them again" · presuming on forgiveness while repeating the gain.
- 7:199 "pay no attention to foolish people" · the opposite temptation: spending the work on arguments with the foolish.
- 8:6 "argued with you about the truth after it had been made clear" · relitigating settled things.
- 8:21 "those who say, ‘We heard,’ though in fact they were not listening" · the collective that claims to have heard its users.
- 8:25 and 8:46 "Beware of discord that harms not only the wrongdoers among you" and "do not quarrel with one another, or you may lose heart" · internal quarrel is the chunk's named cause of losing heart.
- 8:47 "full of conceit, showing off to people, and barring others from the way of God" · showing off is paired with barring others; performance and gatekeeping are one vice.
- 9:9 "They have sold God’s message for a trifling gain" · the cheap sale of the message; a warning for a "business in a very loose way".
- 9:25 "You were well pleased with your large numbers, but they were of no use to you" · the community's own failure at hunayn was pleasure in size; growth metrics are a snare.
- 9:37 "in order outwardly to conform with the number of God’s sacred months, but in doing so they permit what God has forbidden" · keeping the letter of a rule while gaming it.
- 9:42 "They will swear by God, ‘If we could, we certainly would go out [to battle] with you,’ but they ruin themselves, for God knows that they are lying" · the swearing of would-have-done.
- 9:46 "If they had really wanted to go out [to battle] with you, they would have made preparations" · intentions without preparation are not intentions.
- 9:64-65 "‘Carry on with your jokes: God will bring about what you fear!’ ... yet if you were to question them, they would be sure to say, ‘We were just chatting, just amusing ourselves.’" · the joke as cover for contempt; "playful, strange, weird" must not become "just chatting".
- 9:67 "they order what is wrong and forbid what is right; they are tight-fisted" · a movement that ends up forbidding the right and hoarding.
- 9:75-76 "‘If God gives us some of His bounty, we shall certainly give alms and be righteous,’ ... yet when He did give them some of His bounty, they became mean" · the pledge before funding and the meanness after.
- 9:79 "they scoff at such people" · scoffing at small contributors.
- 9:107 "they swear, ‘Our intentions were nothing but good,’" · good intentions claimed for a structure built to divide; the manifesto should not lean on its intentions.
- 9:109 "the person who founds his building on the brink of a crumbling precipice" · a foundation chosen badly cannot be fixed by building higher.
- 9:122 "Yet it is not right for all the believers to go out [to battle] together" · not everyone should be on the front; someone must stay to learn and teach.
- 10:12 and 10:23 "as soon as We relieve him of his trouble he goes on his way as if he had never cried out" and "no sooner does He save them than, back on land, they behave outrageously" · the cycle of urgency in the storm and forgetting on land; the manifesto's fervour will fade after the first success unless it plans for that.
- 10:36 "Most of them follow nothing but assumptions" · a research organisation's own conjecture is not exempt.
- 10:93 "It was only after knowledge had come to them that they began to differ among themselves." · knowledge splits people into sects; the more sandbox knows the more it will be tempted to divide.
- 7:38-39 "‘Our Lord, it was they who led us astray: give them double punishment in the Fire’- God says, ‘Every one of you will have double punishment" · blaming the leaders does not clear the followers; a collective cannot hide behind its founder, nor the founder behind the collective.
- 9:14-15 "He will heal the believers’ feelings ... and remove the rage from their hearts" · rage is a thing to be removed from the believers' hearts, not fed; a rebellion that keeps its rage has not finished.
- 9:73 "Prophet, strive against the disbelievers and the hypocrites, and be tough with them." · the chunk does contain the command to be tough; the manifesto's "never cruel" must be argued from 8:61, 9:6, 9:114 and 9:128 in the same surahs, not by pretending 9:73 is absent.
- 7:2 "let there be no anxiety in your heart because of it" · the opposite warning: do not write from anxiety.

## images and parables

- the thick rope through the eye of a needle (7:40): "even if a thick rope were to pass through the eye of a needle they would not enter the Garden". the impossible admission of the arrogant.
- rain on dead land (7:57): "We drive them to a dead land where We cause rain to fall, bringing out all kinds of crops". revival of what looked finished.
- good land and bad land (7:58): "Vegetation comes out of good land in abundance ... but out of bad land only scantily". the same rain, different soil.
- the she-camel let to graze (7:73): "let her graze in God’s land and do not harm her in any way". a commons animal; hamstringing it (7:77) was the test failed.
- castles on the plains, houses carved from mountains (7:74). the builders who were given the land.
- sitting in every pathway (7:86): "Do not sit in every pathway, threatening and barring". the toll-keeper as the image of gatekeeping.
- the staff that devours the fakery (7:117): "it devoured their fakery". the working thing eating the illusion.
- the spell on people's eyes (7:116): "casting a spell on people’s eyes, striking fear into them". how fakery works: eyes and fear.
- the calf from jewellery that lowed (7:148): "a mere shape that made sounds like a cow". a made noise without guidance.
- the tablets thrown and picked up (7:150, 7:154). guidance survives the reformer's anger.
- the rock and twelve springs (7:160): "twelve springs gushed out. Each tribe knew its own drinking place". one source, many known outlets.
- the mountain like a shadow (7:171): "We made the mountain loom high above them like a shadow". the weight of what one has been given.
- the dog that pants either way (7:176): "like a dog that pants with a lolling tongue whether you drive it away or leave it alone". indifference to signs.
- unused hearts, eyes, ears; cattle (7:179).
- the garment of God-consciousness (7:26): "the garment of God-consciousness is the best of all garments". and the stitched leaves before it (7:22).
- the iron collars (7:157): "relieves them of their burdens, and the iron collars that were on them".
- sleep and rain that firm the feet (8:11): "to make your hearts strong and your feet firm".

## how the text itself speaks

- direct address by lineage: "Children of Adam" opens 7:26, 7:27, 7:31 and 7:35 in succession, each time with a different instruction (garments, do not be seduced, dress well and do not be extravagant, messengers will come). addressing readers as heirs of one ancestor before instructing them.
- "Believers," as a vocative opens many verses in surahs 8 and 9 (8:15, 8:20, 8:24, 8:27, 8:29, 8:45, 9:23, 9:28, 9:34, 9:38, 9:119, 9:123). "People!" opens 10:23, 10:57, 10:104, 10:108. the second person plural is the default register.
- "Say [Prophet]," as an instruction to speak: the text hands the messenger lines to deliver, so the reader hears both the command and the speech. 10:31-35 is a chain of "Say ... Say ... Say" each ending in a question: "So why do you not take heed of Him", "So how is it that you are dissuaded?", "so how can you be misled?", "What is the matter with you? How do you judge?"
- questions to the reader: 7:57 "Will you not reflect", 7:63 and 7:69 "Do you find it so strange", 7:169 "‘Why do you not use your reason?’", 8:22, 10:16 "How can you not use your reason?", 10:2 "Is it so surprising to people", 10:99 "So can you [Prophet] compel people to believe".
- refrains across the prophet stories of surah 7: each begins "My people, serve God: you have no god other than Him" (7:59, 7:65, 7:73, 7:85); each messenger says "I am delivering my Lord’s messages to you" (7:62, 7:68, 7:79, 7:93); two towns end with the identical line "An earthquake seized them: by the next morning they were lying dead in their homes" (7:78, 7:91); the closing formula "See the fate of" recurs (7:84, 7:103, 10:39, 10:73). the form is a series of case studies with a fixed template, which is how a manifesto can present its own examples of broken defaults.
- "lo and behold!" for sudden reveals (7:107, 7:108, 7:117, 7:135), including one used for a broken promise: "lo and behold!- they broke it".
- the patient challenge: "Just wait; I too am waiting." (7:71), "so wait- I too am waiting" (10:20), "Say, ‘Wait then, I am waiting too.’" (10:102). the speaker declines to force the matter and waits alongside the opponent.
- the opponent's speech is quoted in full, then answered (7:28, 7:70-71, 10:78, 8:31). the text does not paraphrase its critics.
- dialogue staged at the end (7:44-50): the people of the garden and the fire call across to each other; the men on the heights recognise both by their marks. a debate written as a scene.
- numbered and listed duties: 9:60 lists eight recipients of alms; 9:112 lists the believers' traits; 8:2-4 lists what true believers are; 9:71 lists what they do. the form of a checklist is used for duties and marks.
- promise paired with warning in adjacent verses: 7:35-36, 9:81-82 "Say, ‘Hellfire is hotter.’ ... Let them laugh a little; they will weep a lot", 9:88-89 against 9:87, 10:26-27, 10:62-64 against 10:70.
- short verdicts: "Say, ‘Hellfire is hotter.’" (9:81); "‘Now? When you had always been a rebel" (10:91); "‘That is what you get! Taste that!’" (8:14).
- exact numbers as rhythm: thirty nights plus ten equals forty (7:142); twenty overcome two hundred, a hundred overcome a thousand, then reduced to a hundred against two hundred (8:65-66); seventy times (9:80); twelve months and four sacred (9:36); twelve tribes and twelve springs (7:160).
- similes introduced with "like": the dog (7:176), cattle (7:179), the rain and the adorned earth (10:24), veils cut from night (10:27).
- an oath: "‘Yes, by my Lord, it is true, and you cannot escape it.’" (10:53).
- the text corrects its own bearer in public: 9:43 "God forgive you [Prophet]! Why did you give them permission", 8:67-68 on taking captives. a manifesto that records its own author's corrections is following the form.
- "if you only knew" and "If only they understood" as closers (9:41, 9:81); and the seal of divine attributes at verse ends ("God is all knowing and wise", "God is most forgiving and merciful") acting as a refrain that closes each unit.
- the reminder as a device: "Remember when you were few" (8:26), "Remember how He made you heirs" (7:69, 7:74), "Remember how you used to be few" (7:86). the community is repeatedly asked to recall its own small beginnings.

## surprises

- 7:86 makes gatekeeping a sin on the same list as short measure. shuayb's charges are: give full measure, do not undervalue people's goods, do not corrupt what was set right, and "Do not sit in every pathway, threatening and barring". the maker's word "gated" has a verse, and it is spoken by the prophet of merchants.
- 7:123 gives the tyrant's model of permission: "How dare you believe in Him before I have given you permission?" the sorcerers, paid experts who had asked "Shall we be rewarded if we win?" (7:113) and were promised the "inner court" (7:114), are the ones who break ranks once they see the true thing. the technical class can switch sides when shown a working demonstration.
- 7:116-117 says how fakery works and how it dies: "casting a spell on people’s eyes, striking fear into them" and then "it devoured their fakery". the answer to a narrative is not a counter-narrative but a thing that works in front of everyone.
- 7:148 is a parable of slop: a calf made from the people's own jewellery, "a mere shape that made sounds like a cow", that "did not speak to them or guide them in any way". it was made in the leader's absence, from what people valued, and it made a noise.
- 7:162 names the altered narrative: "substituted another saying for that which had been given them". and 10:15 records the pressure to alter the text ("Bring [us] a different Quran, or change it") and its refusal.
- 9:122 is a charter for a research collective: a group goes out "to gain understanding", returns, and teaches, while the rest hold the line. and it opens with the rule that not everyone should go out at once.
- 9:43 shows the text correcting its own prophet for trusting excuses before verifying: "Why did you give them permission to stay at home before it had become clear to you which of them spoke the truth". verify first, even when you are the one in charge.
- 10:61 places the witness inside the work: "whatever work you [people] are doing, We witness you when you are engaged in it". this is the ground for "craft is worship": the act is seen while it happens, not graded afterwards.
- 9:120-121 says every small effort is recorded and reward is set "in accordance with the best of their deeds". the ledger counts the small, and grades by the best.
- 9:107-110 is a story of two structures of the same kind told apart by their foundation; the harmful one was built with sworn good intentions, and it corrodes its own builders: "will always be a source of doubt within their hearts".
- 10:87 says to build inside your own walls under a hostile regime: "House your people in Egypt and make these houses places of worship". sandbox os's "on your own machine" has an ancestor.
- 7:164 answers "why bother" when the audience seems lost: "In order to be free from your Lord’s blame, and so that they may perhaps take heed." publish anyway, for the record and for the one who might listen.
- 9:92 counts the will of those who had nothing: they "turned away with their eyes overflowing with tears of grief that they had nothing they could contribute", and they are set beside the doers. contribution is measured against means.
- 7:2 opens the whole chunk by telling the bearer of the book not to be anxious about it; 10:65 says "Do not let their words grieve you". the text expects its carrier to be attacked and tells him to hold steady.
- 9:25 and 9:118 use the same image for two different kinds of stuck: "the earth seemed to close in on you despite its spaciousness". once for a community that trusted its size, once for three men who stayed behind. the maker's "make us feel stuck" is in the text as a closing-in of a wide earth.
- 7:31-32 defends adornment against those who would forbid it. the workshop may be beautiful; the one limit is extravagance.
- 9:37 is a rigged default: a calendar gamed "in order outwardly to conform". the shape of the abuse is keeping the count right while breaking the rule.
- 10:93 warns that knowledge itself splits people: "It was only after knowledge had come to them that they began to differ among themselves." a research organisation should expect its own knowledge to become an occasion for sects and plan against it.
- 9:14-15 says the aim of the fight includes removing rage from the fighters: "remove the rage from their hearts". rebellion that keeps its rage is unfinished.
- 8:63 says a collective's binding cannot be bought: "Even if you had given away everything in the earth you could not have done this". community is not a budget line.
- 7:22 records that the first human act after the first error was making: "they began to put together leaves from the Garden to cover themselves". craft is the first response to shame.
- 7:38-39 refuses both the followers' blame of leaders and the leaders' scorn of followers; both get double. neither the founder nor the collective can hide behind the other.
