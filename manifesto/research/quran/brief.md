# brief for the readers

you are one of eight readers. together you read the whole quran, in the abdel haleem
translation, so that a manifesto can be written on ground the maker actually stands on.
your chunk is one file. read every line of it. do not skim, do not sample, do not stop at
the verses you already know. the point of eight readers is that each one reads slowly.

## who the manifesto is for

the maker runs a small workshop called sandbox (an organisation that researches, publishes,
builds and releases things) and an open-source tool called sandbox os (a maker's operating
system on your own machine, where nothing durable lands without the owner's keypress). the
manifesto is for the organisation, above the tool. the maker's own words, verbatim:

> I started Sandbox because I can see the world changing around me and changing me as well
> and the people I care for and love. Access to knowledge and to empowerment is being gated
> behind curated or altered narratives or knowledge, technical difficulties, networks or

> make us feel stuck. I need to rebel against myself and against the comfort I found myself
> in, against the system that works against my favor and to show people that a different
> path is possible, especially now with AI which is also being used to take jobs away,

> knowledge, science, technology and art in its highest form, its most sincere and raw
> expression, in its playfulness and power to shift perspectives, move emotions and people
> towards a different and hopefully better path. To show people possibilities, give them

the tool already stands on ten axioms anchored in the quran (54:17, 96:6-7, 33:72, 21:22,

## the themes to read for

collect verses under these headings. a verse may sit under more than one. for each entry:
the reference as `S:V`, then the abdel haleem words verbatim (the part that carries the
point, up to about 35 words), then one plain sentence on what it gives the manifesto.

1. knowledge and reading: the pen, reflection, reason, signs; against inheriting beliefs
   blindly from forefathers; against concealing knowledge; the rank of those who know;
   humility about knowing ("above every knower is one who knows more")
2. proof and truth: bring your proof; do not mix truth with falsehood; verify reports;
   testimony; writing things down; witnesses; claims vs what is done (61:2-3)
3. trust (amana), stewardship (khalifa), custody, returning what was entrusted
4. craft and excellence: ihsan, doing a thing well, beauty, "best in deed" (67:2), work that
   God sees (9:105), the crafts taught to david and solomon, noah building under mockery
5. justice and balance: the scales, fair measure, against hoarding, against wealth
   circulating only among the rich (59:7), against consuming others' wealth, against usury
6. agency and self-change: 13:11, striving (53:39), no soul bears another's burden, each
   answers for what they earned
7. time and mortality: the unknown hour, surah 103, the finite span, urgency without panic
8. corruption and decay (fasad): arrogance, pharaoh, false reformers (2:11-12), the spider's
   house (29:41), those who forget themselves (59:19), extravagance and waste
9. community and mutual aid: consultation (42:38), helping in good (5:2), knowing one
   another across difference (49:13), the whole and its parts
10. humility, moderation and warmth: walk gently, lower your voice, mercy, do not despair,
    ease after hardship, kindness in speech
11. wonder, play and nature: the bee, the ant, rain, the tree whose roots are firm (14:24),
    "each acts according to their own disposition" (17:84), creation as signs to think about
12. building and repairing stories: dhul-qarnayn's wall built with the people's own strength
    (18:83-98), khidr and the wall for the orphans (18:60-82), joseph and the storehouses,
    the ark, solomon's builders
13. freedom and no compulsion: 2:256, "you are not a controller over them" (88:21-22),
    "whoever wills, let him believe" (18:29); guidance offered, never forced
14. clarity and plain speech: bayan, a clear book, parables so people reflect, speaking so
    people understand; radical clarity
15. livelihood and trade: honest trade, seeking bounty, giving without reminders of
    generosity (2:262-264), secret charity, giving from what you love (3:92), the endowed
    good that keeps flowing
16. warnings for the manifesto's own posture: pride in one's own reform, saying without
    doing, dividing into sects, loving praise, the tongue outrunning the hand, rebellion that
    turns cruel

then four more sections:

- **images and parables** a writer could carry into a manifesto without preaching (the
  ant, the spider's house, the good tree, the lamp in the niche, the flood, the sealed
  hearts, the mustard seed on the scales, the ship on the sea, the wall, the pen).
- **how the text itself speaks**: its rhetorical devices as you meet them in your chunk.
  direct address, oaths, questions to the reader ("will you not reflect?"), refrains,
  parables, numbered lists of duties, promises paired with warnings, the second person
  plural, short surahs with hard rhythm. a manifesto can learn from the form as well as
  the content.
- **name and mark candidates**: any verse touching these words or ideas: bayan (clear
  expression, 55:4), mizan (balance), fitra (the original disposition, 30:30), furqan
  (the criterion), burhan (proof), qalam (pen), iqra (read), hikma (wisdom), amana, sanad
  or witness chains, nur (light), sabil (way), the hourglass or the passing of time.
- **surprises**: anything in your chunk that bears on the maker's mission and that a
  reader who only knows the famous verses would miss.

## rules

- quote only from your chunk file, verbatim, with the exact reference. never write a verse
  from memory. if you want a verse from outside your chunk, run
  `python3 <scratchpad>/quran/verse.py 2:282` and quote that output, labelled with its
  translation. the four translations on disk are haleem, asad, itani and sahih.
- read the whole chunk. at the end of your notes, write a coverage line: the first and last
  verse you read, the number of verses in the chunk, and any stretch you found nothing in
  (that is a fine and honest finding).
- write plainly. lowercase-first sentences. no em dashes. no hype words. the maker's house keeps
  a table of forbidden words in soul/voice.md (sales adjectives, significance fillers, scenery
  nouns, and the openers that announce a point instead of making it); read it and use none of
  them. do not use emoji.
- aim for depth over breadth: the reader who wrote "2:282 asks that a debt be written down
  by a scribe, justly, with two witnesses; this is a record with witnesses and a
  fingerprint, which is what the tool's approve verb already does" has done the job. a bare
  list of references has not.
- write the notes to the path you were given, as markdown, with the section headings above.
