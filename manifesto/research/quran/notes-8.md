# notes-8 · surahs 48 to 114 · abdel haleem · reader 8 of 8

these are the last 67 surahs. the medinan ones at the front (48 to 66) are law, community and the
ethics of a group under pressure. from 67 onward the surahs are meccan, short and hammered: oaths,
questions, refrains, two-word lines. the chunk holds the first revealed words (96:1-5), the pen (68:1),
the balance (55:7-9), the clear proof (98), the small kindnesses (107) and the creed (112). every
quotation below is copied from the chunk file. the few verses from outside the chunk are pulled with
verse.py and labelled.

## the maker's axioms met here, and what sits around them unused

four of the tool's ten anchors fall in this chunk: 54:17, 96:6-7, 55:7-9, 61:2-3.

- 54:17 "We have made it easy to learn lessons from the Quran: will anyone take heed" is not said once but
  four times (54:17, 54:22, 54:32, 54:40), each time after a people is destroyed. unused around it: 54:13
  "We carried him along on a vessel of planks and nails" (the ark named by its materials), 54:28 "the
  water is to be shared between them: each one should drink in turn" (a commons with a turn-taking rule),
  54:49 "We have created all things in due measure", and 54:52-53 "Everything they do is noted in their
  records every action, great or small, is recorded".
- 96:6-7 "But man exceeds all bounds when he thinks he is self-sufficient" sits right after 96:1-5, the
  command to read and the pen, and right before 96:9-10 "Have you seen the man who forbids [Our] servant to
  pray", 96:14 "Does he not realize that God sees all" and 96:19 "No! Do not obey him". the axiom about
  self-sufficiency is bracketed by reading on one side and refusal of a gatekeeper on the other.
- 55:7-9 "He has raised up the sky. He has set the balance so that you may not exceed in the balance weigh
  with justice and do not fall short in the balance". unused around it: 55:1-4 "It is the Lord of Mercy who
  taught the Quran He created man and taught him to communicate" (teaching and clear speech come before
  the balance in the order of the surah), 55:5-6 "The sun and the moon follow their calculated courses the
  plants and the trees submit to His designs", 55:29 "every day He is at work", 55:33 "if you can pass
  beyond the regions of heaven and earth, then do so", and 55:60 "Shall the reward of good be anything but
  good".
- 61:2-3 "You who believe, why do you say things and then do not do them It is most hateful to God that you
  say things and then do not do them". unused around it: 61:4 "those who fight in solid lines for His cause,
  like a well-compacted wall", 61:8 "they wish to put His light out with their mouths. But He will perfect
  His light", 61:10 "shall I show you a bargain that will save you", and 61:14 "'Who will come with me to
  help God?' The disciples said, 'We shall be God's helpers.'"

## 1. knowledge and reading

- 96:1-5 "Read! In the name of your Lord who created He created manfrom a clinging form Read! Your Lord is
  the Most Bountiful One who taught by [means of] the pen who taught man what he did not know". the first
  command of the whole revelation is read, the instrument is the pen, and knowledge is described as
  something taught, not owned; a workshop that publishes stands on this line.
- 68:1 "Nun By the pen! By all they write". an oath sworn on a tool and on the act of writing; the text
  treats writing as something you can swear by.
- 55:1-4 "It is the Lord of Mercy who taught the Quran He created man and taught him to communicate". the
  order is mercy, teaching, creation, clear speech; teaching precedes even the making of the person.
- 58:11 "if you are told to make room for one another in your assemblies, then do so, and God will make
  room for you ... God will raise up, by many degrees, those of you who believe and those who have been
  given knowledge". the rank of the knower is announced inside a verse about making room for others; rank
  and hospitality are one instruction.
- 62:2 "to recite His revelations to them, to make them grow spiritually and teach them the Scripture and
  wisdom––before that they were clearly astray". a messenger is raised "among the people who had no
  Scripture"; teaching is framed as growth for those who had nothing.
- 62:5 "Those who have been charged to obey the Torah, but do not do so, are like asses carrying books".
  possession of texts without acting on them is dead weight; a warning for any research body that archives
  and never ships.
- 53:23 "these are nothing but names you have invented yourselves, you and your forefathers. God has sent
  no authority for them. These people merely follow guesswork and the whims of their souls". names
  inherited from forefathers carry no authority by themselves.
- 53:28 "They have no knowledge to base this on: they merely follow guesswork. Guesswork is of no value
  against the Truth". the text separates knowledge from guesswork and gives guesswork no standing.
- 53:30 "Their knowledge does not go beyond that". knowledge that stops at the world of gain is described
  as shallow, not false.
- 57:17 "We have made Our revelation clear to you so that you may use your reason". clarity is given for
  the sake of reason, not in place of it.
- 59:21 "We offer people such illustrations so that they may reflect". parables are a method with a stated
  purpose: reflection.
- 67:3-4 "You will not see any flaw in what the Lord of Mercy creates. Look again! Can you see any flaw
  Look again! And again! Your sight will turn back to you, weak and defeated". the text dares inspection
  three times; it invites scrutiny rather than fearing it.
- 67:10 "If only we had listened, or reasoned, we would not be with the inhabitants of the blazing fire".
  the regret of the lost is named as two failures: not listening and not reasoning.
- 67:23 "It is He who brought you into being, He who endowed you with hearing, sight, and understanding-
  what small thanks you give!" hearing, sight and understanding are gifts to be used; unused, they are
  ingratitude.
- 56:62 "You have learned how you were first created: will you not reflect". knowing a fact is followed by
  the demand to reflect on it.
- 80:1-4 "He frowned and turned away when the blind man came to him–– for all you know, he might have grown
  in spirit or taken note of something useful to him". the prophet is rebuked for turning from an eager
  blind learner; the eager unknown is worth more than the important guest.
- 80:5-10 "For the self-satisfied one you go out of your way–– though you are not to be blamed for his lack
  of spiritual growth–– but from the one who has come to you full of eagerness and awe you allow yourself
  to be distracted". courting the self-satisfied while neglecting the eager is named as a fault.
- 80:11-16 "This [Quran] is a lesson from which those who wish to be taught should learn [written] on
  honoured exalted, pure pages by the hands of noble and virtuous scribes". the scribe is honoured; the
  lesson is for "those who wish to be taught".
- 73:4 "recite the Quran slowly and distinctly". slowness is part of the method of reading.
- 73:6 "Night prayer makes a deeper impression and sharpens words". the quiet hours sharpen language.
- 75:16-19 "do not rush your tongue in an attempt to hasten [your memorization of] the Revelation We shall
  make sure of its safe collection and recitation When We have recited it, repeat the recitation and We
  shall make it clear". collection, recitation, repetition, then clarity: a workflow for taking in a text
  before speaking it.
- 64:6 "they said, 'Should we take guidance from mere mortals?', rejected the message, and turned away".
  the objection that knowledge from ordinary people is worthless is recorded as the objection of the lost.
- 54:24-25 "'What? A man? Why should we follow a lone man from amongst ourselves? That would be
  misguided; quite insane Would a mes-sage be given to him alone out of all of us?'" the same objection
  against a single person from within the group.
- 51:20-21 "On earth there are signs for those with sure faith–– and in yourselves too, do you not see?"
  the self is a site of evidence as much as the earth.
- 50:37 "There truly is a reminder in this for whoever has a heart, whoever listens attentively". the
  reminder's condition is attention, not credentials.
- 69:12 "making that event a reminder for you: attentive ears may take heed". again the gate is
  attentiveness.
- 86:5 "Man should reflect on what he was created from". reflection is an order given to everyone.
- 88:17-20 "Do the disbelievers not see how rain clouds are formed how the heavens are lifted how the
  mountains are raised high how the earth is spread out". a four-line list of things to look at, given
  right before "you are not there to control them".
- 49:16 "Do you presume to teach God about your religion". humility about knowing, put as a question.
- 53:32 "so do not assert your own goodness:He knows best who is mindful of Him". the one who knows does
  not announce their own standing.
- 72:25 "I do not know whether what you have been warned about is near, or whether a distant time has been
  appointed for it by my Lord." the messenger says "I do not know" in the first person.
- 68:36-37 "What is the matter with you? On what basis do you judge Do you have a Scripture that tells
  you". the text asks for the basis of a judgement before accepting it.
- 87:6-8 "We shall teach you [the Quran] and you will not forget–– unless God wishes; He knows both what is
  open and what is hidden–– We shall show you the easy way". teaching comes with an easy way, not a hard
  gate.
- 74:52 "Each one of them demands that a scripture be sent down to him and unrolled before his very
  eyes". the demand for private, personal proof before believing anything shared is named as evasion.

## 2. proof and truth

- 49:6 "Believers, if a troublemaker brings you news, check it first, in case you wrong others unwittingly
  and later regret what you have done". verification before action, with the reason given: unverified
  reports harm others.
- 61:2-3 "why do you say things and then do not do them It is most hateful to God that you say things and
  then do not do them". the gap between saying and doing is not a small fault; it is named as hateful.
- 48:11 "they say with their tongues what is not in their hearts". the stayers-behind are defined by a
  tongue that outruns the heart.
- 52:34 "let them produce one like it, if what they say is true". the standard of proof is a produced
  artefact, not an argument.
- 52:38 "Let their eavesdropper produce clear proof". clear proof is demanded from those who claim access
  to the unseen.
- 68:41 "Let them produce their 'partners', if what they say is true". again: produce it or drop the claim.
- 68:39-40 "Have you received from Us solemn oaths, binding to the Day of Resurrection, that you will get
  whatever you yourselves decide Ask them [Prophet] which of them will guarantee this". a claim needs a
  guarantor; the text asks who will stand behind it.
- 53:3 "he does not speak from his own desire". the messenger's truthfulness rests on not speaking from
  desire.
- 53:11-12 "[The Prophet's] own heart did not distort what he saw Are you going to dispute with him what he
  saw with his own eyes". eyewitness report, undistorted by the heart, is the ground of the claim.
- 69:44-47 "if [the Prophet] had attributed some fabrication to Us We would certainly have seized his right
  hand and cut off his lifeblood and none of you could have defended him". fabrication by the messenger
  himself would have been fatal; no one is exempt from the standard.
- 63:1-2 "they say, 'We bear witness that you are the Messenger of God.' God knows that you truly are His
  Messenger and He bears witness that the hypocrites are liars–– they use their oaths as a cover and so bar
  others from God's way". a true statement can still be a lie when the speaker uses it as cover; testimony
  is judged by its use.
- 58:16 "They have used their oaths to cover up [their false deeds], and barred others from the path of
  God". oaths as camouflage, and the effect is that others are barred.
- 58:14 "knowingly swear to lies". the sin is knowing.
- 65:2 "Call two just witnesses from your people and establish witness for the sake of God". a change of
  state in a household is witnessed by two just people; a record with witnesses.
- 70:32-33 "who are faithful to their trusts and their pledges who give honest testimony". trusts and
  testimony are listed side by side as marks of the righteous.
- 92:6 "who testifies to goodness". testifying is an act of the good, not only of the courtroom.
- 85:3 "by the Witness and that which is witnessed". an oath sworn on witnessing itself.
- 50:17-18 "with two receptors set to record, one on his right side and one on his left he does not utter a
  single word without an ever-present watcher". every word is logged, on both sides.
- 54:52-53 "Everything they do is noted in their records every action, great or small, is recorded". an
  append-only ledger of deeds, small ones included.
- 78:29 "We have recorded everything in a Record". the same claim in four words.
- 82:10-12 "Over you stand watchers, noble recorders who know what you do". the recorders are called noble.
- 69:19-20 "Anyone who is given his Record in his right hand will say, 'Here is my Record, read it I knew I
  would meet my Reckoning,'". the one with nothing to hide hands over the log and says read it.
- 83:7-9 "The list of the wicked is in Sijjin–– what will explain to you what Sijjin is?–– a clearly numbered
  list". and 83:18-21 "The list of the truly good is in 'Illiyyin–– what will explain to you what 'Illiyyin
  is?–– a clearly written list witnessed by those brought near". both lists are described by their
  format: numbered, written, witnessed.
- 75:14-15 "man is a clear witness against himself despite all the excuses he may put forward". the self
  is its own witness; excuses do not change the record.
- 81:24 "He does not withhold what is revealed to him from beyond". against concealing what one has
  received.
- 72:27-28 "He sends watchers to go in front and behind to ensure that each of His messengers delivers his
  Lord's message: He knows all about them, and He takes account of everything". the delivery of a message
  is guarded front and back; integrity of transmission is a concern of the text itself.
- 98:1-2 "were not about to change their ways until they were sent clear evidence a messenger from God,
  reading out pages [blessed with] purity". change waits on clear evidence read aloud.
- 86:13-14 "This is truly a decisive statement it is not something to be taken lightly". the text calls
  itself decisive.
- 51:23 "All this is as real as your speaking". the reality of the claim is compared to the reader's own
  speech.
- 89:5 "is this oath strong enough for a rational person". after four oaths the text turns and asks the
  reader whether the oath suffices; it submits its own rhetoric to judgement.
- 52:41 "Do they have [access to] the unseen? Could they write it down". and 68:47 "Do they have knowledge
  of the unseen that enables them to write it down". the test of a knowledge claim is whether you could
  write it down.

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship, custody

- 70:32 "who are faithful to their trusts and their pledges". a plain statement of amana as a mark of the
  righteous.
- 48:10 "anyone who breaks his pledge does so to his own detriment: God will give a great reward to the one
  who fulfils his pledge to Him". a broken pledge harms the breaker first.
- 76:7 "They fulfil their vows; they fear a day of widespread woes". fulfilment of vows heads the list.
- 81:21 "he is obeyed there and worthy of trust". the bearer of the message is described as trustworthy
  before the message is described.
- 57:7 "give out of what He has made pass down to you". wealth is something that has passed into your
  hands, not something you originated; custody language.
- 57:10 "God alone will inherit what is in the heavens and earth". the final owner is not the holder.
- 67:15 "It is He who has made the earth manageable for you––travel its regions; eat His provision- and to
  Him you will be resurrected". the earth is made manageable for people, with return built in; stewardship
  with a term.
- 55:10 "He set down the Earth for His creatures". the earth is for creatures, not for one owner.
- 66:3 "The Prophet told something in confidence to one of his wives. When she disclosed it [to another
  wife] and God made this known to him". a confidence disclosed is treated as a breach worth a passage of
  scripture; a secret told is a trust held.
- 74:38 "Every soul is held in pledge for its deeds". and 52:21 "each person is in pledge for his own
  deeds". the self is the collateral for its own actions.
- 59:18 "let every soul consider carefully what it sends ahead for tomorrow". an audit of what one is
  sending forward.
- 78:40 "every person will see what their own hands have sent ahead for them". the hand is the seat of
  what is sent.
- 73:20 "He knows that you will not be able to keep a measure of it and He has relented towards all of you,
  so recite as much of the Quran as is easy for you". a duty scaled to capacity; a trust is not a demand
  for the impossible.
- 64:16 "be mindful of God as much as you can; hear and obey; be charitable- it is for your own good". "as
  much as you can" is the measure.

## 4. craft and excellence

- 67:2 "who created death and life to test you [people] and reveal which of you does best". the test of
  life is who does best, not who does most.
- 67:3 "You will not see any flaw in what the Lord of Mercy creates. Look again! Can you see any flaw". the
  standard of good making is that it survives a second look, and a third.
- 50:6 "Do they not see the sky above them––how We have built and adorned it, with no rifts in it". built
  and adorned, both; craft includes beauty, and the proof is no rifts.
- 51:47-48 "We built the heavens with Our power and made them vast We spread out the earth- how well We
  smoothed it out". the maker praises his own smoothing; finish matters.
- 77:23 "We determine [it]: how excellently We determine". excellence in determining, in setting limits.
- 64:3 "He formed you and made your forms good". the form is good, not only functional.
- 95:4 "We create man in the finest state". the finest state as the starting point.
- 54:13-14 "We carried him along on a vessel of planks and nails that floated under Our watchful eye, a
  reward for the one who had been rejected". the ark is named by its materials, and it floats as a reward
  for the rejected builder; craft under mockery is honoured by the thing working.
- 71:5-9 "'My Lord, I have called my people night and day but the more I call them, the further they run
  away ... I have tried calling them openly I have tried preaching to them in public and speaking to them
  in private". Noah's persistence is itemised: night, day, open, public, private; a craftsman's account of
  methods tried.
- 89:7-9 "Iram, [the city] of lofty pillars whose like has never been made in any land and the Thamud, who
  hewed into the rocks in the valley". great craft is acknowledged, and then 89:11-12 "All of them
  committed excesses in their lands and spread corruption there"; skill without justice falls.
- 57:25 "We also sent iron, with its mighty strength and many uses for mankind". a material with "many
  uses" is sent down alongside the book and the balance; tools are part of the shipment.
- 76:22 "This is your reward. Your endeavours are appreciated". effort is seen and thanked.
- 88:9 "well pleased with their labour". the good end is being pleased with one's work.
- 84:6 "you humans, toiling laboriously towards your Lord, will meet Him". toil is the road.
- 90:4 "We have created man for toil and trial". work is the design, not the punishment.
- 94:7 "The moment you are freed [of one task] work on". no idle gap between tasks; the next thing follows.
- 53:39-41 "that man will only have what he has worked towards that his labour will be seen and that in the
  end he will be repaid in full for it". work is seen and paid in full.
- 61:4 "God truly loves those who fight in solid lines for His cause, like a well-compacted wall". the
  image of a good team is a well-made wall.
- 55:5-6 "The sun and the moon follow their calculated courses the plants and the trees submit to His
  designs". calculation and design are the words for creation.
- 87:2-3 "who created [all things] in due proportion who determined their destinies and guided them".
  proportion first.
- 54:49 "We have created all things in due measure". and 65:3 "God has set a due measure for everything".
  measure as the mark of the maker.
- 48:29 "like a seed that puts forth its shoot, becomes strong, grows thick, and rises on its stem to the
  delight of its sowers". a group's growth is a plant that delights the ones who sowed it.
- 73:6 "sharpens words". a craft of language, done in the quiet.

## 5. justice and balance

- 55:7-9 "He has set the balance so that you may not exceed in the balance weigh with justice and do not
  fall short in the balance". the balance is cosmic before it is commercial; not exceeding and not falling
  short are both faults.
- 57:25 "We sent Our messengers with clear signs, the Scripture and the Balance, so that people could
  uphold justice: We also sent iron". the book, the balance, and iron arrive together, and the stated
  purpose is that people could uphold justice.
- 83:1-3 "Woe to those who give short measure who demand of other people full measure for themselves but
  give less than they should when it is they who weigh or measure for others". the asymmetry is the sin:
  full for me, short for you.
- 59:7 "this is so that they do not just circulate among those of you who are rich". distribution is
  designed against the closed circuit of wealth.
- 59:9 "They give them preference over themselves, even if they too are poor: those who are saved from
  their own souls' greed are truly successful". the poor giving preference to the poorer; success is
  defined as being saved from one's own greed.
- 64:16 "Those who are saved from their own meanness will be the prosperous ones". prosperity is the
  absence of meanness.
- 70:18 "amasses wealth and hoards it". hoarding listed among what the flame claims.
- 104:1-3 "Woe to every fault-finding backbiter who amasses riches, counting them over thinking they will
  make him live for ever". counting riches over is paired with fault-finding; the hoarder and the critic
  are one person.
- 102:1-2 "Striving for more distracts you until you go into your graves". accumulation as distraction to
  the grave.
- 100:8 "he is truly excessive in his love of wealth". stated flatly.
- 89:17-20 "You [people] do not honour orphans you do not urge one another to feed the poor you consume
  inheritance greedily and you love wealth with a passion". four charges, all about who gets what.
- 51:19 "giving a rightful share of their wealth to the beggar and the deprived". and 70:24-25 "who give a
  due share of their wealth to beggars and the deprived". the share is rightful and due, not charity.
- 54:28 "Tell them the water is to be shared between them: each one should drink in turn." a commons with
  a turn rule; the crime that follows (54:29) is killing the camel that shared it.
- 68:24 "'Make sure no poor person enters the garden today!'". the plot to exclude the poor is the whole
  sin of the garden owners.
- 63:7 "'Give nothing to those who follow God's Messenger, until they abandon him', but to God belong the
  treasures of the heavens and earth". economic siege as a tactic, answered by who owns the treasures.
- 63:8 "'Once we return to Medina the powerful will drive out the weak,' but power belongs to God". the
  rigged default stated by its own beneficiaries, and denied.
- 49:9 "then make a just and even-handed reconciliation between the two of them: God loves those who are
  even-handed". even-handedness is loved.
- 60:8 "He does not forbid you to deal kindly and justly with anyone who has not fought you for your faith
  or driven you out of your homes: God loves the just". justice extends to those outside the group.
- 101:6-9 "the one whose good deeds are heavy on the scales will have a pleasant life but the one whose
  good deeds are light will have the Bottomless Pit for his home". the scales weigh deeds, not claims.
- 99:7-8 "whoever has done an atom's-weight of good will see it but whoever has done an atom's-weight of
  evil will see that". the smallest unit still registers.
- 53:22 "That would be a most unjust distribution!". an unjust distribution is a recognisable wrong even
  in a dispute about idols.
- 57:24 "God does not love the conceited, the boastful, those who are miserly, and who tell other people to
  be miserly". telling others to be miserly is its own item.
- 52:40 "Do you [Prophet] demand a payment from them that would burden them with debt". and 68:46 "Do you
  demand some reward from them that would burden them with debt". the messenger charges nothing that
  indebts; guidance is not sold on credit.
- 65:7 "let the wealthy man spend according to his wealth. But let him whose provision is restricted spend
  according to what God has given him: God does not burden any soul with more than He has given it".
  proportional obligation.
- 90:13-16 "It is to free a slave to feed at a time of hunger an orphaned relative or a poor person in
  distress". the steep path is defined by transfers to the powerless.
- 107:2-3 "It is he who pushes aside the orphan and does not urge others to feed the needy". denying the
  judgement is defined by two economic acts.

## 6. agency and self-change

- 53:38-41 "that no soul shall bear the burden of another that man will only have what he has worked
  towards that his labour will be seen and that in the end he will be repaid in full for it". four lines
  from the older scriptures: own burden, own work, seen, paid.
- 92:4-10 "The ways you take differ greatly There is the one who gives, who is mindful of God who
  testifies to goodness–– We shall smooth his way towards ease There is the one who is miserly, who is
  self-satisfied who denies goodness–– We shall smooth his way towards hardship". the way is smoothed in
  the direction you already take; agency compounds.
- 91:7-10 "by the soul and how He formed it and inspired it [to know] its own rebellion and piety The one
  who purifies his soul succeeds and the one who corrupts it fails". the soul knows its own two directions
  from the start; success and failure are done to the self by the self.
- 74:37-38 "to those of you who choose to go ahead and those who lag behind Every soul is held in pledge
  for its deeds". going ahead or lagging is a choice.
- 73:19 "This is a reminder. Let whoever wishes take the way to his Lord". repeated at 76:29 and 78:39 in
  nearly the same words; the way is offered to "whoever wishes".
- 74:55 "Let whoever wishes to take heed do so". the same door.
- 81:26-28 "So where are you [people] going This is a message for all people for those who wish to take the
  straight path". a question about direction followed by an offer.
- 82:19 "The Day when no soul will be able to do anything for another". the limit of delegation.
- 59:2 "their homes were destroyed by their own hands, and the hands of the believers". the fortified
  people ruined themselves first.
- 62:7 "because of what they have stored up for themselves with their own hands". again the own hand.
- 79:40-41 "for anyone who feared the meeting with his Lord and restrained himself from base desires
  Paradise will be home". self-restraint is the act named.
- 87:14 "Prosperous are those who purify themselves". reflexive verb.
- 90:10-11 "and point out to him the two clear ways [of good and evil] Yet he has not attempted the steep
  path". two ways shown; the steep one not even attempted.
- 84:19 "you will progress from stage to stage". and 71:14 "when He has created you stage by stage". change
  is staged, not sudden.
- 75:2 "and by the self-reproaching soul". the conscience is sworn by.
- 75:13 "On that Day, man will be told what he put first and what he put last". priorities are what is
  judged.
- 89:27-28 "'[But] you, soul at peace return to your Lord well pleased and well pleasing". the end state of
  the changed self.
- 57:22-23 "so you need not grieve for what you miss or gloat over what you gain". a steadiness that frees
  action from outcome.
- 65:1 "for you cannot know what new situation God may perhaps bring about". a reason not to burn a
  bridge: the future is open.
- 64:2 "It is He who created you, yet some of you disbelieve and some believe". the same origin, different
  choices.

## 7. time and mortality

- 103:1-3 "By the declining day man is [deep] in loss except for those who believe, do good deeds, urge one
  another to the truth, and urge one another to steadfastness". the whole surah: the day is declining, the
  default is loss, the exception is four acts, two of them done to one another.
- 102:1-2 "Striving for more distracts you until you go into your graves". accumulation ends at the grave.
- 62:8 "The death you run away from will come to meet you". the direction of running does not matter.
- 63:10-11 "Give out of what We have provided for you, before death comes to one of you and he says, 'My
  Lord, if You would only reprieve me for a little while, I would give in charity and become one of the
  righteous.' God does not reprieve a soul when its turn comes". the deferred good deed and the refused
  extension.
- 55:26-27 "Everyone on earth perishes all that remains is the Face of your Lord, full of majesty,
  bestowing honour". finitude in two lines.

- 70:6-7 "The disbelievers think it is distant but We know it to be close". what looks distant is close.

- 57:20 "the present life is just a game, a diversion, an attraction, a cause of boasting among you, of

- 56:60 "We ordained death to be among you. Nothing could stop Us". death is ordained "among you", inside

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- 59:19 "Do not be like those who forget God, so God causes them to forget their own souls: they are the
  rebellious ones". forgetting the source leads to forgetting the self; note that the word for them is
  "rebellious", so the maker's rebellion has to be the other kind.
- 96:6-7 "But man exceeds all bounds when he thinks he is self-sufficient". the root of transgression is
  the feeling of needing nothing.
- 89:11-12 "All of them committed excesses in their lands and spread corruption there". excess and
  corruption in one breath, about the greatest builders.
- 79:24 "'I am your supreme lord,'". pharaoh's whole speech.
- 91:11 "In their arrogant cruelty, the people of Thamud called [their messenger] a liar". arrogance and
  cruelty as one word pair.
- 74:18-25 "He planned and plotted–– devilishly he plotted ferociously he plotted!–– and looked and frowned
  and scowled and turned away and behaved arrogantly and said, 'This is just old sorcery just the talk of a
  mortal!'". the anatomy of dismissal: plot, look, frown, scowl, turn, sneer.
- 54:2 "Whenever the disbelievers see a sign, they turn away and say, 'Same old sorcery!'". the reflex
  dismissal of anything new as the same old thing.
- 68:10-15 "do not yield to any contemptible swearer to any backbiter, slander-monger or hinderer of good,
  to anyone who is sinful, aggressive coarse, and on top of all that, an imposter Just because he has
  wealth and sons when our revelations are recited to him, he says, 'These are just ancient fables.'". a
  profile of the corrupt influencer, ending in his wealth and his sneer.
- 83:14 "Their hearts are encrusted with what they have done". deeds crust the heart.
- 63:3 "so their hearts have been sealed and they do not understand". sealing follows a profession later
  rejected.
- 63:4 "they are like propped-up tim-bers––they think every cry they hear is against them". hollow
  structures that hear every noise as an attack.
- 59:13-14 "you think they are united but their hearts are divided because they are people devoid of
  reason". apparent unity, divided hearts, and the cause is given as lack of reason.
- 59:2 "they themselves thought their fortifications would protect them against God. God came up on them
  from where they least expected". the fortress falls from the unexpected side.
- 56:45-46 "Before, they overindulged in luxury and persisted in great sin". luxury named first.
- 73:11 "leave to Me those who deny the truth and live in luxury". again luxury and denial paired.
- 74:11-15 "the one I created helpless then gave vast wealth and sons by his side making everything easy
  for him–– yet he still hopes I will give him more". ease breeds appetite for more.
- 71:21-22 "followed those whose riches and children only increase their ruin who have made a grand plan".
  a grand plan by the rich, and it increases their ruin.
- 57:27 "But monasticism was something they invented- We did not ordain it for them- only to seek God's
  pleasure, and even so, they did not observe it properly". inventing a rule you then fail to keep.
- 50:25 "everyone who hindered good, was aggressive, caused others to doubt". hindering good and seeding
  doubt are listed as crimes in themselves.
- 89:15-16 "when his Lord tries him through honour and blessings, he says, 'My Lord has honoured me,' but
  when He tries him through the restriction of his provision, he says, 'My Lord has humiliated me.'". both
  wealth and poverty misread as verdicts.
- 70:19-21 "Man was truly created anxious he is fretful when misfortune touches him but tight-fisted when
  good fortune comes his way". the default human setting: anxious, fretful, tight-fisted.
- 75:33 "walking back to his people with a conceited swagger". the swagger after denial.
- 95:5 "then reduce him to the lowest of the low". decay from the finest state.
- 87:4-5 "who brought out the green pasture then made it dark debris". pasture to debris; decay is in the
  order of things.
- 113:5 "the harm in the envier when he envies". envy named as a harm to seek refuge from.
- 114:4-5 "against the harm of the slinking whisperer–– who whispers into the hearts of people". the
  whisperer that slinks; a good name for an algorithm.
- 57:14 "you allowed yourselves to be tempted, you were hesitant, doubtful, deceived by false hopes". a
  four-part diagnosis of drift.
- 58:19 "Satan has gained control over them and made them forget God". forgetting is induced from outside.
- 69:8 "Can you see any trace of them now". the disappearance of the mighty.
- 105:2 "Did He not utterly confound their plans". a big army's plan confounded by birds.

## 9. community and mutual aid

- 49:13 "People, We created you all from a single man and a single woman, and made you into races and
  tribes so that you should recognize one another. In God's eyes, the most honoured of you are the ones
  most mindful of Him". difference exists for recognition, and honour is not tribal.
- 49:10 "The believers are brothers, so make peace between your two brothers". peacemaking as the duty of
  siblings.
- 49:9 "If two groups of the believers fight, you [believers] should try to reconcile them". the first
  response to internal conflict is reconciliation.
- 49:11 "no one group of men should jeer at another, who may after all be better than them ... do not
  speak ill of one another; do not use offensive nicknames for one another". mockery is forbidden on the
  ground that the mocked may be better.
- 49:12 "avoid making too many assumptions- some assumptions are sinful- and do not spy on one another or
  speak ill of people behind their backs: would any of you like to eat the flesh of your dead brother".
  three rules for a group: do not assume, do not surveil, do not backbite.
- 58:11 "if you are told to make room for one another in your assemblies, then do so, and God will make
  room for you". making room is repaid with room.
- 58:9 "when you converse in secret, do not do so in a way that is sinful, hostile, and disobedient to the
  Messenger, but in a way that is good and mindful". private channels are allowed if their content is good.
- 59:9 "show love for those who migrated to them for refuge and harbour no desire in their hearts for what
  has been given to them. They give them preference over themselves, even if they too are poor". the
  hosts of refugees; preference for the newcomer.
- 59:10 "'Lord, forgive us our sins and the sins of our brothers who believed before us, and leave no
  malice in our hearts towards those who believe'". the later generation prays for the earlier one and
  for no malice.
- 61:4 "those who fight in solid lines for His cause, like a well-compacted wall". the team as masonry.
- 61:14 "'Who will come with me to help God?' The disciples said, 'We shall be God's helpers.'". a call
  and a reply; recruitment by open question.
- 65:6 "Consult together in a good way- if you make difficulties for one another, another woman may suckle
  the child". consultation even in separation, with the cost of not consulting spelled out.
- 103:3 "urge one another to the truth, and urge one another to steadfastness". the exception to loss is
  mutual: one another, twice.
- 90:17 "and to be one of those who believe and urge one another to steadfastness and compassion". the
  same reciprocal verb, with compassion added.
- 48:29 "harsh towards the disbelievers and compassionate towards each other". compassion inside the
  group is the picture; the harshness clause is a caution (see section 16).
- 57:27 "put compassion and mercy into the hearts of his followers". a movement defined by what was put
  in its followers' hearts.
- 51:25-27 "'Peace,' he said, [adding to himself] 'These people are strangers.' He turned quickly to his
  household, brought out a fat calf and placed it before them. 'Will you not eat?' he said". hospitality
  to strangers before knowing their errand.
- 60:7 "God may still bring about affection between you and your present enemies". enmity is not
  permanent.
- 76:8-9 "they give food to the poor, the orphan, and the captive, though they love it themselves saying,
  'We feed you for the sake of God alone: We seek neither recompense nor thanks from you". giving what
  you love, to a captive, without thanks.
- 62:9-10 "hurry towards the reminder of God and leave off your trading ... then when the prayer has
  ended, disperse in the land and seek out God's bounty". gather, then disperse; a rhythm of assembly and
  work.
- 72:11 "Some of us are righteous and others less so: we follow different paths". a community that admits
  its own plurality.
- 57:13 "A wall with a door will be erected between them: inside it lies mercy, outside lies torment". the
  wall has a door; the inside is defined by mercy.
- 48:25 "If there had not been among them, unknown to you, believing men and women whom you would have
  trampled underfoot". restraint against an enemy because unknown allies live among them.
- 70:10-11 "when no friend will ask about his friend even when they are within sight of one another". the
  limit of community on the last day; friendship is for now.

## 10. humility, moderation and warmth

- 49:2-3 "do not raise your voices above the Prophet's ... It is those who lower their voices in the
  presence of God's Messenger whose hearts God has proved to be aware". lowered voices as a sign of an
  aware heart.
- 49:4-5 "most of those who shout to you [Prophet] from outside your private rooms lack understanding It
  would have been better for them if they had waited patiently for you to come out to them". shouting
  from outside is lack of understanding; waiting is better.
- 93:3-5 "your Lord has not forsaken you [Prophet], nor does He hate you and the future will be better for
  you than the past your Lord is sure to give you so much that you will be well satisfied". consolation
  addressed to a discouraged person.
- 93:6-8 "Did He not find you an orphan and shelter you Did He not find you lost and guide you Did He not
  find you in need and make you self-sufficient". the one who made it out is reminded he was found.
- 93:9-11 "So do not be harsh with the orphan and do not chide the one who asks for help talk about the
  blessings of your Lord". the ethic of the one who was helped: be gentle with the one who asks, and say
  what you were given.
- 94:5-6 "So truly where there is hardship there is also ease truly where there is hardship there is also
  ease". said twice, word for word.
- 65:7 "after hardship, God will bring ease". the same promise in a law about maintenance payments.
- 65:4 "God makes things easy for those who are mindful of Him". ease as a reward.
- 73:10 "patiently endure what they say, ignore them politely". the manner of ignoring is specified:
  politely.
- 64:14 "if you overlook their offences, forgive them, pardon them, then God is all forgiving, all
  merciful". three verbs of letting go, even towards enemies in one's own house.
- 53:32 "your Lord is ample in forgiveness ... so do not assert your own goodness". forgiveness paired with
  not claiming goodness.
- 49:17 "'Do not consider your submission a favour to me; it is God who has done you a favour'". joining
  is not a favour to the founder.
- 85:14 "He is the Most Forgiving, the Most Loving". loving is one of the names given here.
- 74:6-7 "do not be overwhelmed and weaken be steadfast in your Lord's cause". against being overwhelmed;
  steadiness is the answer.
- 54:10 "he called upon his Lord, 'I am defeated: help me!'". Noah says out loud that he is defeated.
- 68:48-50 "do not be like the man in the whale who called out in distress if his Lord's grace had not
  reached him, he would have been left, abandoned and blameworthy, on the barren shore but his Lord chose
  him". impatience, the barren shore, and being chosen anyway.
- 110:1-3 "When God's help comes and He opens up your way [Prophet] when you see people embracing God's
  faith in crowds celebrate the praise of your Lord and ask His forgiveness". at the moment of victory the
  instruction is praise and asking forgiveness, not triumph.
- 48:27 "God knew what you did not". humility about foresight.
- 67:22 "Who is better guided: someone who falls on his face, or someone who walks steadily on a straight
  path". steady walking over falling forward.
- 56:25-26 "They will hear no idle or sinful talk there only clean and wholesome speech". the mark of the
  good place is the quality of its talk; also 88:11 "where they will hear no idle talk" and 78:35 "There
  they will hear no vain or lying talk".
- 52:12 "who amuse themselves with idle chatter". and 70:42 "leave them to wallow in idle talk". idle
  talk is a mark of the lost.
- 53:59-61 "Do you [people] marvel at this Why do you laugh instead of weeping Why do you pay no heed".
  three questions about the wrong response.
- 57:23 "you need not grieve for what you miss or gloat over what you gain". moderation in both
  directions.
- 97:5 "[there is] peace that night until the break of dawn". peace as the content of the best night.
- 51:28 "but they said, 'Do not be afraid.'". the guests calm the host.
- 55:1-2 "It is the Lord of Mercy who taught the Quran". mercy is the name attached to teaching.

- 81:1-14 "When the sun is shrouded in darkness when the stars are dimmed when the mountains are set in

  nature undone, line by line.
- 86:11-12 "By the sky and its recurring rain by the earth that cracks open". rain that recurs; earth that

- 51:49 "and We created pairs of all things so that you [people] might take note". pairs as a designed

- 50:16 "We are closer to him than his jugular vein". nearness as anatomy.
- 75:4 "In fact, We can reshape his very fingertips". fingertips as the finest detail of making.
- 53:1 "By the star when it sets". and 53:49 "that He is the Lord of Sirius". a named star.
- 56:75-76 "I swear by the positions of the stars a mighty oath, if you only knew". the positions of

## 12. building and repairing stories

- 54:9-15 Noah: "Noah was rebuked and so he called upon his Lord, 'I am defeated: help me!' ... We carried
  him along on a vessel of planks and nails that floated under Our watchful eye, a reward for the one who
  had been rejected We have left this as a sign: will anyone take heed". defeat admitted, a thing built of
  planks and nails, and the built thing left as a sign.
- 69:11-12 "when the Flood rose high, We saved you in the floating ship making that event a reminder for
  you: attentive ears may take heed". the ship as a reminder for attentive ears.
- 71:5-9 Noah's methods (quoted in section 4): night and day, openly, in public, in private. a builder's
  log of outreach.
- 89:7-9 "Iram, [the city] of lofty pillars whose like has never been made in any land and the Thamud, who
  hewed into the rocks in the valley". builders remembered for their craft and their fall.
- 59:2 "they themselves thought their fortifications would protect them against God ... their homes were
  destroyed by their own hands". the fortress that failed from within.
- 59:14 "Even united they would never fight you, except from within fortified strongholds or behind high
  walls". high walls as the sign of a divided people.
- 61:4 "like a well-compacted wall". the good wall: people standing in line.
- 57:13 "A wall with a door will be erected between them: inside it lies mercy, outside lies torment". the
  wall that separates has a door.
- 66:11 "Pharaoh's wife, who said, 'Lord, build me a house near You in the Garden. Save me from Pharaoh and

- 68:17-32 the garden: "who swore that they would harvest its fruits in the morning and made no allowance

- 85:4-8 "damned were the makers of the trench the makers of the fuel-stoked fire They sat down to watch

- 105:1-2 "Do you [Prophet] not see how your Lord dealt with the army of the elephant Did He not utterly
  confound their plans". the largest machine of its day, undone.
- 106:4 "who provides them with food to ward off hunger, safety to ward off fear". the two things a town
  needs: food and safety.
- 62:2 "It is He who raised a mes-senger, among the people who had no Scripture". a project started among
  people with nothing.
- 50:6 "how We have built and adorned it, with no rifts in it". the model building.
- 79:27-28 "the sky that He built raising it high and perfecting it". built, raised, perfected: three
  stages.
- 78:12 "Did We not build seven strong [heavens] above you". strength as the adjective for the built.
- 57:25 "We also sent iron, with its mighty strength and many uses for mankind, so that God could mark out
  those who would help Him and His messengers". iron is sent to find out who will help; the material tests
  the helper.
- 48:29 the seed that "rises on its stem to the delight of its sowers". growth as the sowers' reward.

## 13. freedom and no compulsion

- 88:21-22 "So [Prophet] warn them: your only task is to give warning you are not there to control them".
  the job description ends with a limit on the job.
- 50:45 "You [Prophet] are not there to force them, so remind, with this Quran, those who fear My warning".
  not force; remind.
- 109:1-6 "Say [Prophet], 'Disbelievers I do not worship what you worship you do not worship what I worship
  I will never worship what you worship you will never worship what I worship you have your religion and I
  have mine.'". the whole surah is a refusal to merge and a refusal to coerce.
- 73:19 "This is a reminder. Let whoever wishes take the way to his Lord". the door is "whoever wishes";
  the same words recur at 76:29 and 78:39, and 74:55 "Let whoever wishes to take heed do so".
- 81:27-28 "This is a message for all people for those who wish to take the straight path". for all, and
  for those who wish.
- 64:12 "If you turn away, remember that Our Messenger's duty is only to make plain his message". the duty
  is to make plain, nothing more.
- 67:26 "my only duty is to give clear warning". the same limit in the first person.
- 72:21 "Say, 'I have no control over any harm or good that may befall you.'". a messenger disclaims power
  over outcomes.
- 72:23 "I only deliver [what I receive] from God- only His messages.'". delivery, not enforcement.
- 83:33 "though they were not sent to be their keepers". the mockers are told they are not keepers; by the
  same rule neither is anyone else.
- 80:7 "though you are not to be blamed for his lack of spiritual growth". the teacher is not responsible
  for the refusal of the rich.
- 51:54 "so ignore them [Prophet]- you are not to blame". and 51:55 "and go on reminding [people], it is
  good for those who believe to be reminded". not to blame, and keep reminding.
- 87:9 "So remind, if reminding will help". reminding is conditional on its usefulness.
- 68:8-9 "So do not yield to those who deny the truth–– they want you to compromise with them and then
  they will compromise with you". the mechanism of co-option is described: mutual compromise offered.
- 96:9-10 "Have you seen the man who forbids [Our] servant to pray". and 96:19 "No! Do not obey him
  [Prophet]: bow down in worship and draw close". the one who forbids another's worship is the figure of
  the gatekeeper, and the answer is not to obey him.
- 72:18 "Places of worship are for God alone- so do not pray to anyone other than God". the space of
  worship belongs to no person.
- 92:12 "Our part is to provide guidance". the divine part is limited to guidance.
- 76:3 "We guided him to the right path, whether he was grateful or not". guidance given regardless of
  gratitude.
- 49:14 "'You do not have faith. What you should say instead is, "We have submitted," for faith has not
  yet entered your hearts.'". faith cannot be declared into existence; words and inner state are kept
  distinct.
- 60:4 "'We disown you and what you worship besides God! We renounce you!'". freedom includes the freedom
  to refuse and walk away.
- 48:15 "They want to change God's words, but tell them [Prophet], 'You may not come with us'". those who
  stayed away cannot rewrite the terms to join later gains.

## 14. clarity and plain speech

- 55:4 "and taught him to communicate". haleem's word for bayan; clear communication is a taught faculty.
- 75:19 "and We shall make it clear". clarity is promised as the last step after collection and
  recitation.
- 57:17 "We have made Our revelation clear to you so that you may use your reason". clear so that reason
  works.
- 65:11 "reciting to you God's revelations that make things clear- to bring those who believe and do
  righteous deeds from darkness into light". clarity is the movement from darkness to light.
- 71:2 "'My people, I am here to warn you plainly". plainly is the manner.
- 51:50-51 "'I am sent by Him to give you clear warning ... I am sent by Him to give you clear warning!'".
  clear warning, said twice in two verses.
- 86:13-14 "This is truly a decisive statement it is not something to be taken lightly". decisive is the
  word the text uses for itself.
- 83:9 "a clearly numbered list". and 83:20 "a clearly written list". the records of good and evil are
  described by their legibility.
- 98:1 "until they were sent clear evidence". and 98:4 "became divided only after they were sent [such]
  clear evi-dence". clear evidence is what changes people, and also what they split over.
- 73:4 "recite the Quran slowly and distinctly". slow and distinct delivery.
- 73:6 "sharpens words". words can be sharpened.
- 59:21 "We offer people such illustrations so that they may reflect". the illustration is a method.
- 66:10 "God has given examples of disbelievers". and 66:11 "God has also given examples of believers".
  examples for both sides, named people.
- 54:17 "We have made it easy to learn lessons from the Quran". ease of learning as a design claim.
- 69:40-42 "this [Quran] is the speech of an honoured messenger not the words of a poet- how little you
  believe nor the words of a soothsayer- how little you reflect". the text distinguishes itself from
  poetry and oracle; it wants to be read as plain speech.
- 52:29 "you are neither oracle nor madman". the same denial.

## 16. warnings for the manifesto's own posture

- 61:2-3 (quoted above). the manifesto will be measured against what the workshop actually ships.
- 53:32 "so do not assert your own goodness". a manifesto is an assertion; this verse asks it not to be
  about its author's goodness.
- 49:1 "Believers, do not push yourselves forward in the presence of God and His Messenger". against
  pushing oneself forward.
- 49:11 "no one group of men should jeer at another, who may after all be better than them". mockery of
  the mainstream, of corporations, of "slop", has to pass this test: they may be better than us.
- 49:12 "avoid making too many assumptions- some assumptions are sinful- and do not spy on one another".
  a manifesto that diagnoses "the system" runs on assumptions; some of them are sinful.
- 104:1 "Woe to every fault-finding backbiter". the critic of defaults must not become the fault-finder.
- 80:1-10 the frown. an organisation courting the self-satisfied (funders, the technical elite) while
  turning from the blind eager learner repeats the fault the prophet was rebuked for.
- 83:29-33 "The wicked used to laugh at the believers–– they would wink at one another when the believers
  passed by them joke about them when they got back to their own people and say, when they saw them,
  'These people are misguided,' though they were not sent to be their keepers". winking, in-jokes, and
  calling others misguided are the manners of the wicked here; the playful, weird workshop should keep its
  play from becoming this.
- 96:6-7 "man exceeds all bounds when he thinks he is self-sufficient". a tool that makes people
  self-sufficient must not make them think they are.
- 107:4-7 "So woe to those who pray but are heedless of their prayer those who are all show and forbid
  common kindnesses". the show without the substance, and the forbidding of small kindnesses.
- 57:24 "God does not love the conceited, the boastful". against boastful copy.
- 57:20 "a cause of boasting among you, of rivalry in wealth and children". rivalry named as part of the
  game.
- 75:33 "walking back to his people with a conceited swagger". the swagger of the rebel.
- 89:15-16 (section 8). reading one's own success as honour and one's setbacks as humiliation.
- 49:17 "Do not consider your submission a favour to me". members joining sandbox are not doing the maker a
  favour, and the maker is not doing them one.
- 63:1 "they say, 'We bear witness that you are the Messenger of God.'". a true sentence used as cover; a
  manifesto's true sentences can be used the same way.
- 98:4 "became divided only after they were sent [such] clear evi-dence". radical clarity does not prevent
  splitting into sects; it can cause it.
- 57:27 "monasticism was something they invented- We did not ordain it for them ... and even so, they did

- 66:1 "why do you prohibit what God has made lawful to you in your desire to please your wives". against

- 68:9 "they want you to compromise with them and then they will compromise with you". the offer of

- 74:6 "do not be overwhelmed and weaken". the risk of the small workshop.
- 71:26 "'Lord, do not leave any of the disbelievers on the earth". even Noah, after every method failed,

- 48:29 "harsh towards the disbelievers and compassionate towards each other". in-group compassion with

- 66:9 "Deal with them sternly". sternness exists in the text; the manifesto should know where it is and

- 102:1 "Striving for more distracts you". a workshop that "moves fast" can be striving for more.
- 54:24 "'Why should we follow a lone man from amongst ourselves?'". the objection the manifesto will

- 62:5 "like asses carrying books". a research organisation that publishes without acting.
- 59:19 (section 8). forgetting the source leads to forgetting the self; the "rebellious ones" is the

- 88:22 "you are not there to control them". the manifesto cannot control its readers, and should not

## how the text itself speaks

this chunk is where the form is hardest and most learnable. the devices, as met:

1. oaths on things. the surahs open by swearing on winds (51:1-4), a mountain and "a Scripture inscribed
   in unrolled parch-ment" (52:1-3), a star (53:1), "the positions of the stars" (56:75), the pen (68:1),
   "what you can see and by what you cannot see" (69:38-39), "the moon ... the departing night ... the
   shining dawn" (74:32-34), "the self-reproaching soul" (75:2), "the forceful chargers" (79:1), sunset and
   full moon (84:16-18), the sky "with its towering constellations" (85:1), "the night-comer" (86:1), "the
   Ten Nights ... the even and the odd" (89:2-3), "this city" (90:1), the sun through to the soul (91:1-7),
   "the enshrouding night" (92:1), "the morning brightness" (93:1), "the fig, by the olive" (95:1), "the
   charging steeds that pant" (100:1), "the declining day" (103:1). the oath is on the made and natural
   world, not on the speaker. then 89:5 breaks the frame: "is this oath strong enough for a rational
   person". a manifesto can open on a concrete thing and then ask the reader whether it holds.
2. name it, ask what it is, answer. "The Inevitable Hour What is the Inevitable Hour What will explain to
   you what the Inevitable Hour is" (69:1-3). the same three-beat at 74:27, 77:14, 82:17-18, 83:8, 83:19,
   86:2, 90:12, 97:2, 101:2-3, 101:10, 104:5. a term is introduced, the reader is told they do not know
   it, then it is defined. this is how to introduce a coined word.
3. refrains. 54:17 four times; "Which, then, of your Lord's blessings do you both deny" thirty-one times
   in surah 55; "Woe, on that Day, to those who denied the truth" ten times in surah 77; "How [terrible]
   was My punishment and [the fulfilment of] My warnings" at 54:16, 54:18, 54:21, 54:30; "No indeed!" as a
   hinge at 83:7, 83:14, 83:15, 83:18, 89:17, 89:21, 102:3-5, 104:4. the refrain makes a list into a
   liturgy; each new item is checked against the same line.
4. the "Did We not" catechism. 56:57-73 ("Consider the seeds you sow ... is it you who make them grow or
   We"), 77:20-27, 78:6-16, 93:6-8 ("Did He not find you an orphan and shelter you"), 94:1-4, 105:1-2,
   90:8-10 ("Did We not give him eyes a tongue, lips"). questions whose answer the reader already has.
5. direct address. "Believers" or "You who believe" opens 49:1, 49:2, 49:6, 49:11, 49:12, 58:9, 58:11,
   58:12, 59:18, 60:1, 60:10, 60:13, 61:2, 61:10, 61:14, 63:9, 64:14, 66:6, 66:8. then the wider address:
   "People" (49:13), "Mankind, what has lured you away from God" (82:6), "So where are you [people] going"
   (81:26), "Is it not time for believers" (57:16). the plural you is the reader's group, and the text
   switches between the inner circle and everyone.
6. the "Say" formula. surahs 109, 112, 113 and 114 are each a single "Say" with the words to say; 67:23-30
   is a run of seven "Say" openings; 72:20-25 five. the text hands the reader a script in the first
   person.
7. contrast pairs. "There is the one who gives ... There is the one who is miserly" (92:5-8); "Those on
   the Right ... Those on the Left" (56:8-9); "some faces will be beaming ... some faces will be
   dust-stained" (80:38-40); "whoever is given his record in his right hand ... whoever is given his record
   from behind his back" (84:7-10); "downcast faces ... faces radiant with bliss" (88:2, 88:8); "The one
   who purifies his soul succeeds and the one who corrupts it fails" (91:9-10); "an atom's-weight of good
   ... an atom's-weight of evil" (99:7-8). two columns, no third.
8. numbered lists of duties and of vices. the righteous at 70:22-34: pray, constant, give a due share,
   believe in the Day, fear, guard chastity, "faithful to their trusts and their pledges", "give honest
   testimony", steadfast in prayer. the steep path at 90:13-17. the confession of the lost at 74:43-46:
   "'We did not pray we did not feed the poor we indulged with others [in mocking the believers] we denied
   the Day of Judgement". the vices at 68:10-13. the pledge of the women at 60:12. the profile of the good
   at 51:17-19 and 76:7-10. duties are listed in the plural and in the order they are done.
9. promise paired with warning. 57:12-15 (light for the believers, "Go back and look for a light" for the
   hypocrites); 92:7 and 92:10 (ease, hardship); 85:10-11; 88:2-16; 65:8-11. the text never gives the
   reward without the cost on the facing page.
10. story in miniature. the garden owners (68:17-32) is sixteen verses and has a plot, a villain plural, a
    wise minority voice, a reversal, reproach and hope. the frown (80:1-10), the elephant (105), Abu Lahab
    (111), the five peoples of surah 54, Abraham's guests (51:24-37), Moses at Tuwa (79:15-26). stories
    are told in under twenty lines and end with the lesson stated.
11. reported dialogue. the keepers of hell ask "'Did no one come to warn you?'" and the answer is "'Yes, a
    warner did come to us, but we did not believe him'" (67:8-9). the garden owners: "'Did I not say to
    you, "Will you not glorify God?"'" (68:28). the hypocrites: "'Were we not with you?' They will reply,
    'Yes. But you allowed yourselves to be tempted'" (57:14). judgement is dramatised as conversation.
12. the shape of the short surah: oath, verdict, exception. surah 103 is three verses: "By the declining
    day man is [deep] in loss except for those who believe, do good deeds, urge one another to the truth,
    and urge one another to steadfastness". surah 112 is four lines of creed. surah 108 is three: gift,
    instruction, dismissal of the hater. surah 110 is three: help comes, crowds come, praise and ask
    forgiveness. a manifesto section can be this short.
13. "Look again" (67:3-4). the same instruction repeated three times with escalation: "Look again! Can you
    see any flaw Look again! And again!".
14. the tender turn to the discouraged speaker. surahs 93 and 94 are addressed to the prophet's own low
    point: "your Lord has not forsaken you [Prophet], nor does He hate you" (93:3); "Did We not relieve
    your heart for you [Prophet] and remove the burden that weighed so heavily on your back" (94:1-3). the
    text speaks to its own author's weariness, in public.
15. the first-person "I do not know". "I do not know whether what you have been warned about is near"
    (72:25); "I have no control over any harm or good that may befall you" (72:21). the messenger's
    authority includes stated limits.
16. the hinge of denial: "No!" and "No indeed!". after a false expectation is voiced, the text says "No!" and corrects:
    75:11 "Truly, there is no refuge"; 96:15 "No! If he does not stop"; 102:3-4 "No indeed! You will come to
    know No indeed! In the end you will come to know". denial then correction, in that order.
17. paired opposites inside one line. "the First and the Last; the Outer and the Inner" (57:3); "bringing
    low and raising high" (56:3); "Lord of the two risings and Lord of the two settings" (55:17); "what he
    put first and what he put last" (75:13); "what it has done and what it has left undone" (82:5).
18. the openings that point at a person: "Have you seen" and "consider". "Have you seen the man who forbids" (96:9), "have you considered
    the person who denies the Judgement" (107:1), "Do you [Prophet] not see how your Lord dealt with the
    army of the elephant" (105:1), "Have you [Prophet] considered how your Lord dealt with [the people] of
    'Ad" (89:6), "[Prophet], consider that man who turned away" (53:33). the case is opened by pointing at
    a person.
19. self-description of the book. "a noble Quran in a protected Record that only the purified can touch"
    (56:77-79); "a glorious Quran [written] on a preserved Tablet" (85:21-22); "a lesson from which those
    who wish to be taught should learn" (80:11-12); "a Reminder for all peoples" (68:52); "We have made it
    easy to learn lessons" (54:17); "not the words of a poet ... nor the words of a soothsayer" (69:41-42).
    the text says what it is, where it is kept, who it is for and what it is not.
20. brevity. lines of two or three words in the openings of 77, 79, 81, 82, 84, 100. "violently storming
    scattering far and wide separating forcefully delivering a reminder as a proof or a warning" (77:2-6).
    the line is a beat, not a sentence.
21. the reader's own speech as evidence. "All this is as real as your speaking" (51:23); "you differ in
    what you say" (51:8). the argument points at the reader's own mouth.

what a manifesto can take: open on a made thing and swear by it; name a term, ask what it is, define
it; keep a refrain that each claim is checked against; list duties in the order they are done, in the
plural; put every promise beside its cost; address the inner circle as "you" and then turn to everyone;
hand the reader a script in the first person; tell a story in sixteen lines with a wise minority voice;
say "I do not know" where it is true; end short.

## surprises

1. 80:1-10, the frown. the prophet himself is corrected for favouring "the self-satisfied one" over "the
   blind man" who "might have grown in spirit or taken note of something useful to him". this is the
   maker's complaint about gated knowledge stated from the inside: the gatekeeping reflex is in the
   founder, not only in the system. a reader who knows only the famous verses will not know the book
   rebukes its own messenger for this.
2. 52:40 and 68:46, "Do you [Prophet] demand a payment from them that would burden them with debt" and "Do you demand some reward from them that would burden them with debt". the messenger's
   economic model is stated as a rhetorical question: no fee that indebts. it answers the maker's 'systems that want to keep us paying' directly, and it sits beside the demand to "write it down" (52:41, 68:47).
3. 63:7-8. the tactic of the powerful is quoted in their own words: "'Give nothing to those who follow
   God's Messenger, until they abandon him'" and "'the powerful will drive out the weak'". economic siege
   and displacement are named as the tools of the hypocrites, and answered with ownership: "to God belong
   the treasures".
4. 54:27-29 and 91:13-14. the she-camel is a commons: "the water is to be shared between them: each one
   should drink in turn", and the crime that destroys Thamud is hamstringing the animal that shares. the maker's 'harmful defaults' has a story here: a shared resource, a turn rule, and a knife.
5. 68:17-32. the garden owners lose everything not for greed in the abstract but for one whispered plan:
   "'Make sure no poor person enters the garden today!'". and there was a wise minority voice among them
   (68:28). then repair: "'maybe our Lord will give us something better in its place'". a venture ruined
   by exclusion and rebuilt on hope.
6. 57:25. scripture, balance and iron are one shipment: "the Scripture and the Balance, so that people
   could uphold justice: We also sent iron, with its mighty strength and many uses". text, measure, and
   tooling. and the iron's purpose is to "mark out those who would help". the tool reveals the helper.
7. 58:11. the rank of "those who have been given knowledge" is announced in the same breath as "make room
   for one another in your assemblies". knowledge rank comes wrapped in hospitality. a reader who only
   knows the second half misses that the first half is a seating instruction.
8. 73:20. after ordering night vigils, the text relents: "He knows that you will not be able to keep a
   measure of it and He has relented towards all of you, so recite as much of the Quran as is easy for
   you", because some are "sick", some "travelling through the land seeking God's bounty". an obligation
   scaled to what a working person can keep. this is the anti-burnout verse.
9. 65:1. in a divorce law: "for you cannot know what new situation God may perhaps bring about". the
   reason not to drive someone out is that the future is open. a rule for not burning bridges, and for
   reversible actions.
10. 75:16-19. a publishing workflow: "do not rush your tongue ... We shall make sure of its safe
    collection and recitation When We have recited it, repeat the recitation and We shall make it clear".
    collect, recite, repeat, clarify. the tool's 'nothing lands without a keypress' has a cousin here in
    "do not rush your tongue".
11. 89:7-9. Iram "of lofty pillars whose like has never been made in any land" and Thamud "who hewed into
    the rocks". the book admires the craft and records the fall in the same breath. craft is not
    protection; "All of them committed excesses in their lands".
12. 98:4. "those who were given the Scripture became divided only after they were sent [such] clear
    evi-dence". clarity is not a cure for sects; division follows clear evidence. radical clarity has to
    expect this.
13. 85:6-7. the makers of the trench "sat down to watch what they were doing to the believers". the
    spectators are named as the makers. watching is doing.
14. 74:31. the number nineteen is "a test for the disbelievers" and the sick at heart ask "'What could God
    mean by this description?'". the text says out loud that some of its details are tests, and that asking what it could mean, in bad faith, is the failure mode. ambiguity is designed.
15. 56:71-73. "the fire you kindle ... We made it a reminder, and useful to those who kindle it". a tool
    that is both a reminder and a use; the earliest technology described as carrying meaning and function
    at once.
16. 97:3. "The Night of Glory is better than a thousand months". one night outweighs eighty-three years.
    time's value is not linear; the right night matters more than the long stretch.
17. 49:6, 49:12 together. "check it first", "avoid making too many assumptions", "do not spy on one
    another". three epistemic rules for a community: verify, do not assume, do not surveil. the third is
    the one a technology organisation needs to hear.
18. 93:6-11. the ethic of the one who made it out. "Did He not find you an orphan and shelter you Did He
    not find you lost and guide you Did He not find you in need and make you self-sufficient So do not be
    harsh with the orphan and do not chide the one who asks for help talk about the blessings of your
    Lord". the maker wrote that those who make it out "gate keep"; this passage is the counter-instruction
    to the one who made it out: do not chide the asker, and tell what you were given.
19. 48:25. the believers were held back from fighting because "believing men and women whom you would have
    trampled underfoot" lived unknown among the enemy. restraint towards the other side because of unknown
    allies inside it. the "system" the maker rebels against has people inside it worth not trampling.
20. 55:33. "if you can pass beyond the regions of heaven and earth, then do so: you will not pass without
    Our authority". a dare to explore with a boundary; exploration is not forbidden, it is bounded by
    permission.
21. 71:5-9. Noah's log of methods: "night and day", "openly", "in public and speaking to them in private".
    a persistence strategy itemised, followed by "the more I call them, the further they run away". the
    honest report of a campaign that did not work.
22. 72:4-5. the jinn say "Outrageous things have been said about God by the foolish among us although we
    had thought that no man or jinn would [dare to] tell a lie about Him". the naivety that no one would
    lie is named as the error. verification (49:6) exists because someone will.
23. 59:2, 62:7, 78:40. the hand recurs as the seat of what you have done: "destroyed by their own hands",
    "stored up for themselves with their own hands", "what their own hands have sent ahead". the tool's
    keypress is a hand; the book already locates responsibility there.
24. 70:19-21. the default human is "anxious", "fretful when misfortune touches him", "tight-fisted when
    good fortune comes his way", and the list that follows (70:22-34) is the set of practices that counter
    the default. the book has a theory of harmful defaults and a practice list against them.
25. 62:9-11. trade is not condemned; it is paused for the reminder and then resumed: "disperse in the land
    and seek out God's bounty". but the reproach is that people "scatter towards trade or entertainment"
    the moment they see it. the manifesto's loose "business" has a rhythm here: gather, then go earn.
26. 52:44. "Even if they saw a piece of heaven falling down on them, they would say, 'Just a heap of
    clouds,'". the denial of the visible; evidence does not force belief.
27. 51:8. "you differ in what you say". the text notes that the reader's own group is not of one voice.
