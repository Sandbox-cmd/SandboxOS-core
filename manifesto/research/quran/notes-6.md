# notes 6 · surahs 26 to 35 · abdel haleem

reader 6 of 8. source: quran/chunks/chunk-6.txt, read line by line. ten surahs: 26 the poets, 27 the ant, 28 the stories, 29 the spider, 30 the romans, 31 luqman, 32 the prostration, 33 the combined forces (the one medinan surah here), 34 sheba, 35 originator. the chunk is heavy on prophets' stories told to a repeating template (26), on solomon's court and its tests (27, 34), on moses from the basket to qarun (28), and on lists of natural signs addressed to different faculties of knowing (30, 31, 35). surah 33 holds the trust verse the tool already uses, and around it sit verses on pledges, sincerity checked, and direct speech that the maker has not used yet.

all quotations below are from the chunk file, verbatim, with the file's own punctuation (curly quotes, double dashes, and a few hyphenation artefacts such as "cele-brate"). the four outside verses cited (9:105, 61:2, 61:3, 67:2) were fetched with quran/verse.py and are labelled haleem.

## the axiom already in this chunk, and what sits around it

33:72 is one of the tool's ten axioms. the haleem words: "We offered the Trust to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains, yet they refused to undertake it and were afraid of it; mankind undertook it- they have always been inept and foolish". the maker has this verse. what sits around it and is not used yet:

- 33:70 "Believers, be mindful of God, speak in a direct fashion and to good purpose" two verses before the trust, the command is about speech: direct, and aimed at something good. a manifesto about radical clarity can stand on this line.
- 33:71 "and He will put your deeds right for you and forgive you your sins." direct speech is followed by deeds being put right. speech first, then the work repairs.
- 33:7 "We took a solemn pledge from the prophets- from you [Muhammad], from Noah, from Abraham, from Moses, from Jesus, son of Mary" the trust is preceded by a pledge taken from everyone, the carrier included.
- 33:8 "God will question [even] the truthful about their sincerity" the truthful are audited too. nobody is exempt from review.
- 33:15 "a promise to God will be answered for" a pledge is a debt.
- 33:23 "There are men among the believers who honoured their pledge to God: some of them have fulfilled it by death, and some are still waiting. They have not changed in the least" holding the trust is measured by not changing under pressure.
- 33:53 "God does not shrink from the truth." a clause in a verse about house manners: the host is too polite to ask guests to leave, God is not too polite to say so.

so the trust verse is the centre of a cluster about pledges, sincerity checked, direct speech, and truth not shrunk from. the tool's "nothing durable lands without the owner's keypress" is the trust; 33:70 is how to talk about it.

the other nine axioms are outside this chunk. two have close cousins here: 61:2-3 (saying without doing) has 26:226, the poets who "say what they do not do", see section 16; 9:105 (work God sees) has 34:11, "Do good, all of you, for I see everything you do", see section 4.

## 1. knowledge and reading

- 26:28 "Lord of the East and West and everything between them. If you would only use your reason!" moses appeals to pharaoh's reason before he shows the sign; the argument comes before the demonstration, even to a tyrant.
- 26:74 "No, but this is what we saw our fathers doing." abraham asks whether the idols hear, help or harm; the only answer is inheritance. the default is defended with nothing but its own age.
- 26:136-137 "It makes no difference to us whether you warn us or not / for we only do what our forefathers used to do" the people of ad refuse even to weigh the warning; the inherited practice is treated as proof. this is the harmful default speaking in its own voice.
- 26:132 "be mindful of Him who has provided you with everything you know" knowledge is listed among the provisions, before livestock, sons, gardens and springs (26:133-134). what you know is a gift before it is a possession.
- 26:197 "Is it not proof enough for them that the learned men of the Children of Israel have recognized it" the recognition of people who know is offered as evidence; expert witness counts.
- 27:15 "We gave knowledge to David and Solomon, and they both said, ‘Praise be to God, who has favoured us over many of His believing servants.’" knowledge received is met with thanks, not with a claim. compare qarun in 28:78.
- 27:16 "People, we have been taught the speech of birds, and we have been given a share of everything: this is a clearly a great favour." solomon announces his knowledge in public as a favour, not as a rank. "taught the speech of birds" is a strange, playful way to say: given access to channels others cannot read.
- 27:22 "I have learned something you did not know: I come to you from Sheba with firm news" the hoopoe, the smallest member of the king's host, corrects the king's ignorance. knowledge does not respect rank, and a good king listens.
- 27:40 "one of them who had some knowledge of the Scripture said, ‘I will bring it to you in the twinkling of an eye.’" against the "powerful and crafty jinn" of 27:39 who offers force and self-declared trustworthiness, the one with knowledge does the job faster. knowledge beats brute strength.
- 27:52 "their homes are desolate ruins- there truly is a sign in this for those who know" ruins are readable; the ground itself is the archive.
- 27:65 "No one in the heavens or on earth knows the unseen except God." and 27:66 "their knowledge cannot comprehend the Hereafter; they are in doubt about it; they are blind to it" the ceiling on human knowing is stated flat. humility about knowing.
- 27:84 "Did you deny My messages without even taking them in? Or what were you doing?" the charge at the reckoning is rejecting without reading. the manifesto can ask the same of anyone who dismisses a thing unread.
- 28:14 "We gave him wisdom and knowledge: this is how We reward those who do good" wisdom and knowledge come as a reward for doing good, not the other way round. the craft comes first, the understanding follows.
- 28:36 "These are mere conjuring tricks; we never heard this from our forefathers." pharaoh's court rejects the new because it is new. novelty itself is the offence.
- 28:43 "We gave Moses the Scripture to provide insight, guidance, and mercy for people" a book's three jobs: insight, guidance, mercy. a published thing can be judged by those three.
- 28:60 "that which is with God is better and more lasting- will you not use your reason" reason is invoked to weigh the temporary against the lasting.
- 28:78 "This wealth was given to me on account of the knowledge I possess." qarun's justification. the verse then asks "Did he not know that God had destroyed many generations before him". knowledge claimed as title to wealth is the exact posture of the gatekeeper the maker names.
- 28:80 "but those who were given knowledge said, ‘Alas for you! God’s reward is better for those who believe and do good deeds" while the crowd envies qarun's pomp (28:79), those with knowledge see through it. knowledge is what lets you not envy the rich.
- 29:8 "do not obey them if they strive to make you serve, beside Me, anything of which you have no knowledge" even parents cannot bind you to what you have no knowledge of. the inherited default has a limit written into the duty of filial respect. repeated at 31:15.
- 29:20 "Travel throughout the earth and see how He brings life into being" the command is empirical: go and look. the same command is at 27:69, 30:9, 30:42, 34:9, 35:44.
- 29:35 "We left some [of the town] there as a clear sign for those who use their reason" evidence is left in place for the reasoner. the ruin is a dataset.
- 29:38 "Satan made their foul deeds seem alluring to them and barred them from the right way, though they were capable of seeing" the failure is not lack of sight but a barred way while sighted. the mechanism that makes the harmful look attractive is named without the modern word for it.
- 29:43 "Such are the comparisons We draw for people, though only the wise can grasp them" parables are for everyone; understanding them is work.
- 29:48 "you never wrote one down with your hand. If you had done so, those who follow falsehood might have had cause to doubt" and 29:49 "a revelation that is clear to the hearts of those endowed with knowledge" the carrier's clean history is offered as provenance, and the clarity lands on those with knowledge.
- 30:7 "they only know the outer surface of this present life and are heedless of the life to come" surface knowledge is named as a kind of knowing that misses. the maker's "deep context" is the opposite of this.
- 30:8 "Have they not thought about their own selves?" self-reflection as the first inquiry.
- 30:9 "They were mightier than them: they cultivated the earth more and built more upon it." predecessors who built more still fell; more building is not more knowing.
- 30:21-24 four sign-verses end with four audiences: "for those who reflect" (30:21), "for those who know" (30:22), "for those who can hear" (30:23), "for those who use their reason" (30:24). four verbs of knowing in four lines: reflect, know, hear, reason.
- 30:22 "the diversity of your languages and colours. There truly are signs in this for those who know" difference itself is a sign, and those who know are the ones who read it.
- 30:29 "the idolaters follow their own desires without any knowledge" desire without knowledge is the definition of going astray here.
- 30:59 "In this way God seals the hearts of those who do not know" the sealed heart is tied to not knowing.
- 31:6 "the sort of person who pays for distracting tales, intending, without any knowledge, to lead others from God’s way, and to hold it up to ridicule" someone pays for distraction, lacks knowledge, and aims to lead others away and to mock. the closest thing in this chunk to the maker's "generate slop".
- 31:20 "some people argue about God, without knowledge or guidance or an illuminating scripture" three things that make an argument legitimate: knowledge, guidance, a lit text. arguing without any of them is the fault.
- 31:21 "We shall follow what we saw our forefathers following." the third statement of the inheritance default in this chunk, now answered with "What! Even if Satan is calling them".
- 31:27 "If all the trees on earth were pens and all the seas, with seven more seas besides, [were ink,] still God’s words would not run out" the pen and the ink as the measure of the unwritable. knowledge is not finite and no archive holds it all.
- 31:34 "No soul knows what it will reap tomorrow, and no soul knows in what land it will die" the two things nobody knows; the ceiling again.
- 32:9 "He gave you hearing, sight, and minds. How seldom you are grateful" the three instruments of knowing are gifts, and ingratitude is not using them.
- 34:6 "those who have been given knowledge can see that what has been sent to you from your Lord is the truth" knowledge and seeing truth are joined.
- 34:44 "though We have not given them any books to study nor sent any warner before you" the deniers had no books; the lack of study is named.
- 34:46 "I advise you to do one thing only: stand before God, in pairs or singly, and think" the whole advice reduced to one act: think, alone or in pairs. a research method in nine words.
- 35:19-20 "The blind and the seeing are not alike / nor are darkness and light" a flat refusal to pretend that knowing and not knowing are the same.
- 35:28 "It is those of His servants who have knowledge who stand in true awe of God." after two verses of colours in fruit, mountains and people, the ones who know are the ones who are awed. awe is the fruit of knowledge, not its enemy.
- 35:32 "We gave the Scripture as a heritage to Our chosen servants: some of them wronged their own souls, some stayed between [right and wrong], and some, by God’s leave, were foremost in good deeds." the book is inherited by all, and the heirs divide into three by what they do with it. an open inheritance with a fork history.
- 35:40 "Have We given them a book that contains clear evidence? No indeed! The idolaters promise each other only delusion" without a book that holds evidence, all that is left is mutual promising.

## 2. proof and truth

- 26:30-31 "Even if I show you something convincing?" / "‘Show it then,’ said Pharaoh, ‘if you are telling the truth.’" the demand for demonstration is put in the tyrant's mouth and moses meets it. show, do not claim.
- 26:154 "Show us a sign, if you are telling the truth." and 26:187 "Make bits of the heavens fall down on us, if you are telling the truth." the same challenge thrown at salih and shuayb; salih answers with a testable arrangement (the camel's turn, 26:155), shuayb with "My Lord knows best what you do." (26:188). two honest responses to a demand for proof: a test you can run, or a refusal to perform on command.
- 27:27-28 "We shall see whether you are telling the truth or lying / Take this letter of mine and deliver it to them, then withdraw and see what answer they send back." solomon does not accept the hoopoe's report; he designs a verification with an observable result. the chunk's clearest verify-the-report protocol.
- 27:41-42 "Disguise her throne, and we shall see whether or not she recognizes it." ... "‘Is this your throne?’ She replied, ‘It looks like it.’" a second test, this time of the queen's perception; her answer is careful and honest, neither yes nor no.
- 27:49 "We did not witness the destruction of his household. We are telling the truth." the nine corrupters plan this false testimony in advance. the enemy is not only the lie but the pre-arranged alibi.
- 27:64 "Say, ‘Show me your evidence then, if what you say is true.’" after five questions about who made the world, the burden is placed on the claimant.
- 27:75 "there is nothing hidden in the heavens or on earth that is not in a clear Record" everything is logged. repeated at 34:3 "It is all recorded in a clear Record" and 35:11 "except in accordance with a Record".
- 28:28 "whichever of the two terms I fulfil, let there be no injustice to me- God is witness to what we say." a labour agreement closed with a named witness. this is a contract with a witness, the same shape as the debt of 2:282 that another reader will hold.
- 28:49 "Then produce a book from God that gives better guidance than these two and I will follow it, if you are telling the truth." the messenger commits in advance to follow a better book if one is produced. falsifiability, offered by the one making the claim.
- 28:75 "We shall call a witness from every community, and say, ‘Produce your evidence,’ and then they will know that truth belongs to God alone" every community gets a witness and every claim is asked for evidence.
- 29:2-3 "Do people think they will be left alone after saying ‘We believe’ without being put to the test / We tested those who went before them: God will certainly mark out which ones are truthful and which are lying" testing separates the truthful from the liar; belief said aloud is not enough.
- 29:52 "God is sufficient witness between me and you" the witness is invoked when human agreement fails.
- 30:35 "Did We send them down any authority that sanctions the partners they ascribe to God" the claim is checked for its warrant.
- 31:11 "Now, show Me what your other gods have created." the challenge to produce.
- 33:4 "These are only words from your mouths, while God speaks the truth" renaming a wife a mother or an adopted son a son does not make it so. words do not change facts.
- 33:5 "Name your adopted sons after their real fathers: this is more equitable in God’s eyes" correct attribution is called equity. provenance again.
- 33:6 "All this is written in the Scripture" the rule is written down.
- 33:8 "God will question [even] the truthful about their sincerity" the truthful are audited.
- 33:45 "Prophet, We have sent you as a witness, as a bearer of good news and warning" the messenger's first job title is witness.
- 33:70 "speak in a direct fashion and to good purpose" the speech rule in the trust cluster.
- 34:3 "Not even the weight of a speck of dust in the heavens or earth escapes His knowledge, nor anything smaller or greater. It is all recorded in a clear Record" the record has no minimum size.
- 34:47 "If I have asked you for any reward, you can keep it. It is God alone who will reward me: He is witness to everything." no fee, and a witness named.
- 34:49 "The Truth has come; falsehood is powerless." short enough for a wall.
- 34:53 "they denied it all in the past, and threw conjecture from a faroff place" conjecture thrown from far off is the opposite of going to see.
- 35:40 "Have We given them a book that contains clear evidence?" a book is judged by whether it holds evidence.

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship (khalifa), custody

- 26:107 "I am a faithful messenger sent to you" (also 26:125) and "I am a faithful messenger to you" (26:143, 162, 178). every prophet in surah 26 opens with faithfulness as his one credential; the word is the same root as the trust of 33:72.
- 26:193 "the Trustworthy Spirit brought it down" the carrier of the message is named by trustworthiness.
- 27:39 "I am strong and trustworthy enough" the jinn claims the two qualities and is passed over for the one with knowledge (27:40). self-declared trustworthiness is not enough.
- 27:62 "Who makes you successors in the earth?" humans as successors, the stewardship word. repeated 35:39 "it is He who made you [people] successors to the land."
- 28:5-6 "We wished to favour those who were oppressed in that land, to make them leaders, the ones to survive / to establish them in the land" custody passes to the oppressed; the pattern of the whole moses story.
- 28:26 "Father, hire him: a strong, trustworthy man is the best to hire." the shepherd's daughter states the hiring standard: strength and trustworthiness, in that order, and nothing else. the maker's "core team" criterion is already written.
- 28:58 "We are the only heir" wealth reverts; custody is temporary.
- 28:77 "Seek the life to come by means of what God has granted you, but do not neglect your rightful share in this world. Do good to others as God has done good to you." granted, not owned; use it, take your share, pass the good on.
- 29:60 "How many are the creatures who do not store their sustenance! God sustains them and you" a check on hoarding, from the animals.
- 30:41 "Corruption has flourished on land and sea as a result of people’s actions and He will make them taste the consequences of some of their own actions so that they may turn back" bad stewardship has consequences, and the consequences are meant to turn people back. repair is the aim of the warning.
- 33:7 "We took a solemn pledge from the prophets" pledge before trust.
- 33:72 the trust, above.
- 34:13 "Work thankfully, family of David, for few of my servants are truly thankful." the builders are told to work thankfully; the craft is held in trust.
- 35:32 "We gave the Scripture as a heritage to Our chosen servants" the text itself is a trust handed down, and the heirs are graded by what they did with it.

## 4. craft and excellence

- 34:10-11 "We softened iron for him / saying, ‘Make coats of chain mail and measure the links well.’ ‘Do good, all of you, for I see everything you do.’" the fullest craft verse in the chunk. the material is made workable, the instruction is specific (measure the links), and the work is watched. 9:105 haleem (outside the chunk): "Take action! God will see your actions- as will His Messenger and the believers". 34:11 puts the same seeing at the forge.
- 34:12-13 "We made a fountain of molten brass flow for him, and some of the jinn worked under his control with his Lord’s permission" ... "They made him whatever he wanted- palaces, statues, basins as large as water troughs, fixed cauldrons. We said, ‘Work thankfully, family of David" a workshop with materials, workers, and an order to work thankfully. the sandbox as a workshop has its ancestor here.
- 27:88 "this is the handiwork of God who has perfected all things. He is fully aware of what you do" God's work is called handiwork and perfected; your work is watched in the same breath.
- 32:7 "who gave everything its perfect form" perfection of form as the maker's signature. 67:2 haleem (outside): "reveal which of you does best".
- 28:14 "We gave him wisdom and knowledge: this is how We reward those who do good" doing good earns knowledge.
- 28:77 "Do good to others as God has done good to you." the ihsan verse of the chunk: do good in the manner good was done to you.
- 29:7 "We shall reward them according to the best of their actions" judgement by the best work, not the average.
- 29:58 "How excellent is the reward of those who labour" labour is named and praised.
- 29:69 "God is with those who do good" the closing words of the spider surah.
- 31:22 "Whoever directs himself wholly to God and does good work has grasped the surest handhold" good work is the handhold.
- 35:10 "good words rise up to Him and He lifts up the righteous deed" words rise on their own; deeds are lifted. word and deed both counted.
- 26:149 "carving your fine houses from the mountains" thamud's craft, used as the charge against them because they thought it made them secure (26:146 "you will be left secure for ever in what you have here"). craft without humility becomes the accusation.
- 26:128-129 "How can you be so vain that you set up monuments on every high place / Do you build fortresses because you hope to be immortal" the anti-craft: building for vanity and permanence.
- 28:38 "Haman, light me a fire to bake clay bricks, then build me a tall building so that I may climb up to Moses’ God" the anti-craft again: bricks and a tower to prove a lie.
- 33:21 "The Messenger of God is an excellent model" excellence is shown as a model, not described.

## 5. justice and balance

- 26:181-183 "Give full measure: do not sell others short / Weigh with correct scales / do not deprive people of what is theirs. Do not spread corruption on earth" shuayb's four commands: full measure, correct scales, no deprivation, no corruption. fair measure and corruption are one sentence apart.
- 27:34 "Whenever kings go into a city, they ruin it and humiliate its leaders- that is what they do" the queen's plain description of power. rulers ruin; that is what they do.
- 28:4 "Pharaoh made himself high and mighty in the land and divided the people into different groups: one group he oppressed" division of the people into groups is the mechanism of oppression. divide, then oppress.
- 28:28 "let there be no injustice to me- God is witness to what we say." the worker asks for no injustice and names a witness.
- 28:76 "We had given him such treasures that even their keys would have weighed down a whole company of strong men." hoarded wealth measured by the weight of its keys.
- 28:82 "It is God alone who gives what He will, abundantly or sparingly" after qarun is swallowed, the envious learn that wealth is distributed, not earned as title.
- 30:38 "So give their due to the near relative, the needy, and the wayfarer" giving is called a due, not a gift.
- 30:39 "Whatever you lend out in usury to gain value through other people’s wealth will not increase in God’s eyes, but whatever you give in charity, in your desire for God’s approval, will earn multiple rewards" the chunk's usury verse. gain through other people's wealth does not increase; giving multiplies. the maker's "corporations that extract our value" is this in modern words.
- 31:16 "if even the weight of a mustard seed were hidden in a rock or anywhere in the heavens or earth, God would bring it [to light]" nothing is too small to be brought to light.
- 32:18 "is someone who believes equal to someone who defies God? No, they are not equal" the text refuses to flatten difference.
- 34:26 "Our Lord will gather us together, then He will judge justly between us" justice deferred to the just judge when argument fails.
- 34:31-33 "Those who were oppressed will say to the oppressors, ‘If it were not for you, we would have been believers.’" / "‘Was it we who prevented you from following right guidance after it had reached you? No! You yourselves were sinners.’" / "‘No, it was your scheming, night and day" a three-turn exchange between the oppressed and the oppressors, and neither is let off: the oppressors schemed, the oppressed still chose.
- 34:34 "Never have We sent a warner to a community without those among them who were corrupted by wealth saying, ‘We do not believe in the message you have been sent with.’" every warner meets the wealthy's refusal. the manifesto should expect it.
- 34:37 "Neither wealth nor children will bring you nearer to Us" wealth is not a measure of worth.
- 34:39 "He will replace whatever you give in alms; He is the best of providers." giving is not loss.
- 35:18 "No burdened soul will bear the burden of another" the balance of accounts is per soul.

## 6. agency and self-change

- 27:40 "if anyone is grateful, it is for his own good, if anyone is ungrateful, then my Lord is self-sufficient and most generous" gratitude benefits the one who gives it.
- 27:44 "My Lord, I have wronged myself: I devote myself, with Solomon, to God" the queen, seeing the glass floor, revises her whole belief in one line. the model of changing one's mind on evidence.
- 27:90 "Are you rewarded for anything except what you have done?" the plainest statement of earned outcome.
- 27:92 "Whoever chooses to follow the right path does so for his own good." choice and benefit belong to the same person.
- 28:15-17 "This must be Satan’s work" ... "Lord, I have wronged myself. Forgive me" ... "I shall never support those who do evil." moses kills a man, owns it in three verses, and turns the mistake into a vow. this is what self-correction looks like.
- 28:19 "You clearly want to be a tyrant in the land; you do not intend to put things right." the man moses helped yesterday reads his violence as tyranny. the reformer who strikes is not read as a reformer.
- 28:47 "as a result of what they have done with their own hands" consequences are of one's own hands.
- 28:55 "We have our deeds and you have yours. Peace be with you! We do not seek the company of ignorant people." a peaceful separation with responsibility kept on each side.
- 29:6 "Those who exert themselves do so for their own benefit––God does not need His creatures––" striving is for the striver. God gains nothing from your effort; the gain is yours.
- 29:12-13 "‘Follow our path and we shall bear [the consequences for] your sins,’ yet they will not do so––they are liars / They will bear their own burdens and others besides" anyone who offers to carry your consequences for you is lying, and will end up carrying more than they meant.
- 29:40 "It was not God who wronged them; they wronged themselves" repeated at 30:9. the chunk insists on self-inflicted harm.
- 29:69 "But We shall be sure to guide to Our ways those who strive hard for Our cause: God is with those who do good" the spider surah ends with striving; guidance follows effort, not the reverse.
- 30:36 "when something bad happens to them- because of their own actions- they fall into utter despair" despair is the wrong response to self-caused harm.
- 30:41 "so that they may turn back" consequences are pedagogic; the point is the turning.
- 30:44 "those who did good deeds will have made good provision for themselves" the good deed is a provision laid down for oneself.
- 31:12 "whoever gives thanks benefits his own soul" the same reflexive logic for thanks.
- 32:12 "Our Lord, now that we have seen and heard, send us back and we shall do good." and 26:102 "If only we could live our lives again, we would be true believers!" the regret of those who wanted a second run. the second chance is what the living have now.
- 33:5 [sahih, via verse.py, because the haleem line carries a word the house forbids] "Call them by [the names of] their fathers; it is more just in the sight of Allah. But if you do not know their fathers - then they are [still] your brothers in religion and those entrusted to you. And there is no blame upon you for that in which you have erred but [only for] what your hearts intended. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful" mistakes are not blamed; intent is.
- 34:25 "You will not be ques-tioned about our sins, nor will we be questioned about what you do." accounts are separate.
- 34:50 "If I go astray, that is my loss, and if I am rightly guided, it is through what my Lord has revealed to me." the messenger owns his own error.
- 35:18 "whoever purifies himself does so for his own benefit" the reflexive again.
- 35:37 "Did We not give you a life long enough to take warning if you were going to?" the answer to "let us out": you had the time.
- 35:43 "the plotting of evil only rebounds on those who plot" harm comes back to its source.

## 7. time and mortality

- 26:203 "Can we have more time?" the one question that comes too late.
- 26:205-207 "Think, if we let them enjoy this life for some years / and then the promised torment came upon them / what good would their past enjoyment be to them" years of ease weighed against their end.
- 27:72 "Maybe some of what you seek to hasten is near at hand." nearness, understated.
- 28:88 "Everything will perish except His Face." the shortest mortality line in the chunk.
- 29:5 "God’s appointed time is bound to come" fixed, and unknown.
- 29:14 "He lived among them for fifty years short of a thousand but when the Flood overwhelmed them they were still doing evil" noah's span, and still no success. persistence is not guaranteed a result.
- 29:53 "it will come to them suddenly and catch them unawares" the hour arrives unannounced.
- 29:57 "Every soul will taste death, then it is to Us that you will be returned" said between the vast earth (29:56 "My earth is vast") and the reward of labour (29:58). mortality sits between roominess and work.
- 29:64 "The life of this world is merely an amusement and a diversion; the true life is in the Hereafter" the play word: this life is amusement.
- 30:54 "It is God who creates you weak, then gives you strength, then weakness after strength, together with your grey hair" the arc of a life in one verse.
- 30:55 "the guilty will swear they lingered no more than an hour" time felt short once it is gone.
- 31:29 "He has subjected the sun and the moon, each to run its course for a stated term" everything runs on a term, even the sun.
- 31:34 "No soul knows what it will reap tomorrow, and no soul knows in what land it will die" the two unknowns: tomorrow's yield and the place of death. the maker's "the exact amount unknown only to God" is this verse.
- 32:5 "on a Day that will measure a thousand years in your reckoning" time is not uniform.
- 33:63 "People ask you about the Hour. Say, ‘God alone has knowledge of it.’ How could you [Prophet] know? The Hour may well be near" even the messenger does not know, and it may be near.
- 34:14 "nothing showed the jinn he was dead, but a creature of the earth eating at his stick: when he fell down they realized- if they had known what was hidden they would not have continued their demeaning labour" solomon dies leaning on his stick; the workers keep working for a dead king until a worm eats through the stick and he falls. the most useful time image in the chunk.
- 34:30 "You have an appointment for a Day which you cannot put off nor bring forward, even by a single moment." an appointment, fixed to the moment.
- 35:11 "no person grows old or has his life cut short, except in accordance with a Record" the span is recorded.
- 35:37 "Did We not give you a life long enough to take warning if you were going to?" the span was enough.
- 35:45 "He gives them respite for a stated time and, whenever their time comes, God has been watching His servants" respite is the name for the time we have.

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- 26:128-130 "How can you be so vain that you set up monuments on every high place / Do you build fortresses because you hope to be immortal / Why do you act like tyrants whenever you attack someone" three symptoms of decay: vanity monuments, fortresses against death, tyranny in conflict.
- 26:151-152 "do not obey those who are given to excess / and who spread corruption in the land instead of doing what is right." excess and corruption are set against doing right; the corrupt are the ones not to obey.
- 27:4 "We have made their deeds seem alluring to them, so they wander blindly" deeds made alluring is the chunk's phrase for the mechanism of corruption; repeated 27:24, 29:38, 35:8.
- 27:14 "They denied them, in their wickedness and their pride, even though their souls acknowledged them as true. See how those who spread corruption met their end" denial against one's own inner knowledge is pride, and it is called corruption.
- 27:48 "There were nine men in the city who spread corruption in the land without making amends" corruption is a small group, not a mass, and its mark is not making amends.
- 28:39 "Pharaoh and his armies behaved arrogantly in the land with no right- they thought they would not be brought back to Us" arrogance rests on thinking there is no accounting.
- 28:58 "We have destroyed many a community that once revelled in its wanton wealth and easy living" wanton wealth and easy living are the pre-conditions of collapse.
- 28:76-83 qarun: "Do not gloat" (28:76), "This wealth was given to me on account of the knowledge I possess." (28:78), "He went out among his people in all his pomp" (28:79), "We caused the earth to swallow him and his home" (28:81). the hoarder's arc in eight verses.
- 28:83 "We grant the Home in the Hereafter to those who do not seek superiority on earth or spread corruption" seeking superiority and spreading corruption are paired as the two disqualifiers.
- 29:36 "serve God and think ahead to the Last Day. Do not commit evil and spread corruption in the land." thinking ahead is set against corruption.
- 29:39 "[Remember] Qarun and Pharaoh and Haman: Moses brought them clear signs, but they behaved arrogantly on earth." the three named figures of corruption: the hoarder, the tyrant, the minister.
- 29:41 "Those who take protectors other than God can be compared to spiders building themselves houses- the spider’s is the frailest of all houses- if only they could understand" the spider's house: a structure that looks like a house and is not one. the maker's "defaults we found ourselves into" are spider houses.
- 30:9 "they cultivated the earth more and built more upon it" more cultivation and more building did not save them.
- 30:41 "Corruption has flourished on land and sea as a result of people’s actions" fasad is caused by hands, and the remedy named is turning back.
- 31:6 paying for distracting tales, see section 1.
- 31:18 "Do not turn your nose up at people, nor walk about the place arrogantly, for God does not love arrogant or boastful people" arrogance at the scale of a walk.
- 33:19 "when fear has passed, they attack you with sharp tongues and begrudge you any good" cowardice in danger, sharp tongues in safety.
- 33:67 "Lord! We obeyed our masters and our chiefs, and they led us astray" obedience to chiefs is the excuse that fails.
- 34:16 "We let loose on them a flood from the dam and replaced their two gardens with others that yielded bitter fruit, tamarisk bushes, and a few lote trees" the infrastructure fails and the gardens turn bitter. decay of a built system through ingratitude.
- 34:19 "Our Lord has made the distance between our staging posts so long!" then "They wronged themselves" given towns within sight of each other (34:18), they complained about distance. ingratitude for ease is a form of decay.
- 34:34 corrupted by wealth, see section 5.
- 35:8 "What about those whose evil deeds are made alluring to them so that they think they are good?" the corrupt who think themselves good; the false reformer without the word.
- 35:43 "became more arrogant in the land, and intensified their plotting of evil- the plotting of evil only rebounds on those who plot" arrogance escalates and rebounds.

## 9. community and mutual aid

- 26:111 "Why should we believe you when the worst sort of people follow you?" and 26:114-115 "I will not drive believers away / I am here only to give people a clear warning." noah refuses to exclude the "worst sort of people" from his following. the door stays open.
- 26:215 "lower your wing tenderly over the believers who follow you" a bird's wing over the followers.
- 27:18 "Ants! Go into your homes, in case Solomon and his hosts unwittingly crush you." one ant warns the whole colony. the smallest member protects the community by speaking.
- 27:32-33 "Counsellors, give me your counsel in the matter I now face: I only ever decide on matters in your presence." ... "but you are in command, so consider what orders to give us." the queen consults and the counsellors defer; both sides of a working council. this is the chunk's consultation scene, without the word.
- 28:12 "Shall I tell you about a household which could bring him up for you and take good care of him?" moses' sister offers help to the enemy household; the offer restores the child to his mother. an offer made across a line.
- 28:24-25 "He watered their flocks for them, withdrew into the shade, and prayed" ... "he wants to reward you for watering our flocks for us" unasked help, then withdrawal, then a reward offered by the helped. the sequence is the whole ethic of the small good deed.
- 28:54 "repel evil with good, give to others out of what We have provided for them" giving from what one was given.
- 29:46 "argue only in the best way with the People of the Book" ... "our God and your God are one [and the same]" argument across difference, in the best way, from a shared centre.
- 30:21 "He ordained love and kindness between you" love and kindness are ordained, not incidental.
- 30:22 "the diversity of your languages and colours" diversity as sign; 35:28 adds "various colours among human beings, wild animals, and livestock too".
- 30:38 "give their due to the near relative, the needy, and the wayfarer" the three claimants.
- 32:16 "they give to others some of what We have given them" giving as a mark of the believer.
- 33:5 "if you do not know who their fathers are [they are your] ‘brothers-in-religion’ and proteges" a name for those who join without blood ties. the collective the maker describes, people who use sandbox "knowingly or maybe it was shared with them", are proteges.
- 33:35 "believing men and women, obedient men and women, truthful men and women, steadfast men and women, humble men and women, charitable men and women" ten paired qualities, each said for both sexes.
- 33:58 "those who undeservedly insult believing men and women will bear the guilt of slander and flagrant sin" the community is protected from slander.
- 34:18 "other towns within sight of one another to which they could travel easily" and "Travel safely in this land by night and by day" towns within sight of one another: a network of reachable places, built for safe travel. the maker's "blueprints that anyone may adopt" could be towns within sight.

## 10. humility, moderation and warmth

- 26:3 "[Prophet], are you going to worry yourself to death because they will not believe" the opening of the chunk is a check on over-worry. do the work, do not die of the outcome.
- 26:12-13 "I fear they will call me a liar / and I will feel stressed and tongue-tied, so send Aaron too" moses admits fear, stress and a stammer, and asks for a partner. weakness is stated, and the response is a team.

- 27:19 "Solomon smiled broadly at her words and said, ‘Lord, inspire me to be thankful for the blessings You have granted me" the king smiles at an ant and turns to gratitude. power that can be delighted by a small thing.
- 27:70 "do not grieve over them; do not be distressed by their schemes" a check on distress about the opposition.
- 28:24 "My Lord, I am in dire need of whatever good thing You may send me" moses, having just done a favour, asks openly for help. need is not hidden.
- 28:27 "I do not intend to make things difficult for you: God willing, you will find I am a fair man." the employer's promise: not to make things difficult. a kind contract.
- 28:55 "Peace be with you! We do not seek the company of ignorant people." the parting is peace, not insult.
- 28:77 "do not neglect your rightful share in this world" moderation is not deprivation; take your share.
- 30:49 "though before it is sent they may have lost all hope" rain comes after hope is lost; hardship, then ease.
- 30:60 "so be patient, for God’s promise is true: do not let those with no firm beliefs discourage you" patience against discouragement from the uncommitted.
- 31:18-19 "Do not turn your nose up at people, nor walk about the place arrogantly" ... "Go at a moderate pace and lower your voice, for the ugliest of all voices is the braying of asses." luqman's last words to his son are about gait and voice. moderation in the body.
- 31:23 "do not let their refusal sadden you [Prophet]" consolation to the messenger.
- 32:15 "bow down in worship, cele-brate their Lord’s praises, and do not think themselves above this" not thinking oneself above bowing.
- 33:28 "release you with kindness" and 33:49 "release them in an honourable way" even parting is to be kind and honourable.
- 33:32 "speak in an appropriate manner" speech neither seductive nor harsh.
- 33:53 "he would shrink from asking you to leave. God does not shrink from the truth." the messenger's shyness is respected, and the truth is spoken for him.
- 35:8 "[Prophet], do not waste your soul away with regret for them" the fourth consolation in the chunk (26:3, 27:70, 31:23, 35:8): do not spend yourself on those who refuse.
- 35:15 "People, it is you who stand in need of God- God needs nothing and is worthy of all praise" need is the human condition, said plainly.

## 13. freedom and no compulsion

- 26:49 "How dare you believe in him before I have given you permission?" pharaoh claims the right to license belief. the gatekeeper's voice.
- 26:216 "If they disobey you, say, ‘I bear no responsibility for your actions.’" the messenger is not accountable for others' choices.
- 27:80-81 "You cannot make the dead hear, you cannot make the deaf listen to your call when they turn their backs and leave" the limits of persuasion, repeated at 30:52-53.

- 28:27 "on condition that you serve me for eight years: if you complete ten, it will be of your own free will." the extra two years are voluntary, said in the contract. free will written into an agreement.
- 28:56 "You [Prophet] cannot guide everyone you love to the truth" even love does not give the right to force.
- 29:8 "do not obey them if they strive to make you serve, beside Me, anything of which you have no knowledge" and 31:15 "then do not obey them. Yet keep their company in this life according to what is right" refuse compulsion, keep the relationship.

- 32:13 "If it had been Our will, We could certainly have given every soul its true guidance" compulsion withheld by design.
- 34:21 "even though he had no authority over them" satan had no authority; they followed anyway. following is a choice even when the caller has no power.
- 34:28 "We have sent you [Prophet] only to bring good news and warning to all people" only, again.
- 34:46 "stand before God, in pairs or singly, and think" the invitation is to think, not to obey.

- a counter-note the manifesto should know: 33:36 "it is not fitting for any believing man or woman to claim freedom of choice in that matter" this verse limits choice on a matter God and the messenger have decided. ## how the text itself speaks

- the eightfold refrain of surah 26: "There truly is a sign in this, though most of them do not believe / your Lord alone is the Almighty, the Merciful" closes each story (26:8-9, 67-68, 103-104, 121-122, 139-140, 158-159, 174-175, 190-191). the refrain does two things at once: it marks the sign and it admits that most will not believe. a manifesto can have a refrain that admits its own odds.
- the prophetic template: "Will you not be mindful of God / I am a faithful messenger sent to you / be mindful of God and obey me / I ask no reward of you, for my only reward is with the Lord of the Worlds" said five times with five names (26:106-109, 124-127, 142-145, 161-164, 177-180). identical opening, then each prophet's specific charge: hud on monuments, salih on excess, lot on lust, shuayb on scales. the form says: same stance, local diagnosis.
- the objectors' template: "You are nothing but a man like us" (26:154, 26:186); "if you are telling the truth" (26:31, 154, 187; 27:64; 28:49; 32:28; 34:29). the text gives the opposition a recurring voice and answers it every time.
- "lo and behold!" as a hinge of surprise (26:32, 33, 45; 30:20, 33). the text allows itself an exclamation at a turn.
- the fivefold question of 27:60-64: "Who created the heavens and earth? ... Is it another god beside God?" five times, then "Show me your evidence then". question stacked on question, then the burden shifted.
- the sign-list of 30:20-25: "One of His signs ... Another of His signs ... Among His signs", each closing with a different faculty: reflect, know, hear, reason. a numbered list of evidence addressed to different readers.
- the "Say" series: 34:24-27 (four in a row), 34:46-50 (five in a row). the messenger is given exact words. a manifesto can have lines meant to be said aloud.
- antithesis in short lines: 35:19-22, four verses, four pairs.
- the paired thought experiment: 28:71-72, perpetual night then perpetual day, each ending in a question ("Do you not listen?", "Do you not see").
- direct address by role: "[Prophet]" throughout; "People!" (31:33, 35:3, 35:5, 35:15); "My believing servants!" (29:56); "Believers" (33:41, 33:69, 33:70); "Wives of the Prophet" (33:30, 33:32); "Ants!" (27:18); "You mountains, echo God’s praises together with him, and you birds, too." (34:10). even ants, mountains and birds are addressed in the second person.
- the father's counsel as a list: 31:13-19, luqman to his son: do not attribute partners, keep up the prayer, command what is right, forbid what is wrong, bear steadfastly, do not turn up your nose, go at a moderate pace, lower your voice. it ends in the body, not in doctrine.
- the paired list of 33:35: ten qualities, each said for "men and women".
- courtroom dialogue: the oppressed and the oppressors (34:31-33), the ringleaders disowning followers (28:63), the followers blaming chiefs (33:67), the gods bickering (26:96-102). the text stages the reckoning as speech.
- the oath: 34:3 "Yes, by my Lord, [it will], by Him who knows the unseen!" and the false oath of 35:42.
- the imperative to travel and look: "Travel through the earth and see" (27:69), "Travel throughout the earth and see" (29:20), "Have they not travelled through the land" (30:9, 35:44), "Travel through the land, and see" (30:42). an empirical refrain.
- consolation to the speaker as a recurring aside: 26:3, 27:70, 31:23, 35:8. the text talks to its own carrier about not burning out.
- promise paired with warning in one verse or pair: 27:89-90, 28:84, 32:19-20, 35:7. the pairing is the text's default cadence.
- the opening letters: "Ta Sin Mim" (26:1, 28:1), "Ta Sin" (27:1), "Alif Lam Mim" (29:1, 30:1, 31:1, 32:1). a mark before the text.
- the maker speaking of making in the first person plural: "We softened iron for him" (34:10), "We made a fountain of molten brass flow for him" (34:12).
- "only" as a limiter on the messenger's job, five times: 26:115, 27:92, 29:18, 34:28, 35:23.

## surprises

1. the hiring verse. 28:26 "Father, hire him: a strong, trustworthy man is the best to hire." said by a young woman about a fugitive who just did unpaid work at a well. the criterion has two words and no mention of credentials, network or funding. then 28:27-28: a term of eight years, an optional two more "of your own free will", the employer's promise "I do not intend to make things difficult for you", the worker's condition "let there be no injustice to me", and "God is witness to what we say". a complete fair-work agreement in three verses, with a voluntary extension clause and a witness. the maker's "opens opportunities for others to do the same" can stand on this.

2. verification as court procedure. 27:20-28: the king notices an absence ("Why do I not see the hoopoe?"), demands "a convincing excuse", receives an unsolicited report ("I have learned something you did not know"), and does not act on it. he says "We shall see whether you are telling the truth or lying" and sends a letter with instructions to "withdraw and see what answer they send back". the report is trusted enough to act on, not enough to believe. then 27:41: "Disguise her throne, and we shall see whether or not she recognizes it." two experiments in one story. the tool's approve verb has an ancestor in a king who checks a bird's report.

3. knowledge beats force, and the winner calls it a test of gratitude. 27:39-40: the "powerful and crafty jinn" offers strength and self-vouched trust; "one of them who had some knowledge of the Scripture" does it faster. solomon's response is not pride but "This is a favour from my Lord, to test whether I am grateful or not". the gated technical class of the maker's letter is the jinn; the answer is knowledge held with thanks.

4. the queen's council. 27:32-35: "I only ever decide on matters in your presence." her counsellors defer: "you are in command, so consider what orders to give us." she distrusts kings on principle: "Whenever kings go into a city, they ruin it and humiliate its leaders- that is what they do", and she tests rather than fights: "I am going to send them a gift, then see what answer my envoys bring back." a ruler who consults, distrusts power, and runs an experiment before committing. she is the only non-prophet in the chunk who changes her mind on evidence (27:44 "My Lord, I have wronged myself").

5. workers labouring for a dead king. 34:14: solomon dies standing, and "nothing showed the jinn he was dead, but a creature of the earth eating at his stick: when he fell down they realized- if they had known what was hidden they would not have continued their demeaning labour". the workers kept working because the authority appeared to stand. the maker's "systems that want to keep us paying for them" is this: labour continued for a power that is already dead, until something small eats through the stick.

6. funded distraction. 31:6: "the sort of person who pays for distracting tales, intending, without any knowledge, to lead others from God’s way, and to hold it up to ridicule". money, distraction, lack of knowledge, intent to mislead, ridicule. all five marks of the slop economy in one verse.

7. the price of sorcery. 26:41-42: "‘Shall we be rewarded if we win?’ / and he said, ‘Yes, and you will join my inner court.’" the technical experts of pharaoh's court ask for payment and are paid in access to power. set against the five prophets who say "I ask no reward of you" (26:109, 127, 145, 164, 180). the chunk stages the exact contrast between gated expertise and free knowledge.

8. permission to believe. 26:49: "How dare you believe in him before I have given you permission?" the tyrant assumes belief is licensed. the sorcerers, having seen the evidence, answer "That will do us no harm" (26:50). the strongest anti-gatekeeping moment in the chunk is a tyrant's outrage that people believed without asking him.

9. the reformer read as tyrant. 28:19: "Moses, are you going to kill me as you killed that person yesterday? You clearly want to be a tyrant in the land; you do not intend to put things right." said by the man moses helped the day before. moses's own vow after the killing was "I shall never support those who do evil" (28:17), and the next day his help looks like tyranny to its beneficiary. the maker's "rebellion is constructive, never cruel" has its cautionary scene.

10. the spider verse is followed by a verse about who can read it. 29:41 gives the parable; 29:43 says "Such are the comparisons We draw for people, though only the wise can grasp them". the parable is public, the reading is work. then 29:45 "prayer restrains outrageous and unacceptable behaviour" and 29:46 "argue only in the best way". the spider's frailty, the wisdom to see it, restraint, and good argument sit within six verses.

11. no one can withhold what God opens. 35:2: "No one can withhold the blessing God opens up for people, nor can anyone but Him release whatever He withholds". a plain statement that gates are not final. eight verses later, 35:10: "If anyone desires power, all power belongs to God; good words rise up to Him and He lifts up the righteous deed".

12. the trust is surrounded by speech and pledge. 33:70 "speak in a direct fashion and to good purpose" is two verses before 33:72. the maker uses the trust verse; the direct-speech verse next to it is unused.

13. the ark is for all people. 29:15: "We made this a sign for all people". not for one nation. an open blueprint.

14. the camel's schedule. 26:155: "She should have her turn to drink and so should you, each on a specified day". a shared well governed by turns, and the collapse comes from breaking the turn (26:157). a commons with a schedule.

15. noah does not vet his followers. 26:111-114: told that "the worst sort of people follow you", he answers "What knowledge do I have of what they used to do" and "I will not drive believers away". the collective the maker describes, where people use the tools without knowing what sandbox is, has this permission.

16. towns within sight, and the complaint about them. 34:18-19: infrastructure described as "other towns within sight of one another to which they could travel easily", with the instruction "Travel safely in this land by night and by day". the network was built; the people complained "Our Lord has made the distance between our staging posts so long!" ease taken for granted is named as wronging oneself.

17. the oppressed are still answerable. 34:31-33: the oppressed blame the oppressors; the oppressors say "You yourselves were sinners"; the oppressed answer "it was your scheming, night and day". the text does not resolve it in favour of either. a manifesto for the "stuck" cannot make them blameless.

18. wisdom given to a non-prophet and passed to a child. 31:12-19: 31:12 "We endowed Luqman with wisdom" and he spends it on his son, ending not in theology but in gait and voice. wisdom arrives as parenting, and its last word is "lower your voice".

19. stand in pairs or singly and think. 34:46: "I advise you to do one thing only: stand before God, in pairs or singly, and think". the whole method reduced to one line.

20. the text on its own provenance. 29:48: "you never wrote one down with your hand. If you had done so, those who follow falsehood might have had cause to doubt". a claim of authenticity based on the carrier's history. a chain of custody without the word for it.

21. leaders are raised after steadfastness, and from the oppressed. 32:24 "When they became steadfast and believed firmly in Our messages, We raised leaders among them" and 28:5 "We wished to favour those who were oppressed in that land, to make them leaders". leadership is a consequence, not a starting position.

## coverage

first verse read: 26:1 "Ta Sin Mim". last verse read: 35:45 "If God were to punish people [at once] for the wrong they have done, there would not be a single creature left on the surface of the earth." 773 verses in ten surahs (26: 227, 27: 93, 28: 88, 29: 69, 30: 60, 31: 34, 32: 30, 33: 73, 34: 54, 35: 45), 804 lines in the file, every line read.

stretches where i found little for the manifesto: 26:160-175, 27:54-58 and 29:28-34 (lot's people, told three times, on sexual conduct and the town's destruction; only 29:35 "a clear sign for those who use their reason" carried over); 33:28-34 and 33:50-55 (the prophet's household, marriage rules, screens; only 33:53 "God does not shrink from the truth" and 33:32 "speak in an appropriate manner" carried over); 33:59-62 (garments, and the fate of the city's liars); 32:10-14, 34:40-42 and 34:51-54 (scenes of the last day with no working content beyond the regret already noted at 32:12). that is an honest finding: about 60 of the 773 verses gave nothing to the sixteen headings.
