# notes 2 of 8: surahs 4 to 6 (an-nisa, al-ma'idah, al-an'am)

reader 2. chunk file: `quran/chunks/chunk-2.txt`, abdel haleem translation. every quote below is copied from that file unless it is labelled with another translation and a verse.py pull. references are `S:V`. where a quote is cut short inside a sentence, the cut is marked `[...]`.

one of the ten axioms lives in this chunk: 4:135 (justice against yourselves). the other nine are elsewhere. what sits around 4:135 and has not been used yet: 4:58 (return what was entrusted, judge justly), 5:8 (hatred must not bend justice), 6:152 (full measure, and just speech even about a relative), and the whole witness protocol in 5:106-108. these four are the operational neighbours of the axiom; they say how justice against yourself is actually done.

a note on the three surahs. surah 4 is medinan law: orphans, inheritance, trust, testimony, hypocrites. surah 5 is medinan too: obligations, food, the covenant, judging by what was sent down, the witness protocol, the table. surah 6 is meccan and almost entirely argument: signs, proof, "say", "think", against following conjecture and forefathers. so the chunk moves from custody (4) through covenant (5) to epistemology (6). that order is itself useful to a manifesto: first what you hold, then what you owe, then how you know.

## 1. knowledge and reading

- `4:82` "Will they not think about this Quran? If it had been from anyone other than God, they would have found much inconsistency in it" · the text asks to be tested for inconsistency; it offers coherence under scrutiny as its evidence, a research posture rather than a devotional one.
- `4:83` "Whenever news of any matter comes to them, whether concerning peace or war, they spread it about; if they referred it to the Messenger and those in authority among them, those seeking its meaning would have found it out from them" · a rule against forwarding unverified news; refer it first to those who can extract what it means. this is the chunk's clearest verse on misinformation.
- `4:113` "God has sent down the Scripture and Wisdom to you, and taught you what you did not know" · knowledge is something received and then held, not owned by birth.
- `4:162` "But those of them who are well grounded in knowledge and have faith do believe in what has been revealed to you" · being grounded in knowledge is the named mark of the good among the people of the book; knowledge and faith travel together, not against each other.
- `5:15` "Our Messenger has come to make clear to you much of what you have kept hidden of the Scripture" · the messenger's job is described as un-hiding; disclosure of concealed knowledge is a divine act.
- `5:44` "So [rabbis and scholars] do not fear people, fear Me; do not barter away My messages for a small price" · scholars entrusted with preserving knowledge are told two things: do not fear the crowd, and do not sell what you hold cheap.
- `5:82` "for there are among them people devoted to learning and ascetics. These people are not given to arrogance" · learning and the absence of arrogance are placed in one profile; the praised learned are the unarrogant ones.
- `5:100` "Bad cannot be likened to good, though you may be dazzled by how abundant the bad is. Be mindful of God, people of understanding" · abundance is not quality, and the verse is addressed to people of understanding; a direct answer to slop.
- `5:103` "the disbelievers invent lies about God. Most of them do not use reason" · invention and unreason are paired.
- `5:104` "‘What we inherited from our forefathers is good enough for us,’ even though their forefathers knew nothing and were not guided" · the inherited default is refused on the ground that the ancestors themselves had no knowledge; inheritance is not a source.
- `6:50` "Say, ‘Is a blind person like one who can see? Why will you not reflect?’" · seeing and reflecting are the same demand, put as a question.
- `6:76` "When the night grew dark over him he saw a star and said, ‘This is my Lord,’ but when it set, he said, ‘I do not like things that set.’" · abraham reaches his belief by observation and elimination, star then moon (6:77) then sun (6:78), rejecting each because it sets. this is the model of deriving conviction rather than receiving it, which is how the maker describes his own faith.
- `6:79` "I have turned my face as a true believer towards Him who created the heavens and the earth. I am not one of the polytheists.’" · the conclusion of the method: what does not set.
- `6:83` "Such was the argument We gave to Abraham against his people- We raise in rank whoever We will" · rank is tied to the argument one is given and can make.
- `6:91` "which you made into separate sheets, showing some but hiding many? You were taught things that neither you nor your forefathers had known" · two things at once: the curated release (knowledge cut into sheets, some shown, many hidden) and the fact that knowledge can be truly new, unknown to any forefather.
- `6:97` "It is He who made the stars, so that they can guide you when land and sea are dark: We have made the signs clear for those who have knowledge" · signs are clear to those who know; knowledge is the instrument for reading them.
- `6:105` "This is how We explain Our revelations in various ways- though they will say, ‘You [Muhammad] have been studying’- to make them clear for those who know" · "You [Muhammad] have been studying" is recorded as the mockers' accusation; the text does not treat study as a shame.
- `6:116` "If you obeyed most of those on earth, they would lead you away from the path of God. They follow nothing but speculation; they are merely guessing" · the majority is not evidence; most opinion is guessing. a verse against consensus as proof, and against the algorithmic average.
- `6:119` "But many lead others astray by their desires, without any true knowledge" · leading without knowledge is the named fault.
- `6:143` "Tell me based on knowledge if you are telling the truth" and `6:148` "Say, ‘Have you any knowledge that you can show us? You follow only supposition and tell only lies.’" · the test of a claim is showable knowledge.
- `6:151` "This is what He commands you to do: perhaps you will use your reason" · the commandments close on reason, not on obedience alone.
- humility about knowing. `5:109` the messengers themselves say on the last day "We do not have that knowledge: You alone know things that cannot be seen." `5:116` jesus: "You know all that is within me, though I do not know what is within You". `6:50` "I do not have the treasures of God, nor do I know the unseen, nor do I tell you that I am an angel. I only follow what is revealed to me." `4:11` "You cannot know which of your parents or your children is more beneficial to you". `6:59` "He has the keys to the unseen: no one knows them but Him." · the prophets disclaim superpowers in their own voice; a manifesto can adopt the same disclaimer.

## 2. proof and truth

- `4:6` "When you give them their property, call witnesses in; but God takes full account of everything you do" · handover of custody needs human witnesses even though God already keeps the account; the divine record does not excuse the human one.
- `4:135` "You who believe, uphold justice and bear witness to God, even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or your close relatives. Whether the person is rich or poor, God can best take care of both." · the axiom. note the second half: "Refrain from following your own desire, so that you can act justly- if you distort or neglect justice, God is fully aware of what you do". distortion and neglect are named as the two ways justice fails.
- `4:157` "those that disagreed about him are full of doubt, with no knowledge to follow, only supposition" · disagreement without knowledge is supposition; the word appears again at 6:116 and 6:148.
- `4:174` "People, convincing proof has come to you from your Lord and We have sent a clear light down to you" · proof and light in one line, addressed to people at large.
- `5:8` "You who believe, be steadfast in your devotion to God and bear witness impartially: do not let hatred of others lead you away from justice, but adhere to justice, for that is closer to awareness of God." · impartial witness is a form of devotion; hatred is named as the pressure that bends testimony.
- `5:106` "‘We will not sell our testimony for any price, even if a close relative is involved. We will not hide God’s testimony, for then we should be doing wrong.’" · the oath two witnesses swear over a bequest, after prayer, when there is doubt.
- `5:107` "If it is discovered that these two are guilty [of perjury], two of those whose rights have been usurped have a better right to bear witness in their place." · witnesses can be replaced when found lying, and the replacements come from the side that was harmed.
- `5:108` "that will make it more likely they will give true and proper testimony, or fear that their oaths might be refuted by others afterwards" · the design rationale is written into the law: the possibility of later refutation keeps testimony honest. this is a review process with an audit trail and the reason for it stated in plain words. it extends 2:282 (haleem, pulled with verse.py): "Do not disdain to write the debt down, be it small or large, along with the time it falls due: this way is more equitable in God’s eyes, more reliable as testimony, and more likely to prevent doubts arising between you." both passages justify the procedure by the doubt it prevents.
- `5:113` "They said, ‘We wish to eat from it; to have our hearts reassured; to know that you have told us the truth; and to be witnesses of it.’" · the disciples ask for evidence and are given it; wanting to verify is not treated as unbelief.
- `5:115` "anyone who disbelieves after this will be punished with a punishment that I will not inflict on anyone else in the world" · once the evidence has been seen, the bar rises; proof carries responsibility.
- `5:119` "This is a Day when the truthful will benefit from their truthfulness." · truthfulness pays out in the end, stated as a settled account.
- `6:19` "Say, ‘What counts most as a witness?’ Say, ‘God is witness between you and me. This Quran was revealed for me to warn you [people] and everyone it reaches." · the question of what counts as a witness is asked outright.
- `6:57` "Say, ‘I stand on clear proof from my Lord, though you deny it. What you seek to hasten is not within my power." · standing on proof while admitting the limits of one's power in the same breath.
- `6:93` "or says, ‘I too can reveal something equal to God’s revelation’" · the false claim to produce equivalent revelation is listed among the worst wrongs; fabricated content presented as the real thing.
- `6:104` "Now clear proof has come to you from your Lord: if anyone sees it, that will be to his advantage; if anyone is blind to it, that will be to his loss" · proof is offered; the gain or loss from it is the reader's own.
- `6:115` "The word of your Lord is complete in its truth and justice. No one can change His words" · truth and justice are one property of the word; an unalterable record.
- `6:149` "Say, ‘The conclusive argument belongs to God alone." and `6:150` "Say, ‘Bring your witnesses to testify that God has forbidden all this.’ If they do testify, do not bear witness with them." · demand witnesses; and if false witnesses are produced, refuse to co-sign.
- `6:130` "They will say, ‘We testify against ourselves.’" · the final testimony is self-testimony.
- claims against what is done. `4:81` "They say, ‘We obey you,’ but as soon as they leave your presence, some of them scheme by night to do other than what you said. God records what they scheme". `4:62` after the disaster they caused: "‘We only wanted to do good and achieve harmony.’" `5:41` "those who say with their mouths, ‘We believe,’ but have no faith in their hearts". `5:61` "they say, ‘We believe,’ but they come disbelieving and leave disbelieving". · the gap between the said and the done is the hypocrite's whole definition in surah 4 and 5.
- distortion. `5:13` "They distort the meaning of [revealed] words and have forgotten some of what they were told to remember". `4:46` "twisting it abusively with their tongues so as to disparage religion". · altered narrative and selective memory named as one failing.
- `4:112` "anyone who commits an offence or a sin, and then throws the blame on to some innocent person, has burdened himself with deceit as well as flagrant sin" · blame-shifting is counted as a second sin on top of the first.
- limits of proof. `6:7` "Even if We had sent down to you [Prophet] a book inscribed on parchment, and they had touched it with their own hands, the disbelievers would still say, ‘This is nothing but blatant sorcery.’" `6:111` "Even if We sent the angels down to them, and the dead spoke to them, and We gathered all things right in front of them, they still would not believe". · evidence does not compel belief; a warning against expecting proof alone to convert anyone.

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship (khalifa), custody, returning what was entrusted

- `4:58` "God commands you [people] to return things entrusted to you to their rightful owners, and, if you judge between people, to do so with justice: God’s instructions to you are excellent, for He hears and sees everything" · the amana verse in its plainest form, and it is not among the ten axioms. the axioms use 33:72 (haleem, verse.py): "We offered the Trust to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains, yet they refused to undertake it and were afraid of it; mankind undertook it- they have always been inept and foolish". 33:72 says what the trust is; 4:58 says what to do with it: give it back to whom it belongs, and judge justly in between. the two together are the origin and the operation.
- `4:5` "Do not entrust your property to the feeble-minded. God has made it a means of support for you: make provision for them from it, clothe them, and address them kindly" · custody is matched to capacity, and the one who cannot yet hold it is still provided for and spoken to kindly.
- `4:6` "Test orphans until they reach marriageable age; then, if you find they have sound judgement, hand over their property to them. Do not consume it hastily before they come of age" · custody is temporary; it ends with a test of judgement and a witnessed handover. the guardian who is well off abstains; the poor one takes "only what is fair".
- `4:10` "Those who consume the property of orphans unjustly are actually swallowing fire into their own bellies" · consuming what is held in trust is self-harm in the image.
- `4:105` "Do not be an advocate for those who betray trust" and `4:107` "Do not argue for those who betray their own souls: God does not love anyone given to treachery and sin" · do not lend your voice to the treacherous.
- `5:1` "You who believe, fulfil your obligations." · the surah opens on this and nothing else.
- `5:7` "Remember God’s blessing on you and the pledge with which you were bound when you said, ‘We hear and we obey.’" · a pledge is remembered as a spoken commitment.
- `5:44` "So did the rabbis and the scholars in accordance with that part of God’s Scripture which they were entrusted to preserve, and to which they were witnesses." · knowledge itself is a trust with keepers and witnesses; preservation is custodial work.
- `5:89` "This is the atonement for breaking your oaths- keep your oaths." · breaking an oath has a cost paid to the poor, ten people fed or clothed.
- `6:89` "Even if these people now disbelieve in them, We have entrusted them to others who do not disbelieve" · when the custodians fail, the trust moves to others who will hold it. custody can be forked to those who keep faith with it.
- `6:152` "keep any promises you make in God’s name" · placed in the same breath as full measure and just speech.
- `6:165` "It is He who made you successors on the earth and raises some of you above others in rank, to test you through what He gives you." · the khalifa idea in this chunk: successors on the earth, and unequal rank is a test of the one who holds more, not a right.
- `6:61` "He sends out recorders to watch over you until, when death overtakes any of you, those sent by Us take his soul- they never fail in their duty" · the recorders never fail in their duty; the standard for a keeper of records.

## 4. craft and excellence

- `4:58` "God’s instructions to you are excellent, for He hears and sees everything" · the instruction itself is called excellent; quality is claimed for the rule before it is asked of the ruled.
- `4:86` "when you [believers] are offered a greeting, respond with a better one, or at least return it: God keeps account of everything" · the floor is return in kind; the standard is to do better. a rule for every reply.
- `4:125` "Who could be better in religion than those who direct themselves wholly to God, do good, and follow the religion of Abraham, who was true in faith?" · wholeness of direction plus doing good is the definition of better.
- `5:4` "what you have taught your birds and beasts of prey to catch, teaching them as God has taught you" · training a hawk is put in parallel with God's teaching of humans; skill passed down is a chain, and the chain reaches into the workshop.
- `5:48` "so race to do good" · competition is redirected into good works.
- `5:93` "as long as they are mindful of God, believe and do good deeds, then are mindful of God and believe, then are mindful of God and do good deeds: God loves those who do good deeds" · the triple repetition in one verse; the same phrase "God loves those who do good" closes 5:13 too.
- `5:110` "how, by My leave, you fashioned the shape of a bird out of clay, breathed into it, and it became, by My leave, a bird" · the one act of making by a prophet's hands in this chunk, and "by My leave" frames it twice; craft under permission.
- `6:96` "He made the sun and the moon to a precise measure. That is the design of the Almighty, the All Knowing" · precise measure and design are the maker's own attributes here.
- `6:99` "alike yet different. Watch their fruits as they grow and ripen!" · the only exclamation of wonder in the chunk, and it is about watching a thing ripen.
- `6:132` "Everyone is assigned a rank according to their deeds; your Lord is not unaware of anything they do" · rank follows deed, and every deed is seen.
- `6:154` "perfecting [Our favour] for those who do good, explaining everything clearly, as guidance and mercy" · perfecting and explaining clearly are one act.
- `6:160` "Whoever has done a good deed will have it ten times to his credit, but whoever has done a bad deed will be repaid only with its equivalent" · the ledger is asymmetric in favour of good; ten to one.
- `4:95` "Those believers who stay at home, apart from those with an incapacity, are not equal to those who commit themselves and their possessions to striving in God’s way" · commitment of self and possessions ranks above staying home, with an honest exemption for incapacity.

## 5. justice and balance

- `4:29` "You who believe, do not wrongfully consume each other’s wealth but trade by mutual consent." · consent is the line between trade and consumption.
- `4:32` "Do not covet what God has given to some of you more than others- men have the portion they have earned; and women the portion they have earned- you should rather ask God for some of His bounty" · earned portion, for both sexes, and the answer to envy is to ask for more from the source, not to take.
- `4:37` "who are miserly and order other people to be the same, hiding the bounty God has given them" · hoarding and concealment are one vice; the miser also hides.
- `4:40` "He does not wrong anyone by as much as the weight of a speck of dust: He doubles any good deed and gives a tremendous reward of His own" · the scale of justice is a speck of dust; the scale of reward is doubling.
- `4:49` "no one will be wronged by as much as the husk of a date stone"; `4:53` "they would not give away so much as the groove of a date stone"; `4:77` "you will not be wronged by as much as the fibre in a date stone"; `4:124` "will not be wronged by as much as the dip in a date stone" · surah 4 measures wrong in the smallest units it can name, four parts of one date stone. a manifesto can use that: justice is measured in fibres.
- `4:161` "for taking usury when they had been forbidden to do so; and for wrongfully devouring other people’s property" · usury and devouring property are listed together as the reason good things were withdrawn.
- `5:42` "if you do judge between them, judge justly: God loves the just" · one may decline to judge, but if one judges, only justly.
- `5:45` "if anyone forgoes this out of charity, it will serve as atonement for his bad deeds" · the right to equal retaliation is real, and waiving it is praised; justice allows mercy without cancelling the right.
- `5:64` "Truly, God’s hands are open wide: He gives as He pleases." · the open hand is the counter-image to the tight fist earlier in the verse.
- `6:152` "give full measure and weight, according to justice’- We do not burden any soul with more than it can bear- ‘ when you speak, be just, even if it concerns a relative" · full measure and just speech in one verse; and the aside that no soul is burdened beyond what it can bear sits between them.
- `6:115` "The word of your Lord is complete in its truth and justice." · truth and justice are one completeness.
- `4:12` "[In all cases, the distribution comes] after payment of any bequests or debts" (three times in one verse) and "with no harm done to anyone" · debts are settled before heirs are paid, said three times; nobody is harmed in the split.
- `4:7` "whether the legacy be small or large: this is ordained by God" · a share is a share regardless of size.
- `6:141` "So when they bear fruit, eat some of it, paying what is due on the day of harvest, but do not be wasteful: God does not like wasteful people" · pay what is due at harvest, then enjoy, and do not waste; three rules of a fair yield.

## 6. agency and self-change

- `4:79` "Anything good that happens to you [Prophet] is from God; anything bad is [...] from yourself." · (the omitted word is a bracketed translator's insert.) the direction of blame is inward, the direction of credit is outward; a posture that keeps agency without pride.
- `4:84` "You are accountable only for yourself. Urge the believers on." · own responsibility and encouragement of others, in two sentences.
- `4:85` "Whoever speaks for a good cause will share in its benefits and whoever speaks for a bad cause will share in its burden" · advocacy has a share in the outcome; speaking is an act with consequences you hold.
- `4:97` "They reply, ‘We were oppressed in this land,’ and the angels say, ‘But was God’s earth not spacious enough for you to migrate to some other place?’" · the excuse of being stuck is not accepted from those who could have moved. `4:98` exempts "the truly helpless men, women, and children who have no means in their power nor any way to leave". the maker's line about defaults that make us feel stuck meets its answer here: the earth is spacious, and only real helplessness excuses staying.
- `4:99` "Anyone who migrates for God’s cause will find many a refuge and great plenty in the earth" · moving is rewarded with refuge and plenty.
- `4:110` "Yet anyone who does evil or wrongs his own soul and then asks God for forgiveness will find Him most forgiving and merciful" · the door to self-change stays open.
- `5:23` "Yet two men whom God had blessed among those who were afraid said, ‘Go in to them through the gate and when you go in you will overcome them." against `5:24` "so you and your Lord go in and fight, and we will stay here.’" · the many stay put and outsource the work; two act. the story of israel refusing the land is the story of a people who would not take the first step.
- `5:25` "He said, ‘Lord, I have authority over no one except myself and my brother" · moses names the true size of his power: himself and one other.
- `5:35` "seek ways to come closer to Him and strive for His cause, so that you may prosper" · seeking ways and striving are the route to prospering.
- `5:54` "who strive in God’s way without fearing anyone’s reproach" · striving without fear of reproach is the described character of those God loves.
- `5:105` "You who believe, you are responsible for your own souls; if anyone else goes astray it will not harm you so long as you follow the guidance" · your own soul is your responsibility; another's straying does not damage you.
- `6:14` "Say, ‘I am commanded to be the first [of you] to devote myself [to Him].’" and `6:163` "This is what I am commanded, and I am the first to devote myself to Him.’" · the messenger leads by going first; nobody is asked to do what he has not done.
- `6:52` "You are in no way accountable for them, nor they for you" · mutual non-accountability between the believer and the one who chooses otherwise.
- `6:123` "but they scheme only against themselves, without realizing it" · schemes rebound on the schemer.
- `6:135` "‘My people, you carry on as you are, and so will I: you will come to realize who will have a happy homecoming in the Hereafter.’" · agency stated as parallel paths, not as a fight.
- `6:164` "Each soul is responsible for its own actions; no soul will bear the burden of another. You will all return to your Lord in the end, and He will tell you the truth about your differences" · the chunk's last statement of the principle, and it closes on differences being explained rather than erased.

## 7. time and mortality

- `4:18` "It is not true repentance when people continue to do evil until death confronts them and then say, ‘Now I repent,’" · change deferred to the deadline is not change.
- `4:77` "If only You would give us just a little more time.’ Say to them, ‘Little is the enjoyment in this world" · the plea for a little more time is recorded and answered.
- `4:78` "Death will overtake you no matter where you may be, even inside high towers.’" · the tower does not protect; the image is architectural.
- `4:133` "If He so willed, He could remove you altogether and replace you with new people: He has full power to do so" · replaceability is stated flatly; the same at 6:133.
- `6:2` "He is the one who created you from clay and specified a term [for you] and another fixed time, known only to Him; yet still you doubt" · the maker's own words, "our time on this earth is limited and the exact amount unknown only to God", are almost this verse.
- `6:31` "when the Hour suddenly arrives, they say, ‘Alas for us that we disregarded this!’ They will bear their burdens on their backs." · the hour comes suddenly, and regret is carried as a load.
- `6:32` "The life of this world is nothing but a game and a distraction; the Home in the Hereafter is best for those who are aware of God." · a caution for a workshop that calls itself playful: play is fine, play as the whole of life is the warning.
- `6:44` "Then, as they revelled in what they had been given, We struck them suddenly and they were dumbfounded" · the gates opened first, then the blow; abundance precedes the fall.
- `6:60` "It is He who calls your souls back by night, knowing what you have done by day, then raises you up again in the daytime until your fixed term is fulfilled." · each night is a small death and each morning a return; the working day is bounded on both ends and audited in between.
- `6:67` "Every prophecy has its fixed time to be fulfilled: you will come to realize this.’" · things have their time; hastening is not in the messenger's power (6:57, 6:58).
- `6:128` "but now we have reached the appointed time You decreed for us." · the appointed time ends even the profitable partnership of the wicked.
- `6:158` "Say, ‘Wait if you wish: we too are waiting.’" · patience without anxiety, said in the first person plural.
- `6:6` "Do they not realize how many generations We destroyed before them? We established them in the earth more firmly than you, sent down abundant rain on them from the sky" · firm establishment did not save the earlier generations.
- `5:26` "God said, ‘The land is forbidden to them for forty years: they will wander the earth aimlessly." · the price of refusing to act was forty years of aimless movement.

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- `4:36` "God does not like arrogant, boastful people" and `4:37` "who are miserly and order other people to be the same, hiding the bounty God has given them" · arrogance, boasting, hoarding and concealing form one chain.
- `4:119` "I will command them to tamper with God’s creation.’" and `4:120` "he makes them promises and raises false hopes, but Satan’s promises are nothing but delusion" · the adversary's two tools are tampering and false hope.
- `4:142` "When they stand up to pray, they do so sluggishly, showing off in front of people, and remember God only a little" · performance for an audience is the hypocrite's worship.
- `4:155` "for saying ‘Our minds are closed’- No! God has sealed them in their disbelief" · the closed mind is self-declared before it is sealed.
- `4:160` "for having frequently debarred others from God’s path" and `4:167` "Those who have disbelieved and barred others from God’s path have gone far astray" · barring others from the path is a counted offence, distinct from straying oneself. gatekeeping is its own sin.
- `5:32` "Our messengers came to them with clear signs, but many of them continued to commit excesses in the land" · excess in the land, after clear signs, is the shape of decay.
- `5:63` "Why do their rabbis and scholars not forbid them to speak sinfully and consume what is unlawful? How evil their deeds are" and `5:79` "they did not forbid each other to do wrong. How vile their deeds were" · decay is dated from the moment the learned stop objecting and people stop correcting one another.
- `5:64` "Whenever they kindle the fire of war, God will put it out. They try to spread corruption in the land, but God does not love those who corrupt" · corruption is spread; the verb is active.
- `5:91` "With intoxicants and gambling, Satan seeks only to incite enmity and hatred among you, and to stop you remembering God and prayer." · the mechanism of vice is named: enmity between people and distraction from what matters. it reads well against attention systems.
- `6:43` "But no, their hearts became hard and Satan made their foul deeds alluring to them" and `6:44` "So, when they had forgotten the warning they had received, We opened the gates to everything for them." · hardness, allure, forgetting, then open gates; a sequence.
- `6:70` "those who take their religion for a mere game and distraction and are deceived by the life of this world" · again game and distraction, this time as what religion becomes when it decays.
- `6:112` "They suggest alluring words to one another in order to deceive" · the corrupt exchange alluring words among themselves.
- `6:123` "And so We have put chief evildoers in every city to perpetrate their schemes there- but they scheme only against themselves, without realizing it" · every city has its chief schemers, and the scheme is self-directed.
- `6:129` "In this way, We make some evildoers have power over others through their misdeeds" · the rigged system runs on the wrongdoing of those inside it; power over others is gained through misdeeds.

"People," opens 4:1 and 4:174. "People of the Book" opens 4:47, 4:171, 5:15, 5:19, 5:59, 5:68, 5:77. the second person plural is the default register; a manifesto addressed to "you who" rather than about "they who" borrows this.
- the "say" script. surah 6 is built from "Say," commands (6:11 to 6:19, 6:37, 6:40, 6:46, 6:47, 6:50, 6:56 to 6:58, 6:63, 6:64, 6:71, 6:90, 6:91, 6:109, 6:114, 6:135, 6:143 to 6:151, 6:158, 6:161 to 6:164). the messenger is handed lines to say in the first person; the text models its own replies to objections. a manifesto can contain its own "say" section: here is what we answer when asked.
- questions to the reader. "Will they not think about this Quran?" (4:82). "What is the matter with these people that they can barely understand what they are told" (4:78). "Will you not give them up" (5:91). "Do they not realize" (6:6). "Why will you [people] not understand" (6:32). "Why will you not reflect?" (6:50). "How can you not take heed" (6:80). "so how can you turn away from the truth" (6:95). "Think:" opens 6:40, 6:46, 6:47. the question is the primary tool of surah 6.
- refrains and triples. "after payment of any bequests or debts" three times in 4:12. "See how We explain Our revelations in various ways" at 6:46, 6:65, 6:105. "This is what He commands you to do, so that you may" closes 6:151, 6:152, 6:153 with three different endings: "use your reason", "take heed", "refrain from wrongdoing". "Those who do not judge according to what God has revealed are" at 5:44, 5:45, 5:47 with escalation: "rejecting [God’s teachings]", "doing grave wrong", "lawbreakers". "leave them" at 6:91, 6:112, 6:137. the closing formulae "God is all knowing, all wise" and "God is most forgiving and merciful" end dozens of verses; each rule lands on a name of God.
- promise paired with warning, in adjacent verses: 4:13 gardens then 4:14 fire; 4:56 fire then 4:57 gardens; 5:9 reward then 5:10 fire; 6:48 no fear then 6:49 torment; 6:160 ten to one. the pairing is structural, not occasional.
- oaths. "By your Lord" (4:65). the sworn witness formulae of 5:106 and 5:107. "They swear by God with their most solemn oaths" (6:109), reported as unreliable.
- numbered and listed duties. 4:23 the forbidden marriages as one long list. 4:36 the list of those to be good to. 5:3 the forbidden foods. 6:151 to 6:153 the commandments as a numbered decalogue-like run. 5:89 the atonement as a graded list: feed ten, or clothe, or free, or fast three days.
- parables and stories placed inside argument: adam's sons (5:27 to 5:31), moses at the gate (5:20 to 5:26), abraham and the setting lights (6:74 to 6:79), the man in the ravine (6:71), the dead man given light (6:122).
- sarcasm in the aside: "so they claim!" at 6:136 and 6:138. "how badly they judge" (6:136). "How evil their practices are" (5:62). "How vile their deeds were" (5:79). short verdicts after description.
- self-inclusion. "we too are waiting" (6:158). "you carry on as you are, and so will I" (6:135). "I am the first to devote myself to Him" (6:163). the speaker puts himself under the same rule.
- design rationale stated inside the rule: 5:108 "that will make it more likely they will give true and proper testimony"; 4:176 "so that you do not make mistakes"; 5:6 "God does not wish to place any burden on you". the law explains why, in the same sentence.
- escalation by repetition of a word: "far, far astray" (4:116, 4:136, 4:167). "then are mindful of God and believe, then are mindful of God and do good deeds" (5:93).
- the "[Prophet], have you not considered" opener (4:44, 4:49, 4:51, 4:60, 4:77) turns the reader into an observer of a case.

## name and mark candidates

- burhan (proof): `4:174` "convincing proof has come to you from your Lord"; `6:57` "I stand on clear proof from my Lord"; `6:104` "Now clear proof has come to you from your Lord". three uses, all with "clear" or "convincing".
- nur (light): `4:174` "a clear light"; `5:15` "A light has now come to you from God, and a Scripture making things clear"; `5:16` "bringing them from darkness out into light"; `5:44` "the Torah with guidance and light"; `5:46` "the Gospel with guidance, light"; `6:1` "made darkness and light"; `6:91` "a light and a guide to people"; `6:122` "given light with which to walk among people". the last is the strongest for a mark: light one walks among people with.
- sabil (way, path): `6:153` "this is My path, leading straight, so follow it, and do not follow other ways"; `5:16` "guides to the ways of peace"; `5:48` "We have assigned a law and a path to each of you"; `4:160` "debarred others from God’s path". "a law and a path to each" is a plural of paths.
- hikma (wisdom): `4:54` "the Scripture and wisdom"; `4:113` "the Scripture and Wisdom"; `5:110` "the Scripture and wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel"; `6:89` "the Scripture, wisdom, and prophethood". wisdom always paired with the book.
- mizan (measure, balance): `6:152` "give full measure and weight, according to justice"; `6:96` "the sun and the moon to a precise measure"; `6:91` "They have no grasp of God’s true measure". measure as both the trader's scale and the cosmic setting.
- amana (trust): `4:58` "return things entrusted to you to their rightful owners"; `5:44` "entrusted to preserve"; `6:89` "We have entrusted them to others who do not disbelieve".
- witness, testimony, chain: `4:41` "a witness from each community"; `4:135` "bear witness to God, even if it is against yourselves"; `5:8` "bear witness impartially"; `5:83` "count us amongst the witnesses"; `5:106` to `5:108` the protocol; `5:117` "I was a witness over them during my time among them"; `6:19` "What counts most as a witness?"; `6:150` "Bring your witnesses". "What counts most as a witness?" could name a whole practice.
- record: `4:81` "God records what they scheme"; `6:38` "We have missed nothing out of the Record"; `6:59` "written in a clear Record"; `6:61` "He sends out recorders to watch over you". the clear record and the recorders who never fail.
- bayan (making clear): `5:15` "a Scripture making things clear"; `5:19` "to make things clear for you"; `6:114` "the Scripture, clearly explained"; `6:154` "explaining everything clearly"; `6:55`, `6:105`, `6:126` "We have explained". the arabic root is not visible in translation but the idea is dense in this chunk.
- furqan (criterion): no direct use. the nearest are `5:100` "Bad cannot be likened to good" and `4:82` "they would have found much inconsistency in it", both criterion-shaped tests.
- fitra (original disposition): no direct use. the inverse appears at `4:119` "I will command them to tamper with God’s creation", and abraham at `6:79` turns his face toward "Him who created the heavens and the earth" as "a true believer", the hanif; `6:161` "the faith of Abraham, a man of pure faith".
- time: `6:2` "a term [for you] and another fixed time, known only to Him"; `6:60` "until your fixed term is fulfilled"; `6:67` "Every prophecy has its fixed time". no hourglass image; the term is fixed and unseen.
- keys: `6:59` "He has the keys to the unseen: no one knows them but Him." a strong single image not in the brief's list.
- "the first": `6:14` and `6:163` "the first to devote myself". "first" as a mark of going ahead.
- "alike yet different": `6:99`, `6:141`. a phrase for a family of tools or forks.
- "single soul": `4:1` and `6:98` "produced you from a single soul".
- "race to do good": `5:48`. a phrase for the posture toward difference.
- "Go in to them through the gate": `5:23`. a phrase for the first step.
- qalam (pen) and iqra (read): not in this chunk. the closest material is `6:7` "a book inscribed on parchment" and `6:91` "separate sheets".

## surprises

- `6:138` the quran's own picture of gatekeeping: "‘These cattle and crops are reserved, and only those we allow may eat them’- so they claim!" the pagans invented reservations, dressed them as divine rule, and the text mocks it in an aside. this is the closest scriptural line to the maker's question about why only one company gets the powerful tools. it also warns that gatekeepers always claim their gate is sacred.
- `6:90` "I ask no reward for it from you: it is a lesson for all people." the prophetic teaching is released free, to all, by its bearer. open release has a first-person scriptural voice.
- `6:91` "which you made into separate sheets, showing some but hiding many" and "You were taught things that neither you nor your forefathers had known." the curated narrative is named exactly, and in the same verse knowledge is said to be truly new; the forefathers had not known it. against inheriting and against hiding, in one line.
- `5:100` "dazzled by how abundant the bad is." the verse on slop, addressed to "people of understanding".
- `6:116` "If you obeyed most of those on earth, they would lead you away from the path of God. They follow nothing but speculation; they are merely guessing" the majority as guessers; a verse against the average and the trend.
- `4:83` the news-forwarding verse. spreading a report before referring it to those who can extract its meaning is called out as near to following satan (the verse ends "you would almost all have followed Satan"). the chunk has a verse on misinformation hygiene that few know.
- `5:106` to `5:108` the witness protocol with its design rationale. two witnesses, sworn after prayer when there is doubt, replaceable if perjured by two from the harmed side, and the reason stated: "fear that their oaths might be refuted by others afterwards". a review process that explains itself. together with 4:6 (call witnesses when handing over custody) and 2:282 (write the debt, witnesses, "more likely to prevent doubts arising between you"), the scripture has a full theory of the auditable handover.
- `4:97` "was God’s earth not spacious enough for you to migrate to some other place?" the feeling of being stuck is answered not with comfort but with the width of the earth; and 4:98 keeps the exemption honest for those who truly cannot move.
- `6:2` "specified a term [for you] and another fixed time, known only to Him" the maker wrote "the exact amount unknown only to God" without, I think, knowing he was paraphrasing this verse.
- `6:60` the nightly return. "It is He who calls your souls back by night, knowing what you have done by day, then raises you up again in the daytime until your fixed term is fulfilled." a rhythm for a workshop: each day closed, reviewed, reopened.
- `4:82` "they would have found much inconsistency in it" the text invites a consistency audit of itself. a manifesto could do the same: invite readers to find where it contradicts itself.
- `6:35` the tunnel and the ladder. the text tells its own messenger not to dig into the earth or climb into the sky to win over those who refuse. the temptation to over-persuade is named as a temptation.
- `5:48` "We have assigned a law and a path to each of you. If God had so willed, He would have made you one community" and "so race to do good" plurality of paths is designed, and the response is a race in good, not a merger. the maker's collective of people who may not even know sandbox exists fits under this.
- `6:129` "We make some evildoers have power over others through their misdeeds" the system that works against you is powered by the wrongdoing of those inside it; it is not neutral machinery.
- `5:63` and `5:79` the silence of scholars and the failure of mutual correction are named as the point where a community rots: "they did not forbid each other to do wrong." decay is not the wrongdoers alone; it is everyone who stopped objecting.
- `4:119` "tamper with God’s creation." the adversary's programme includes tampering; a verse for the altered narrative and the altered image.
- `4:63` "speak to them about themselves using penetrating words" the prescribed reply to hypocrisy is not denunciation but words that make people see themselves.
- `5:82` "people devoted to learning and ascetics. These people are not given to arrogance" the praised are learned and unarrogant at once; the text does not separate the two.
- `4:86` "respond with a better one, or at least return it" a rule for every reply and every pull request: at minimum match, aim to improve.
- `6:99` "Watch their fruits as they grow and ripen!" the only exclamation of wonder in 461 verses, and it is about patience and observation.
- `5:2` mutual aid is commanded in the same verse as restraint toward those who barred you. "help one another to do what is right and good" is not separable from "Do not let your hatred for the people who barred you from the Sacred Mosque induce you to break the law". the famous half and the forgotten half belong together.
- `5:101` "do not ask about matters which, if made known to you, might make things difficult for you" a scriptural argument for scope discipline: not every question serves.
- `6:138` to `6:140` and `4:160`, `4:167`: barring others from the path is a counted sin distinct from straying oneself. the text has a separate charge for gatekeeping.
