# notes 5 · surahs 18 to 25 (al-kahf, maryam, taha, al-anbiya, al-hajj, al-mu'minun, an-nur, al-furqan)

reader 5 of 8. abdel haleem translation, quoted verbatim from chunk-5.txt. where a second translation is cited it is labelled and came from verse.py.

this chunk is unusually rich for a maker's manifesto. it holds three of the quran's long worked stories about building and repairing (khidr's boat and wall, dhul-qarnayn's iron barrier, noah's ark built to spec), the two big "light" texts (the lamp in the niche and the layered darkness), the scales with the mustard seed, david taught to make armour, the whole household code of asking permission before entering (surah 24), and the closing portrait of the servants who walk gently and keep a just balance (25:63-76). it also holds the surah literally named the criterion.

---

## 1. knowledge and reading

- 18:5 "They have no knowledge about this, nor did their forefathers––it is a monstrous assertion that comes out of their mouths" · an inherited assertion is not knowledge; passing a claim down a line of forefathers adds nothing to its truth.
- 18:22 "guessing in the dark ... Only a few have real knowledge about them, so do not argue, but stick to what is clear, and do not ask any of these people about them" · when the numbers cannot be known, do not argue about the numbers. say what is clear and stop. this is radical clarity joined to humility about knowing.
- 18:26 "Say [Prophet], ‘God knows best how long they stayed.’ His is the knowledge of all that is hidden in the heavens and earth" · a formula for the edge of knowledge, spoken out loud rather than papered over.
- 18:65-66 "a man to whom We had granted Our mercy and whom We had given knowledge of Our own" / "May I follow you so that you can teach me some of the right guidance you have been taught?" · moses, a prophet with scripture, asks to be an apprentice. the rank of the learner is not lowered by asking to follow.
- 18:68 "How could you be patient in matters beyond your knowledge?" · patience is the price of learning past what you already know; the impatient questioner cannot be taught.
- 18:70 "If you follow me then, do not query anything I do before I mention it to you myself." · an apprenticeship rule: watch the whole process before judging one step of it.
- 18:109 "If the whole ocean were ink for writing the words of my Lord, it would run dry before those words were exhausted" · knowledge is unbounded and ink is finite; a research house never finishes.
- 19:43 "Father, knowledge that has not reached you has come to me, so follow me: I will guide you to an even path" · knowledge can run from son to father. inheritance is not the only direction; the young may teach the old.
- 20:52 "My Lord alone has knowledge of them, all in a record; my Lord does not err or forget" · the ideal of a record that does not err or forget, against human memory that does.
- 20:114 "[Prophet], do not rush to recite before the revelation is fully complete but say, ‘Lord, increase me in knowledge!’" · do not publish before the reading is finished; ask for more knowledge instead. a research posture in one line.
- 21:7 "if you [disbelievers] do not know, ask people who know the Scripture" · when you do not know, ask those who do; the text sends its own reader to other people's expertise.
- 21:10 "And now We have sent down to you [people] a Scripture to remind you. Will you not use your reason" · scripture and reason in one breath, not opposed.
- 21:53-54 "They replied, ‘We found our fathers worshipping them.’ He said, ‘You and your fathers have clearly gone astray.’" · the plain answer to the argument from forefathers: then you and your fathers are both wrong.
- 21:79 "and made Solomon understand the case [better], though We gave sound judgement and knowledge to both of them" · even among the knowing, understanding differs by degree; there is always one who understands the case better.
- 22:8 "some who, with no knowledge or guidance or any book of enlightenment, argue about God" · arguing without knowledge, guidance or a book is the failure mode named three ways.
- 22:5 "some are left to live on to such an age that they forget all they once knew" · knowledge decays in a person; this is the plainest case for writing things down.
- 22:46 "Have these people [of Mecca] not travelled through the land with hearts to understand and ears to hear? It is not people’s eyes that are blind, but their hearts within their breasts" · travel and look, but the organ of understanding is the heart, and it can go blind while the eyes work.
- 22:54 "He causes those given knowledge to realize that this Revelation is your Lord’s Truth, so that they may believe in it and humble their hearts" · those given knowledge are the ones who recognise truth, and their response is humility, not pride.
- 23:24 "we never heard of anything like this from our forefathers" · the objection every new thing meets, put in the mouths of noah's opponents.
- 23:68 "Have they not contemplated the Word of God? Has something come to them that did not come to their forefathers" · a neat inversion: the message is not new, the forefathers had it too; contemplation is what is missing.
- 23:78 / 23:80 "It is God who endowed you with hearing, sight, and hearts- how seldom you are grateful" / "will you not use your minds" · the senses and the mind are gifts and the gratitude owed for them is to use them.
- 24:15 "When you took it up with your tongues, and spoke with your mouths things you did not know [to be true], you thought it was trivial but to God it was very serious" · repeating what you do not know is not trivial; it is the sin named in the rumour episode.
- 25:32-33 "We sent it in this way to strengthen your heart [Prophet]; We gave it to you in gradual revelation" / "They cannot put any argument to you without Our bringing you the truth and the best explanation" · gradual release is a method, not a weakness, and every argument deserves the best explanation rather than a dismissal.
- 25:73 "who, when reminded of their Lord’s signs, do not turn a deaf ear and a blind eye to them" · attention itself is a virtue in the closing portrait of the servants.

## 2. proof and truth

- 18:15 "Why do they not produce clear evidence about them? Who could be more unjust than someone who makes up lies about God" · the young men of the cave demand evidence of their whole society; the burden of proof is on the claimant.
- 18:49 "The record of their deeds will be laid open ... ‘Woe to us! What a record this is! It does not leave any deed, small or large, unaccounted for!’" · the complete log, nothing small omitted; an audit trail as the shape of justice.
- 19:79 "No! We shall certainly record what he says and prolong his punishment" · speech is recorded, not just deeds.
- 20:58-59 "make an appointment between us which neither of us will fail to keep, in a mutually agreeable place" / "Your meeting will be on the day of the feast, so let the people be assembled when the sun has risen high" · the contest with pharaoh's sorcerers is set at an agreed time, in an agreed place, in full daylight, before the public. truth is tested in the open.
- 20:61 "Beware, do not invent lies against God or He will destroy you with His punishment. Whoever invents lies will fail." · fabrication fails; a flat rule.
- 20:62-63 "So they discussed their plan among themselves, talking secretly" · the opposition confers in secret; the message is proclaimed at noon. secrecy marks the wrong side twice in this chunk (see 21:3).
- 21:18 "We hurl the truth against falsehood, and truth obliterates it- see how falsehood vanishes away!" · truth is active and thrown; falsehood does not survive contact with it.
- 21:24 "Say, ‘Bring your proof. This is the Scripture for those who are with me and the Scripture for those who went before me.’" · bring your proof, and here is mine: a symmetric demand with the speaker's own evidence on the table.
- 21:56 "Your true Lord is the Lord of the heavens and the earth, He who created them, and I am a witness to this" · abraham speaks as a witness, not an opinion holder.
- 21:61 / 21:63 "Bring him before the eyes of the people, so that they may witness [his trial]" / "He said, ‘No, it was done by the biggest of them- this one. Ask them, if they can talk.’" · abraham's demonstration: leave the largest idol standing and let it answer for itself. a test the thing must pass on its own.
- 21:94 "If anyone does good deeds and is a believer, his efforts will not be ignored: We record them for him" · effort is recorded on the credit side too; the log is not only for wrongs.
- 22:70 "Are you [Prophet] not aware that God knows all that is in the heavens and earth? All this is written in a Record; this is easy for God" · everything is written; the written record is the ideal of complete knowledge.
- 22:71 "Yet beside God they serve that for which He has sent no authority and of which they have no knowledge" · serving what has no authority and no knowledge behind it; both are needed.
- 23:62 "We do not burden any soul with more than it can bear- We have a Record that tells the truth- they will not be wronged" · a truthful record is the guarantee against injustice.
- 23:117 "a god for whose existence he has no evidence- will face his reckoning" · belief without evidence is itself the fault named.
- 24:4 "As for those who accuse chaste women of fornication, and then fail to provide four witnesses, strike them eighty times, and reject their testimony ever afterwards: they are the lawbreakers" · an evidentiary standard with a cost for failing it: the accuser who cannot prove loses standing as a witness for life.
- 24:11-13 "every one of them will be charged with the sin he has earned. He who took the greatest part in it will have a painful punishment" / "why did believing men and women not think well of their own people and declare, ‘This is obviously a lie’" / "why did the accusers not bring four witnesses to it? If they cannot produce such witnesses, they are the liars in God’s eyes" · the rumour protocol: each repeater is charged for their share, the largest share pays most, the default should be good faith, and an unproven accusation makes the accuser the liar.
- 24:16 "why did you not say, ‘We should not repeat this- God forbid!- It is a monstrous slander’" · the correct response to an unverified report is to refuse to repeat it.
- 24:24 "on the Day when their own tongues, hands, and feet will testify against them about what they have done" · the body as witness; what the hands did is evidence.
- 24:25 "they will realize that God is the Truth that makes everything clear" · truth and clarity are one attribute here.
- 24:47 "They say, ‘We believe in God and the Messenger; we obey,’ but then some of them turn away: these people are not true believers" · saying and then not doing; the local relative of 61:2-3.
- 24:53 "Tell them, ‘Do not swear: it is reasonable obedience that is required, and God is aware of everything you do.’" · do not swear oaths about what you will do; ordinary observable action is what is asked. (itani: "Obedience will be recognized.")
- 25:4-5 "‘This can only be a lie he has forged with the help of others’" / "‘It is just ancient fables, which he has had written down: they are dictated to him morning and evening.’" · the text records the accusations of forgery and ghost-writing against itself, then answers them at 25:6 and 25:33.
- 25:72 "those who do not give false testimony" · in the list of the servants' qualities, the negative duty of not bearing false witness.

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship, custody

- 18:19 "One of you go to the city with your silver coins, find out where the best food is there, and bring some back. But be careful not to let anyone know about you" · a small errand of trust with the group's money and the group's safety.
- 18:79 "the boat belonged to some needy people who made their living from the sea and I damaged it because I knew that coming after them was a king who was seizing every [serviceable] boat by force" · custody of others' property can mean damaging it to keep it from seizure; the steward acts for the owner's real interest, not the appearance.
- 18:82 "The wall belonged to two young orphans in the town and there was buried treasure beneath it belonging to them. Their father had been a righteous man, so your Lord intended them to reach maturity and then dig up their treasure" · custody for those not yet able to hold what is theirs; the wall is a vault kept until maturity.
- 19:5-6 "grant me a successor––a gift from You–– to be my heir and the heir of the family of Jacob" · succession is prayed for; what is built needs someone to hand it to.
- 19:40 "It is We who will inherit the earth and all who are on it: they will all be returned to Us" · everything reverts; ownership is temporary custody.
- 19:54 "the story of Ishmael. He was true to his promise" · a whole prophet summarised as keeping his word.
- 20:39 "Put your child into the chest, then place him in the river. Let the river wash him on to its bank, and he will be taken in by an enemy of Mine and his" · the infant entrusted to a river and raised in the enemy's house; custody can pass through hostile hands and still be kept.
- 20:86-87 "Did you want anger to fall on you from your Lord and so broke your word to me?" / [sahih, via verse.py, because the haleem line carries a word the house forbids] "They said, "We did not break our promise to you by our will, but we were made to carry burdens from the ornaments of the people [of Pharaoh], so we threw them [into the fire], and thus did the Samiri throw" · a promise broken under the weight of other people's wealth; the calf is what carrying that weight produced.
- 21:105 "We wrote in the Psalms, as We did in [earlier] Scripture: ‘My righteous servants will inherit the earth.’" · the earth is inherited by the righteous, a written promise.
- 22:41 "those who, when We establish them in the land, keep up the prayer, pay the prescribed alms, command what is right, and forbid what is wrong" · what being established in a place requires of the one established.
- 23:8 "who are faithful to their trusts and pledges" · amana named directly in the list of the prosperous believers (itani: "faithful to their trusts and pledges").
- 24:27-28 "Believers, do not enter other people’s houses until you have asked permission to do so and greeted those inside" / "If you find no one in, do not enter unless you have been given permission to do so. If you are told, ‘Go away’, then do so- that is more proper for you." · the consent rule. no entry without the owner's permission; absence is not permission; a refusal must be obeyed. this is the owner's keypress as scripture.
- 24:29 "You will not be blamed for entering houses where no one lives, and which could provide you with some useful service" · the one exception: an unowned, uninhabited place that is of use may be entered. the commons is distinguished from the private.
- 24:33 "If any of your slaves wish to pay for their freedom, make a contract with them accordingly, if you know they have good in them, and give them some of the wealth God has given you" · a path to freedom is made contractual and the owner is told to fund it.
- 24:54 "he is responsible for the duty placed upon him, and you are responsible for the duty placed upon you" · duties are assigned per person and each answers for their own.
- 24:55 "He will make them successors to the land, as He did those who came before them" · succession to the land as a promise to those who believe and do good; the khalifa idea in verb form.
- 24:58 "at other times, there is no blame on you or them if you move around each other freely. In this way God makes messages clear" · permission at set times of privacy, free movement otherwise: a permission model with scope and schedule.
- 24:61 "houses you have the keys for, or any of your friends’ houses, you will not be blamed" · the key as the mark of granted custody.
- 25:20 "But We have made some of you a means of testing others- will you stand fast? Your Lord is always watching" · people are entrusted to one another as tests.

## 4. craft and excellence

- 18:7 "We have adorned the earth with attractive things so that We may test people to find out which of them do best" · the test of life is which do best, a craft standard stated at the start of the surah.
- 18:30 "We do not let the reward of anyone who does a good deed go to waste" · no good work is wasted.
- 18:46 "Wealth and children are the attractions of this worldly life, but lasting good works have a better reward with your Lord" · lasting work outranks wealth.
- 18:77 "they saw a wall there that was on the point of falling down and the man repaired it" · repair is the work of the man given knowledge; he fixes a wall in a town that refused him food.
- 18:96 "bring me lumps of iron!’ and then, when he had filled the gap between the two mountainsides [he said], ‘Work your bellows!’ and then, when he had made it glow like fire, he said, ‘Bring me molten metal to pour over it!’" · a forging process described step by step: raw material, fill the gap, bellows, heat to glow, pour the alloy. the quran narrates a build.
- 18:104 "whose efforts in this world are misguided, even when they think they are doing good work" · effort and sincerity are not enough; misguided work still comes to nothing.
- 20:18 "‘It is my staff,’ he said, ‘I lean on it; restrain my sheep with it; I also have other uses for it.’" · the humble many-use tool, described fondly, then thrown down to become a sign. a maker's object at the centre of a theophany.
- 20:50 "Our Lord is He who gave everything its form, then gave it guidance" · form first, then guidance; a design principle stated by moses to pharaoh.
- 21:73 "We inspired them to do good works, to keep up the prayer, and to give alms" · the prophets are makers of good works before they are anything else.
- 21:80 "We taught him how to make coats of mail for the benefit of you [people], to protect you in your wars, but are you grateful for this" · david is taught a craft, and the craft is armour for the people's protection. the maker's line "Knowledge is armor" has a verse.
- 21:81-82 "We harnessed the stormy wind for Solomon" / "We made some of the jinn subservient to him, to dive for him and do other works besides" · natural force and skilled labour placed in service of a builder.
- 23:14 "and later We made him into other forms––glory be to God, the best of creators!" · God is praised as the best of creators; making is a divine attribute and the standard of excellence is set by the maker of makers.
- 23:27 "Build the Ark under Our watchful eye and according to Our revelation" · build under watch and to a specification; the ark is engineered, not improvised.
- 23:29 "My Lord, let me land with Your blessing: it is You who provide the best landings" · the landing is prayed for as well as the launch; shipping matters as much as building.
- 23:51 "Messengers, eat good things and do good deeds: I am well aware of what you do" · to the messengers themselves: good food, good deeds, under observation.
- 23:61 "are the ones who race toward good things, and they will be the first to get them" · racing toward good deeds; speed in the right direction is praised.
- 24:38 "God will reward such people according to the best of their actions" · reward is measured against the best of what they did.
- 25:2 "who created all things and made them to an exact measure" · exact measure as the mark of creation; precision is a virtue of the maker.
- 25:70 "God will change the evil deeds of such people into good ones" · past bad work can be turned to good; iteration is possible.

## 5. justice and balance

- 18:87-88 "‘We shall punish those who have done evil ... while those who believed and did good deeds will have the best of rewards: we shall command them to do what is easy for them.’" · dhul-qarnayn given power uses it to make things easy for the good; power well held lowers the burden on the well-behaved.
- 18:105 "Their deeds come to nothing: on the Day of Resurrection We shall give them no weight" · weight is the measure of a deed.
- 21:47 "We will set up scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection so that no one can be wronged in the least, and if there should be even the weight of a mustard seed, We shall bring it out- We take excellent account" · one of the ten axioms. the scales, the mustard seed, and "excellent account": no wrong so small it is not weighed.
- 21:78-79 "when they gave judgement regarding the field into which sheep strayed by night and grazed. We witnessed their judgement" / "and made Solomon understand the case [better]" · a concrete case of damage and liability, judged, witnessed, and with one judge understanding better than the other.
- 21:112 "He said,‘My Lord, pass the true judgement.’" · the prophet's own appeal is to a true judgement, not a favourable one.
- 22:10 "‘This is for what you have stored up with your own hands: God is never unjust to His creatures.’" · outcomes are earned by hands; justice is never arbitrary.
- 22:39-40 "Those who have been attacked are permitted to take up arms because they have been wronged" / "If God did not repel some people by means of others, many monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques, where God’s name is much invoked, would have been destroyed" · pushback is permitted to the wronged, and its purpose is to keep the sanctuaries of every faith standing. resistance as a balance of powers that protects pluralism.
- 22:60 "God will help those who retaliate against an aggressive act merely with its like and are then wronged again" · proportionality: the response is measured to the act, "merely with its like".
- 23:62 "We do not burden any soul with more than it can bear" · fair load; nobody is asked for more than their capacity.
- 23:102-103 "those whose good deeds weigh heavy will be successful but those whose balance is light will have lost their souls for ever" · the balance again, heavy and light.
- 24:22 "Those who have been graced with bounty and plenty should not swear that they will [no longer] give to kinsmen, the poor, those who emigrated in God’s way: let them pardon and forgive" · the wealthy may not cut off giving out of a grudge; plenty carries a duty that anger does not cancel.
- 24:25 "on that Day, God will pay them their just due in full" · the account is settled in full.
- 24:33 "Do not force your slave-girls into prostitution, when they themselves wish to remain honourable, in your quest for the short-term gains of this world" · extracting from the vulnerable for short-term gain is named and forbidden.
- 25:2 "made them to an exact measure" · measure as a property of everything made.
- 25:67 "They are those who are neither wasteful nor niggardly when they spend, but keep to a just balance" · a just balance in spending, between waste and meanness (itani: "choose a middle course").
- 25:68 "nor take a life, which God has made sacred, except in the pursuit of justice" · the sanctity of life and the single exception of justice.

## 6. agency and self-change

- 18:29 "Say, ‘Now the truth has come from your Lord: let those who wish to believe in it do so, and let those who wish to reject it do so.’" · the truth is placed before people and the choice is theirs; consequences follow but the choosing is free.
- 18:35 "He went into his garden and wronged himself by saying, ‘I do not think this will ever perish" · the rich man's fall is described as wronging himself; the harm is self-inflicted by a belief.
- 18:57 "ignoring what his hands are storing up for him" · the hands store up the future; the future is made by what one does now.
- 18:110 "Anyone who fears to meet his Lord should do good deeds" · the answer to fear is deeds.
- 19:25 "and, if you shake the trunk of the palm tree towards you, it will deliver fresh ripe dates for you" · mary in labour is given a stream at her feet, but the dates come only if she shakes the trunk. grace provides, agency collects. this is the whole relation between help and effort in one image.
- 19:80 / 19:95 "he will come to Us all alone" / "they will each return to Him on the Day of Resurrection all alone" · each answers alone; no crowd, no following, no company at the reckoning.
- 20:15 "for each soul to be rewarded for its labour" · reward is for labour, per soul.
- 20:56 "We showed Pharaoh all Our signs, but he denied them and refused [to change]" · the refusal to change is pharaoh's sin, before the drowning.
- 20:72 "So decide whatever you will: you can only decide matters of this present life" · the converted sorcerers to pharaoh: your power over us stops at this life. a bounded tyrant.
- 20:96 "I saw something they did not; I took in some of the teachings of the Messenger but tossed them aside: my soul prompted me to do what I did." · the samiri owns his own agency in the wrong direction: special sight, partial learning, then his own soul's prompting.
- 21:35 "We test you all through the bad and the good, and to Us you will all return" · good fortune is a test as much as bad.
- 22:11 "if something good comes their way, they are satisfied, but if they are tested, they revert to their old ways, losing both this world and the next- that is the clearest loss" · reverting to old defaults under pressure is named "the clearest loss".
- 22:15 "Anyone who thinks that God will not support him in this world and the next should stretch a rope up to the sky, climb all the way up it, and see whether this strategy removes the cause of his anger" · a sardonic image about anger that reaches for impossible strategies instead of changing itself.
- 22:78 "Strive hard for God as is His due: He has chosen you and placed no hardship in your religion" · striving and no hardship in the same verse; effort without cruelty to oneself.
- 23:99-100 "he cries, ‘My Lord, let me return so as to make amends for the things I neglected.’ Never! This will not go beyond his words" · the wish to go back and fix what was neglected comes too late; the time to act is before.
- 24:11 "every one of them will be charged with the sin he has earned" · responsibility is individual and proportional.
- 25:17-18 "‘Was it you [false gods] who led these creatures of Mine astray, or did they stray from the path by themselves?’" / "You granted them and their forefathers pleasures in this life, until they forgot Your Reminder and were ruined." · the idols disown the blame; the cause was comfort that led to forgetting. the maker's own line about rebelling against comfort has its verse here.
- 25:27-29 "‘If only I had taken the same path as the Messenger Woe is me! If only I had not taken so and so as a friend he led me away from the Revelation after it reached me." · the company you keep changes your path; the regret is about a friend, not an enemy.
- 25:43 "Think [Prophet] of the man who has taken his own passion as a god: are you to be his guardian" · the one ruled by his own appetite has made a god of it; and nobody can be his guardian for him.
- 25:70-71 "God will change the evil deeds of such people into good ones" / "People who repent and do good deeds truly return to God" · change is real and available; the past can be rewritten by repentance and good work.

## 7. time and mortality

- 18:23-24 "do not say of anything, ‘I will do that tomorrow,’ without adding, ‘God willing,’" · a rule of speech about the future; every plan is conditional and said so.
- 18:45 "We send water down from the skies and the earth’s vegetation absorbs it, but soon the plants turn to dry stubble scattered about by the wind" · the life of this world as one season of growth then stubble.
- 18:60 "‘I will not rest until I reach the place where the two seas meet, even if it takes me years!’" · moses commits years to reaching a teacher; long time spent for knowledge is honoured.
- 18:98 "But when my Lord’s promise is fulfilled, He will raze this barrier to the ground: my Lord’s promise always comes true." · dhul-qarnayn says of his own finished work that it will be razed. the builder knows the wall is temporary and says so at the ribbon-cutting.
- 19:4 "‘Lord, my bones have weakened and my hair is ashen grey, but never, Lord, have I ever prayed to You in vain" · zachariah prays from old age, and the answer is a son named for the first time.
- 19:84 "There is no need for you to be impatient concerning them: We are counting down their [allotted] time" · urgency belongs to God's count, not to the prophet's anxiety; work without panic.
- 19:94 "He has counted them all: He has numbered them exactly" · everyone is counted exactly.
- 20:15 "The Hour is coming––though I choose to keep it hidden––for each soul to be rewarded for its labour" · the hour is hidden by choice, and the hiddenness is tied to labour being rewarded.
- 20:103-104 "‘You stayed only ten days [on earth]’ ... the more perceptive of them will say, ‘Your stay [on earth] was only a single day.’" · at the end, the perceptive judge the whole life as a single day.
- 20:130 "celebrate the praise of your Lord, before the rising and setting of the sun, celebrate His praise during the night, and at the beginning and end of the day, so that you may find contentment" · a daily rhythm of remembrance, framed by sunrise and sunset, whose fruit is contentment.
- 21:1 "Ever closer to people draws their reckoning, while they turn away, heedless" · the surah of the prophets opens with the reckoning approaching and people looking away.
- 21:35 "Every soul is certain to taste death" · the plainest statement of the finite span.
- 21:37 "Man was created hasty: I will show you My signs soon, so do not ask Me to hasten them" · haste is the human default; the signs come at their own pace.
- 21:104 "On that Day, We shall roll up the skies as a writer rolls up [his] scrolls" · the end of time as a scribe rolling up the page.
- 21:109 "I do not know whether the judgement you are promised is near or far" · the messenger himself does not know the hour; honest uncertainty about the deadline.
- 22:5 "Whatever We choose We cause to remain in the womb for an appointed time, then We bring you forth as infants and then you grow and reach maturity. Some die young and some are left to live on to such an age that they forget all they once knew" · the whole human span, with the two ends named: dying young or outliving your own knowledge.
- 22:47 "a Day with your Lord is like a thousand years by your reckoning" · two clocks; the human one is not the only one.
- 23:43 "no community can advance or delay its time" · collective mortality: communities have spans too.
- 23:113-114 "‘We stayed a day or a part of a day, but ask those who keep count.’ He will say, ‘You stayed but a little, if you had only known" · the dead defer to "those who keep count"; keeping count is a role.
- 25:45-46 "Do you not see how your Lord lengthens the shade? If He had willed, He could have made it stand still- We made the sun its indicator but We gradually draw it towards Us, little by little" · the sundial: the shadow lengthens, the sun marks it, and it is drawn in little by little. this is the chunk's hourglass.
- 25:47 "It is He who made the night a garment for you, and sleep a rest, and made the day like a resurrection" · each day is a small resurrection; each night a garment. time as daily renewal rather than only as countdown.

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- 18:28 "those who follow their own low desires, those whose ways are unbridled" · the heedless heart, low desire and the unbridled way, named as what not to yield to.
- 18:34-36 "‘I have more wealth and a larger following than you.’ He went into his garden and wronged himself by saying, ‘I do not think this will ever perish or that the Last Hour will ever come" · the garden owner's three marks: wealth, following, and belief in his own permanence. follower counts are in the quran.

## 12. building and repairing stories

- 18:21 "[Some] said, ‘Construct a building over them: their Lord knows best about them.’ Those who prevailed said, ‘We shall build a place of worship over them.’" · the first reflex after a wonder is to build over it; the text records the debate about what to build.
- 18:71 / 18:79 "when they got into a boat, and the man made a hole in it" / "the boat belonged to some needy people who made their living from the sea and I damaged it because I knew that coming after them was a king who was seizing every [serviceable] boat by force" · the deliberate flaw as protection. a boat too damaged to be worth seizing stays with the poor who need it. a tool made less attractive to the empire is a tool that survives in the owner's hands.
- 18:77 / 18:82 "they came to a town and asked the inhabitants for food but were refused hospitality, they saw a wall there that was on the point of falling down and the man repaired it. Moses said, ‘But if you had wished you could have taken payment for doing that.’" / "The wall belonged to two young orphans in the town and there was buried treasure beneath it belonging to them" · repair done unpaid, in a town that refused them food, for orphans who did not know what was under the wall. the repairer's motive is invisible to the town and to the beneficiaries.

- 18:94-97 "Will you build a barrier between them and us if we pay you a tribute?’" / "‘The power my Lord has given me is better than any tribute, but if you lend me your strength, I will put up a fortification between you and them" / "bring me lumps of iron!’ ... ‘Work your bellows!’ ... ‘Bring me molten metal to pour over it!’" / "Their enemies could not scale the barrier, nor could they pierce it" · the full build: a request with payment offered, payment refused, the people's labour asked instead, materials called for, a process run, and a result tested against two failure modes (scaling and piercing).
- 18:98 "‘This is a mercy from my Lord. But when my Lord’s promise is fulfilled, He will raze this barrier to the ground" · the builder credits the mercy, not himself, and names the wall's expiry.
- 20:39 "Put your child into the chest, then place him in the river. Let the river wash him on to its bank" · a chest built to carry a child down a river into the enemy's house; the smallest vessel in the chunk.
- 21:80-82 "We taught him how to make coats of mail for the benefit of you [people]" / "We harnessed the stormy wind for Solomon" / "to dive for him and do other works besides" · david's armour, solomon's wind and divers: taught craft, harnessed force, skilled labour.
- 22:26 "‘Do not assign partners to Me. Purify My House for those who circle around it, those who stand to pray, and those who bow and prostrate themselves" · abraham shown the site and told to purify the house for those who will use it; a building spec written from the users' postures.
- 23:27-29 "‘Build the Ark under Our watchful eye and according to Our revelation. - 23:44 "so We destroyed them one after the other and made them into cautionary tales" · ruined communities become cautionary tales; the record of failure is itself a product.
- 24:36 "shining out in houses of worship. God has ordained that they be raised high and that His name be remembered in them" · the lamp shines in houses built to be raised high; architecture as a home for light.
- 25:53 "put an insurmountable barrier between them" · the natural barrier between the two seas, set beside the built one at 18:95.

## 14. clarity and plain speech

- 18:13 "We shall tell you their story as it really was" · the story told as it was, against the crowd's guessing at 18:22.
- 18:22 "so do not argue, but stick to what is clear" · the rule for disputed detail: say what is clear.
- 18:54 "In this Quran We have presented every kind of description for people but man is more contentious than any other creature" · every kind of description is offered; the obstacle is contentiousness, not lack of explanation.
- 19:97 "We have made it easy, in your own language [Prophet], so that you may bring glad news to the righteous and warnings to a stubborn people" · made easy, in the hearer's own language, for a purpose.
- 20:27-28 "Untie my tongue so that they may understand my words" · moses' prayer before facing pharaoh is for speech that can be understood.
- 20:113 "We have sent the Quran down in the Arabic tongue and given all kinds of warnings in it, so that they may beware or take heed" · the local tongue and varied warnings, for the sake of taking heed.
- 21:48 "[the Scripture] that distinguishes right from wrong, a light and a reminder" · the criterion that distinguishes, and a light: discrimination and illumination as the two functions of a clear text (itani and sahih: "the Criterion").
- 22:16 "In this way, We send the Quran down as clear messages" · clear messages is the stated form.
- 22:73 "People, here is an illustration, so listen carefully" · the parable is announced before it is given; the audience is told to listen.

- 24:58 / 24:59 / 24:61 "In this way God makes messages clear: God is all knowing, all wise" / "This is how God makes His messages clear to you" / "This is how God makes His messages clear to you so that you may understand" · the household rules each close with the same refrain: this is how clarity is made. and what precedes each refrain is a rule with its edge cases spelled out.
- 24:54 "the Messenger’s duty is only to deliver the message clearly" · the duty is clarity, nothing more and nothing less.
- 25:1 "Exalted is He who has sent the Differentiator down to His servant so that it may be a warning to all people" · the book named as the thing that differentiates.
- 25:33 "They cannot put any argument to you without Our bringing you the truth and the best explanation" · every argument is met with the best explanation.
- 25:50 "Many times We have repeated this to people so that they might take heed, but most persist in their ingratitude" · repetition is a deliberate method of clarity.

## 15. livelihood and trade

- 18:19 "go to the city with your silver coins, find out where the best food is there, and bring some back" · coins and a market errand inside a miracle story; commerce is ordinary and fine.
- 18:77 "‘But if you had wished you could have taken payment for doing that.’" · moses thinks the repair should have been paid for; khidr's silence and then the orphans' treasure answer him. some repairs are done for those who cannot yet pay.
- 18:79 "some needy people who made their living from the sea" · the boat is a livelihood; damaging it to save it protects an income, not an object.
- 18:94-95 "Will you build a barrier between them and us if we pay you a tribute?’ He answered, ‘The power my Lord has given me is better than any tribute, but if you lend me your strength" · payment offered for protection is refused in favour of shared labour; the builder already has what he needs and asks the community for its hands instead.
- 19:31 "He commanded me to pray, to give alms as long as I live" · alms as a lifelong duty.
- 20:132 "We are not asking you to give Us provision;We provide for you" · provision flows one way; the maker is not asked to feed God.
- 22:28 "to attain benefits and celebrate God’s name, on specified days, over the livestock He has provided for them- feed yourselves and the poor and unfortunate" · the pilgrimage is for benefits as well as worship, and the benefit is shared with the poor.
- 23:72 "Do you [Prophet] ask them for any payment? Your Lord’s is the best payment: He is the Best of Providers" · the messenger asks no fee for the message.
- 24:33 "in your quest for the short-term gains of this world" · short-term gain from the vulnerable is the named sin.
- 24:37 "men who are not distracted, either by commerce or profit, from remembering God, keeping up the prayer, and paying the prescribed alms" · commerce and profit are not condemned; being distracted by them is. the lamp of 24:35 shines in houses of men who trade and are not distracted.
- 25:7 / 25:20 "‘What sort of messenger is this? He eats food and walks about in the marketplaces!" / "No messenger have We sent before you [Muhammad] who did not eat food and walk about in the marketplace." · every messenger walked in the marketplace. the maker's line that sandbox is a business only in a loose sense has its precedent: prophets ate and traded.
- 25:57 "‘I am not asking for any reward for it, but anyone who wishes should take a path to his Lord.’" · the message itself is free.
- 25:67 "neither wasteful nor niggardly when they spend, but keep to a just balance" · spending in balance is a mark of the servants.

## 16. warnings for the manifesto's own posture

- 18:6 "are you going to worry yourself to death over them if they do not believe in this message" · the reformer's burnout is warned against directly.
- 18:34 "‘I have more wealth and a larger following than you.’" · the garden owner's boast is followers; a workshop that counts followers has read this verse.
- 18:104 "whose efforts in this world are misguided, even when they think they are doing good work" · the sharpest warning in the chunk for anyone who believes they are repairing the world: misguided effort feels like good work from the inside.
- 19:14 / 19:32 "not domineering or rebellious" / "He did not make me domineering or graceless" · the quran praises john and jesus for not being domineering, and john for not being rebellious. the maker's word rebellion needs its object stated every time: rebellion against harmful defaults and one's own comfort, never against the Lord or against parents, and never domineering. the manifesto should say what its rebellion is against and what it is not.
- 19:73 "‘Which side is better situated? Which side has the better following?’" · the wrong side's argument is status and following; do not answer in kind.
- 20:83-85 "‘Moses, what has made you come ahead of your people in such haste?’" / "‘They are following in my footsteps. I rushed to You, Lord, to please You,’" / "‘We have tested your people in your absence: the Samiri has led them astray.’" · the leader who rushes ahead and assumes the people are following comes back to find them worshipping a calf. move fast, but not ahead of the people you are moving for.
- 20:94 "I was afraid you would say, “You have caused division among the children of Israel and have not heeded what I said.”" · aaron's fear of dividing the community; the manifesto should weigh unity against correction as he had to.
- 20:96 "I saw something they did not" · the samiri's excuse is special insight. beware of "I saw something they did not" as a self-justification.
- 21:2-3 / 21:16 "they listen to it playfully with frivolous hearts" / "We did not create the heavens and the earth and everything between them playfully" · the maker wants sandbox to be playful. the text distinguishes play from frivolity: creation is not a pastime. the manifesto's playfulness must be the earnest kind.
- 21:37 "Man was created hasty" · haste is the default, and a house that moves fast must know it.
- 21:55 "‘Have you brought us the truth or are you just playing about?’" · the question abraham's people put to him is the question a weird, experimental house will be asked. the answer was a demonstration (21:58-63), not a defence.
- 21:93 / 23:53 "They have torn their unity apart" / "each rejoicing in their own" · sect-making, and the pleasure of one's own sect, is named as the community's failure.
- 22:11 "if they are tested, they revert to their old ways" · reverting to old defaults under test is the clearest loss.
- 23:24 "trying to gain some superiority over you" · the charge noah faced: that reform is a bid for status. the manifesto should be written so that this charge finds nothing to hold.
- 24:11-16 the rumour protocol (quoted in section 2) · a publishing house must not repeat what it has not verified; the repeater is charged for his share.
- 24:47 "‘We believe in God and the Messenger; we obey,’ but then some of them turn away" · saying and not doing.
- 24:53 "‘Do not swear: it is reasonable obedience that is required" · no grand oaths about what will be done; ordinary, visible doing.
- 24:2 "Do not let compassion for them keep you from carrying out God’s law" · the text has a severe side. a manifesto that leans on mercy should not pretend the book is only gentle; it should say plainly where it is choosing its emphasis.
- 22:39-40 / 22:60 "Those who have been attacked are permitted to take up arms because they have been wronged" / "retaliate against an aggressive act merely with its like" · the text's own rebellion is defensive, for the wronged, and proportional. "constructive, never cruel" has its bounds here: like for like, and no more.
- 25:43 "the man who has taken his own passion as a god" · the maker's own passion for the mission can become the god; the verse is aimed at the reader.
- 25:72 "when they see some frivolity, pass by with dignity" · the response to slop is to pass by with dignity, not to fight it in its own register.

## how the text itself speaks

- direct address to one person in the middle of a public text: "[Prophet]" and "Say," recur throughout (18:6, 18:9, 18:22, 18:26, 18:29, 18:103, 18:109, 18:110, 21:24, 25:6, 25:15, 25:57). the reader overhears instructions to the speaker; the manifesto could speak to its own author in front of its readers.
- questions as refrains: "Will you not use your reason" (21:10), "will you not use your minds" (23:80), "Will you not heed Him?" (23:23, 23:32), "Have you no sense?" (21:67), "but are you grateful for this" (21:80), "Do you not see" (24:41, 24:43, 25:45). the question puts the work on the reader.
- the catechism at 23:84-89: "Say [Prophet], ‘Who owns the earth and all who live in it, if you know [so much]?’ and they will reply, ‘God.’ Say, ‘Will you not take heed?’", repeated three times with rising questions, ending "‘Then how can you be so deluded?’". a socratic ladder built from the opponent's own answers.
- serial openers that turn a surah into a gallery: "Mention too, in the Quran, the story of" (19:41, 19:51, 19:54, 19:56); "And remember" (21:78, 21:83, 21:85, 21:87, 21:89, 21:91); "He travelled on" (18:85, 18:89, 18:92); "Exalted is He who" (25:1, 25:10, 25:61); "It is He who" (25:47, 25:48, 25:53, 25:54); "So it will be" (22:60, 22:61, 22:62).
- closing refrains after rules: "This is how God makes His messages clear to you" (24:58, 24:59, 24:61). each rule ends by naming clarity as its own purpose.
- the same cry repeated across a surah: "‘Woe to us! We were wrong!’" (21:14, 21:46, 21:97).
- lists of qualities as portraits: 23:1-11 ("[How] prosperous are the believers Those who pray humbly who shun idle talk who pay the prescribed alms ... who are faithful to their trusts and pledges") and 25:63-76 ("The servants of the Lord of Mercy are those who"). the manifesto can describe its people as a list of habits rather than a list of beliefs.
- drama in dialogue, with the speakers' own words: khidr and moses (18:66-82), abraham and his father (19:42-48), abraham and the idol-makers (21:52-67), moses and pharaoh and the sorcerers (20:49-73), moses and aaron and the samiri (20:86-97), the two garden owners (18:34-41). the text argues by staging the argument.
- parables announced before they are given: "Tell them the parable of two men" (18:32); "Tell them, too, what the life of this world is like" (18:45); "People, here is an illustration, so listen carefully" (22:73); "His Light is like this" (24:35). the reader is told a comparison is coming.
- promise and warning paired in adjacent lines: 18:29-31, 18:105-108, 20:74-76, 22:14 against 22:19-24, 25:11-16. the text never gives the warning without the promise or the promise without the warning.
- oaths spoken by characters, not only by the narrator: "By God I shall certainly plot against your idols" (21:57); "By your Lord [Prophet] We shall gather them" (19:68).
- the second person plural to the crowd: "People," (22:1, 22:5, 22:73), "[People]" (24:56), "Believers," (24:21, 24:27, 24:58), "Messengers," (23:51).
- the speaker limits himself in public: "I am only a human being, like you" (18:110); "I do not know whether the judgement you are promised is near or far" (21:109); "God knows best how long they stayed" (18:26). authority is established by admitting what is not known.
- the text names its critics' words before answering: "‘Muddled dreams’; others, ‘He made it up’; yet others, ‘He is just a poet" (21:5); "‘It is just ancient fables, which he has had written down" (25:5). a manifesto can quote its expected objections in their own voice.
- the story told straight, then the gossip dismissed: "We shall tell you their story as it really was" (18:13) and then "guessing in the dark" (18:22).
- the method stated inside the text: "We sent it in this way to strengthen your heart [Prophet]; We gave it to you in gradual revelation" (25:32); "Many times We have repeated this to people so that they might take heed" (25:50). the text explains why it is gradual and why it repeats.
- rules with their edge cases: 24:27-29 (houses with no one in, houses no one lives in), 24:58-61 (three times of privacy, children reaching puberty, elderly women, the blind, the lame, houses you have the keys for). the form of a spec: the rule, then the exceptions, then "so that you may understand".
- the physical build narrated as imperatives: "bring me lumps of iron!" / "Work your bellows!" / "Bring me molten metal" (18:96). a process written as commands in sequence.

## name and mark candidates

- furqan (the criterion): surah 25 is "Al-Furqan (The Criterion)" and 25:1 has "sent the Differentiator down to His servant so that it may be a warning to all people" (itani and sahih: "the Criterion"; asad: "the standard by which to discern the true from the false"). 21:48 gives the same word for moses' scripture: "[the Scripture] that distinguishes right from wrong, a light and a reminder" (itani: "the Criterion, and illumination"). a house that "exposes and aims to repair and gives Radical Clarity" has its word here: the criterion is what separates true from false, and it is called a light in the same breath.
- nur (light): surah 24 is "An-Nur (The Light)"; 24:35 "God is the Light of the heavens and earth ... light upon light"; 24:40 "The one to whom God gives no light has no light at all"; 21:48 "a light and a reminder"; 25:61 "a radiant light, and an illuminating moon". the niche, the lamp, the glass and the olive (24:35) are four sub-names inside one image; the niche (sahih: "a niche within which is a lamp") is a strong small mark for a place that holds a light.
- mizan (balance, scales): 21:47 "scales of justice"; 23:102-103 "weigh heavy" / "balance is light"; 25:67 "a just balance"; 25:2 "an exact measure".
- burhan (proof): 21:24 "Bring your proof"; 23:117 "a god for whose existence he has no evidence".
- witness and the chain of witnesses: 21:56 "I am a witness to this"; 21:61 "so that they may witness [his trial]"; 21:78 "We witnessed their judgement"; 22:78 "so that the Messenger can bear witness about you and so that you can bear witness about other people" (a two-link chain); 24:4 and 24:13 "four witnesses"; 24:24 "tongues, hands, and feet will testify".
- the record, the pen, the scroll: 18:49 "The record of their deeds will be laid open"; 18:109 "If the whole ocean were ink"; 20:52 "all in a record"; 21:104 "as a writer rolls up [his] scrolls"; 22:70 "written in a Record"; 23:62 "a Record that tells the truth"; 25:5 "which he has had written down".
- raqim (the inscription): 18:9 "the Companions in the Cave and al-Raqim", left untranslated by haleem; itani and sahih give "the Inscription". an obscure, strange name meaning a written tablet, tied to a story of refuge and long sleep; it fits "playful, strange, weird".
- kahf (the cave): 18:10 "sought refuge in the cave"; 18:16 "take refuge in the cave. God will shower His mercy on you and make you an easy way out of your ordeal." a refuge with an easy way out; the sandbox as a cave.
- sabil / path / routes: 20:53 "traced routes in it"; 21:31 "set broad paths on it, so that they might follow the right direction"; 19:43 "an even path"; 23:73 "a straight path"; 25:57 "take a path to his Lord".
- hikma (wisdom) and sound judgement: 19:12 "While he was still a boy, We granted him wisdom"; 21:51 "right judgement"; 21:74 and 21:79 "sound judgement and knowledge".
- amana (trust): 23:8 "faithful to their trusts and pledges".
- iqra / recite: 20:114 "do not rush to recite before the revelation is fully complete".
- time marks: 25:45-46 the shade and "the sun its indicator" (a sundial rather than an hourglass); 19:84 "counting down"; 23:113 "ask those who keep count"; 21:104 the rolled-up scroll.
- the barrier (sadd) and the two seas: 18:95 "fortification" (itani and sahih: "a dam"); 25:53 "an insurmountable barrier between them". a mark for something that keeps the corrupting thing out without mixing.
- the bellows (18:96): a workshop word inside scripture.
- tuwa: 20:12 "Take off your shoes: you are in the sacred valley of Tuwa". a place-name for where shoes come off and instruction begins.
- the staff (20:18): "I also have other uses for it".
- the differentiator (25:1, haleem's own coinage): an english mark that no one else is using.

## surprises

- 24:27-28 is the owner's keypress. "do not enter other people’s houses until you have asked permission to do so" and "If you are told, ‘Go away’, then do so". the tool's rule that nothing durable lands without the owner's consent is not a security preference; it is household law in surah 24, complete with the case of an empty house (absence is not consent) and the case of the commons at 24:29.
- 18:79 is a parable about making things un-seizable. khidr damages a poor family's boat on purpose "because I knew that coming after them was a king who was seizing every [serviceable] boat by force". a tool that is plain, local and owner-held is a boat with a hole in it: worthless to the empire, still afloat for the fishermen.
- 18:95 refuses tribute and asks for hands. the barrier against ruin is not sold to the people; they are asked to "lend me your strength", and the wall is built from their iron and their bellows. this is the blueprint-anyone-may-adopt model: the builder brings method and asks the community to bring labour.
- 18:77 and 18:82 together: khidr repairs a wall in a town that "refused hospitality", for orphans who do not know what is under it, and declines the payment moses thinks he is owed. work done for people who do not know sandbox exists, and may never know, is exactly what the maker said he is fine with.
- 20:71 names the gate: "‘How dare you believe in him before I have given you permission?" pharaoh's fury is that understanding happened without going through him. the sorcerers' answer, "you can only decide matters of this present life" (20:72), bounds his jurisdiction.
- 20:63 shows harmful defaults defending themselves as tradition: the reformers are said to want to "put an end to your time-honoured way of life".
- 20:87-89, the calf: the people were "burdened with the weight of people’s jewellery", threw it in the fire, and out came an idol that lowed and "gave them no answer". a system built from what people hand over, that emits sound and cannot answer, is a golden calf.
- 21:80 gives "Knowledge is armor" a verse: david "taught ... how to make coats of mail for the benefit of you [people], to protect you". the craft is taught by God and its purpose is public protection.
- 21:16-17 corrects the word playful: "We did not create the heavens and the earth and everything between them playfully". the text uses "playfully" for the mockers (21:2). sandbox's play must be the earnest kind; the surah's own play is abraham leaving the biggest idol standing and saying "Ask them, if they can talk" (21:63).
- 19:14 and 19:32 praise prophets for being "not domineering or rebellious" and "not ... domineering or graceless". the maker's key word, rebellion, is used in the negative here. the manifesto should be explicit about the object of its rebellion so that this verse does not read against it.
- 20:114 is a research rule: "do not rush to recite before the revelation is fully complete but say, ‘Lord, increase me in knowledge!’". finish the reading before you publish; ask for more.
- 20:83-85: moses "come ahead of your people in such haste", assuming "They are following in my footsteps", returns to a calf. moving fast ahead of the people you are moving for loses them.
- 22:40: pushback by the wronged is what keeps "monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques" standing. the text's defence of resistance is pluralist, naming four kinds of sanctuary.
- 22:5: people may live "to such an age that they forget all they once knew". the case for writing down and publishing is human forgetting.
- 24:11-16, the rumour episode, is an editorial policy: do not repeat what you did not know to be true, demand witnesses, and know that "every one of them will be charged with the sin he has earned. He who took the greatest part in it will have a painful punishment".
- 24:37: the lamp of 24:35 shines in houses of "men who are not distracted, either by commerce or profit". trade is assumed; the fault is distraction, not commerce.
- 24:53: "Do not swear: it is reasonable obedience that is required". no oaths, no manifestos of intent; ordinary observable doing.
- 25:20: "No messenger have We sent before you [Muhammad] who did not eat food and walk about in the marketplace." the messengers were in the market. a house that needs to make a living is in good company.
- 25:32: gradual release is a method "to strengthen your heart". shipping in pieces is not a compromise.
- 25:45-46 is a sundial in scripture: the shade lengthens, "We made the sun its indicator", and it is drawn in "little by little". the hourglass has a quranic cousin.
- 23:14 calls God "the best of creators". the craft standard is set by a maker.
- 18:104 is the reformer's mirror: "misguided, even when they think they are doing good work". read it before every release.
- 18:98: the builder of the great wall says at its completion that it will be razed. finished work is temporary and the good builder says so.
- 19:25: mary is given the stream but must shake the trunk. the sandbox gives tools; the shaking is the user's.
- 20:18: the staff "I lean on it; restrain my sheep with it; I also have other uses for it". the plain many-use tool is what becomes the sign.
- 21:109 and 18:26: the speaker says out loud what he does not know. authority in this text is built partly from admitted ignorance.
- 21:3 and 20:62: the wrong side "conferred in secret" and were "talking secretly"; the right side sets a public contest at noon (20:59). openness is a moral marker, not only a practical one.
- 19:47: abraham's father threatens to stone him and abraham says "Peace be with you: I will beg my Lord to forgive you". rebellion against the father's idols with warmth toward the father is the tone the maker asked for, shown rather than stated.
- 25:72: the servants who "when they see some frivolity, pass by with dignity". the answer to slop is not to fight it in its register.

## the ten axioms met here, and what sits around them

- 21:22 (coherence): "If there had been in the heavens or earth any gods but Him, both heavens and earth would be in ruins". around it, unused: 21:16-17 (not created playfully), 21:18 (truth hurled at falsehood, "see how falsehood vanishes away"), 21:23 ("He cannot be called to account for anything He does, whereas they will be called to account"), 21:24 ("Bring your proof"), 21:30-33 (the joined heavens, life from water, orbits). the argument from ruin is one of a cluster on truth, proof and order.
- 21:47 (the scales): "if there should be even the weight of a mustard seed, We shall bring it out- We take excellent account". around it, unused: 21:44 ("shrinking their borders"), 21:45 ("I warn you only through the Revelation"), 21:48 (the criterion, "a light and a reminder"), 21:35 ("We test you all through the bad and the good"). the mustard seed sits next to the criterion and the warning-only role.
- none of the other eight axiom verses fall in this chunk. near relatives here: 23:8 "faithful to their trusts and pledges" for 33:72 (amana); 24:47 "‘We believe ... we obey,’ but then some of them turn away" and 24:53 "Do not swear" for 61:2-3 (saying vs doing); 25:67 "a just balance" and 23:102-103 for 55:7-9 (mizan); 24:55 "successors to the land" for the khalifa idea; 20:114 "increase me in knowledge" for 96:1-5 (read).
