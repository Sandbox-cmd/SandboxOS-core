# notes, reader 1 of 8: surahs 1 to 3 (abdel haleem)

chunk file: scratchpad/quran/chunks/chunk-1.txt. every quotation below is copied from that file unless it is labelled with a translation name in square brackets, in which case it came from `verse.py` and sits outside my chunk.

## the axioms i met, and what sits around them

of the ten axioms (54:17, 96:6-7, 33:72, 21:22, 55:7-9, 2:222, 47:24, 61:2-3, 4:135, 21:47) only one lives in my chunk.

- 2:222 "God loves those who turn to Him, and He loves those who keep themselves clean". the axiom already uses this. what sits around it and is not yet used: 2:219 ends "In this way, God makes His messages clear to you, so that you may reflect" and 2:220 gives "God knows those who spoil things and those who improve them". so the cleanliness verse is bracketed on one side by a call to reflect and on the other by the split between spoilers and improvers. a workshop that cleans up broken defaults stands between those two lines.
- 2:256 is not one of the ten axioms but is a named reading target; see section 13.
- 4:135 lives in chunk 2 but its cousins are here: 3:18 "He upholds justice", 3:21 those "who kill those who command that justice is done", and 2:282 "more equitable in God’s eyes". for the record, 4:135 [haleem]: "uphold justice and bear witness to God, even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or your close relatives".
- 61:2-3 lives in chunk 7 but its direct ancestor is 2:44 (section 16). 61:2 [haleem]: "You who believe, why do you say things and then do not do them".
- 96:4 [haleem] "who taught by [means of] the pen" is echoed in 2:282 "let him write as God has taught him". the pen verse and the debt verse are the same teaching seen from two sides: God taught writing, so write.

## 1. knowledge and reading

- 2:31 "He taught Adam all the names [of things], then He showed them to the angels and said, ‘Tell me the names of these if you truly [think you can].’" the first thing the successor on earth is given is not power but names; knowing what things are called comes before ruling them.
- 2:32 "We have knowledge only of what You have taught us. You are the All Knowing and All Wise." the angels' reply is the model for saying what you do not know; every finding has a source and a limit.
- 2:33 "I know what you reveal and what you conceal". concealment is watched from the first chapter of the human story.
- 2:78 "Some of them are uneducated, and know the Scripture only through wish-ful thinking. They rely on guesswork". a verse against guesswork; the maker's "research and note the facts" has a scriptural enemy named here.
- 2:79 "woe to those who write something down with their own hands and then claim, ‘This is from God,’ in order to make some small gain. Woe to them for what their hands have written!" forged authority for small gain is the oldest altered narrative; provenance matters.
- 2:118 "We have made Our signs clear enough to those who have solid faith". the signs are already clear; the failure is in the reader who wants a private miracle instead.
- 2:140 "Who knows better: you or God? Who could be more wicked than those who hide a testimony [they received] from God?" hiding a testimony is ranked among the most wicked acts.
- 2:146 "Those We gave Scripture know it as well as they know their own sons, but some of them hide the truth that they know". the concealers are not ignorant; they know it as well as their own children and still hide it. gatekeeping is a choice by those who know best.
- 2:159 "As for those who hide the proofs and guidance We send down, after We have made them clear to people in the Scripture, God rejects them, and so do others". concealing clear proofs draws rejection from God and from people; a direct verse for the maker's line about gated ontology frameworks.
- 2:160 "unless they repent, make amends, and declare the truth". the repair for concealment is three verbs: repent, make amends, declare. publishing is the third.
- 2:164 "in the ships that sail the seas with goods for people; in the water which God sends down from the sky to give life to the earth ... there are signs in all these for those who use their minds". trade ships and rain are listed together as signs for people who think.
- 2:170 "they answer, ‘We follow the ways of our fathers.’ What! Even though their fathers understood nothing and were not guided". the verse against inherited defaults; the question mark after "What!" is the manifesto's tone.
- 2:174 "those who conceal the Scripture that God sent down and sell it for a small price, they only fill their bellies with Fire". selling concealed knowledge for a small price is described as eating fire.
- 2:239 "remember God, for He has taught you what you did not know". learning is framed as a gift to be remembered, not a possession.
- 2:247 "‘God has chosen him over you, and has given him great knowledge and stature. God grants His authority to whoever He pleases’". the objection to Talut was "He does not even have great wealth"; the answer is knowledge and stature. leadership is not bought.
- 2:255 "they do not comprehend any of His knowledge except what He wills". the ceiling on human knowing is stated in the most famous verse of the chunk.
- 2:269 "Whoever is given wisdom has truly been given much good, but only those with insight bear this in mind". wisdom is called "much good" in a passage about money; it is a form of wealth.
- 2:282 "let him write as God has taught him ... Be mindful of God, and He will teach you: He has full knowledge of everything". writing is a taught skill and teaching continues; the scribe writes with borrowed knowledge.
- 3:7 "Those firmly grounded in knowledge say, ‘We believe in it: it is all from our Lord’- only those with real perception will take heed". the rank of those who know is defined by their restraint before ambiguity, not by their cleverness with it.
- 3:18 "God bears witness that there is no god but Him, as do the angels and those who have knowledge. He upholds justice." those who know are placed as witnesses beside the angels, and the same breath names justice; knowledge and justice share one verse.
- 3:66 "You argue about some things of which you have some knowledge, but why do you argue about things of which you know nothing? God knows and you do not". the boundary of honest argument is the boundary of your knowledge.
- 3:79 "‘You should be devoted to God because you have taught the Scripture and studied it closely.’" teaching and close study are given as reasons for devotion, not substitutes for it.
- 3:118 "We have made Our revelations clear for you; will you not use your reason". the question a manifesto can ask its reader.
- 3:137 "travel through the land, and see what was the end of those who disbelieved". go and look; the text sends the reader out to check the record for themselves.
- 3:187 "God took a pledge from those who were given the Scripture- ‘Make it known to people; do not conceal it’- but they tossed the pledge over their shoulders, they bartered it for a small price: what a bad bargain they made". the strongest anti-gatekeeping verse in the chunk: to be given knowledge is to be under a pledge to publish it.
- 3:190-191 "There truly are signs in the creation of the heavens and earth, and in the alternation of night and day, for those with understanding who remember God standing, sitting, and lying down, who reflect on the creation". reflection is posture-independent; it happens standing, sitting and lying down.

## 2. proof and truth

- 2:23 "then produce a single sura like it- enlist whatever supporters you have other than God- if you truly [think you can]". the text opens its own claim to a challenge; it invites the test.
- 2:42 "Do not mix truth with falsehood, or hide the truth when you know it". two sins in one line: adulteration and concealment. both are the work of curated narratives.
- 2:72 "although God was to bring what you had concealed to light". the murder in the cow story is solved by disclosure; concealment is temporary.
- 2:80 "‘Have you received a promise from God- for God never breaks His promise- or are you saying things about Him of which you have no real knowledge?’" the two options are proof or no real knowledge; there is no third.
- 2:111 "‘Produce your evidence, if you are telling the truth.’" the plainest burhan verse; four words the workshop can put on a wall.
- 2:143 "We have made you [believers] into a just community, so that you may bear witness [to the truth] before others". a community exists in order to witness; publishing is the community's job description.
- 2:169 "to say things about God that you do not really know". saying more than you know is listed as satan's command.
- 2:181 "If anyone alters the bequest after hearing it, the guilt of the alteration will fall on them: God is all hearing and all knowing". altering a record after it is heard is a named guilt; immutable records have a verse.
- 2:204 "he even calls on God to witness what is in his heart, yet he is the bitterest of opponents". calling a witness is not the same as being truthful; a signature can lie.
- 2:258 "‘God brings the sun from the east; so bring it from the west.’ The disbeliever was dumbfounded". abraham wins by a testable claim that the other side cannot meet.
- 2:260 "‘Yes,’ said Abraham, ‘but just to put my heart at rest.’ So God said, ‘Take four birds and train them to come back to you.’" even the believing heart may ask to see; the request is granted with an experiment, not a rebuke.
- 2:282 "You who believe, when you contract a debt for a stated term, put it down in writing: have a scribe write it down justly between you. No scribe should refuse to write". the debt is written by a third party, justly, and the writer may not refuse; the record is a duty owed to the deal, not a favour.
- 2:282 "Call in two men as witnesses ... Let the witnesses not refuse when they are summoned". witnesses are summoned and may not refuse; witnessing is an obligation, not a courtesy.
- 2:282 "Do not disdain to write the debt down, be it small or large, along with the time it falls due: this way is more equitable in God’s eyes, more reliable as testimony, and more likely to prevent doubts arising between you". three reasons for a written record with a timestamp: fairness, reliability, and fewer doubts later. this is the tool's approve verb with its reasons attached.
- 2:282 "let no harm be done to either scribe or witness, for if you did cause them harm, it would be a crime on your part". the recorder and the witness are protected persons; harming them is a crime. a verse for protecting whoever keeps the log.
- 2:283 "Do not conceal evidence: anyone who does so has a sinful heart, and God is fully aware of everything you do". concealing evidence is a heart condition, not an administrative lapse.
- 3:53 "record us among those who bear witness [to the Truth]". the disciples ask to be written down as witnesses; being on the record is a request, not a burden.
- 3:61 "let us gather our sons and your sons, our women and your women, ourselves and yourselves, and let us pray earnestly and invoke God’s rejection on those of us who are lying". the dispute ends with both sides staking everything on who is lying; a claim you will not stake your family on is not a claim.
- 3:71 "why do you mix truth with falsehood? Why do you hide the truth when you recognize it". 2:42 turned into a question addressed to people who know better.
- 3:78 "There are some who twist the Scripture with their tongues to make you [people] think that what they say is part of the Scripture when it is not". the technique of the altered narrative is described exactly: make the reader think the addition is part of the source.
- 3:93 "‘Bring the Torah and read out [the relevant passage] if you are telling the truth’". cite the source; read the passage aloud.
- 3:167 "They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts: God knows exactly what they conceal". the tongue and the heart are checked against each other.
- 3:181 "We shall record everything they say". the record is kept even of sneers.
- 3:188 "those who exult in what they have done and seek praise for things they have not done". claiming unearned credit is a separate sin from doing nothing.

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship (khalifa), custody

- 2:30 "‘I am putting a successor on earth,’ they said, ‘How can You put someone there who will cause damage and bloodshed’". the steward is appointed with full knowledge that he will break things; stewardship was never conditional on being safe.
- 2:30 "‘I know things you do not.’" the reply to the angels' risk assessment; the trust is placed anyway.
- 2:40 "Honour your pledge to Me and I will honour My pledge to you". the pledge is two-sided; trust is a relationship, not a permission.
- 2:124 "‘My pledge does not hold for those who do evil.’" abraham asks whether leadership passes to his descendants; the answer is that the pledge does not pass through blood to wrongdoers. authority is not inherited.
- 2:177 "who keep pledges whenever they make them; who are steadfast in misfortune, adversity, and times of danger". keeping pledges is in the definition of goodness, next to giving and steadfastness.
- 2:220 "‘It is good to set things right for them. If you combine their affairs with yours, remember they are your brothers and sisters: God knows those who spoil things and those who improve them’". orphans' property is the archetype of custody of what is not yours; the test is whether you spoil or improve it.
- 2:283 "if you decide to trust one another, then let the one who is trusted fulfil his trust; let him be mindful of God, his Lord". when no written record is possible, trust is allowed, and the one trusted carries the whole weight.
- 3:37 "entrusted her to the charge of Zachariah". mary is placed in custody; the guardian's job is to let her grow, and he finds her provisioned without him.
- 3:75 "There are People of the Book who, if you [Prophet] entrust them with a heap of gold, will return it to you intact, but there are others of them who, if you entrust them with a single dinar, will not return it to you unless you keep standing over them". the test of trust is returning what was entrusted without being watched; a tool that returns the owner's data intact passes it.
- 3:76 "God loves those who keep their pledges and are mindful of Him". keeping pledges is loved, not merely required.
- 3:161 "It is inconceivable that a prophet would ever dishonestly take something from the battle gains. Anyone who does so will carry it with him on the Day of Resurrection". skimming from shared gains is unthinkable in a leader; what is taken is carried.
- 3:180 "It is God who will inherit the heavens and earth". everything held is held on loan; the final owner is named.

## 4. craft and excellence

- 2:110 "Whatever good you store up for yourselves, you will find it with God: He sees everything you do". the cousin of 9:105 in this chunk; work is seen.
- 2:127 "As Abraham and Ishmael built up the foundations of the House [they prayed], ‘Our Lord, accept [this] from us. You are the All Hearing, the All Knowing’". the only building scene in the chunk: father and son laying foundations and praying while they lay them. "we build in worship" has its picture here.
- 2:148 "race to do good deeds and wherever you are, God will bring you together". good work is a race, and the race is what gathers people who started in different directions.
- 2:195 "do good, for God loves those who do good". the shortest form of ihsan in the chunk.
- 2:197 "whatever good you do, God is well aware of it. Provide well for yourselves". provisioning yourself well is placed beside doing good.
- 2:238 "Take care to do your prayers, praying in the best way". even prayer has a best way; excellence applies to worship itself.
- 2:267 "give charitably from the good things you have acquired ... Do not give away the bad things that you yourself would only accept with your eyes closed". a quality bar for anything released: would you accept it with your eyes open. the test for every published tool.
- 3:49 "I will make the shape of a bird for you out of clay, then breathe into it and, with God’s permission, it will become a real bird’". shaping clay with the hands is the sign; craft is how the sign is shown.
- 3:114 "who order what is right and forbid what is wrong, who are quick to do good deeds". speed in good work is praised; "move fast" has a verse.
- 3:134 "who give, both in prosperity and adversity, who restrain their anger and pardon people- God loves those who do good". the definition of those who do good is giving in both states, holding anger, and pardoning.
- 3:136 "How excellent is the reward of those who labour". labour is the word; the reward is for workers.
- 3:195 "‘I will not allow the deeds of any one of you to be lost, whether you are male or female, each is like the other [in rewards]’". no work is lost, and the maker's sex does not change its weight.

## 5. justice and balance

- 2:178 "But if the culprit is pardoned by his aggrieved brother, this shall be adhered to fairly ... This is an alleviation from your Lord and an act of mercy. If anyone then exceeds these limits, grievous suffering awaits him". retribution is bounded and pardon is built in; exceeding the limit is the crime.
- 2:188 "Do not consume your property wrongfully, nor use it to bribe judges, intending sinfully and knowingly to consume parts of other people’s property". consuming others' wealth through bribed judges; the rigged system named.
- 2:212 "The life of this world is made to seem glamorous to the disbelievers, and they laugh at those who believe". the glamour is manufactured and the mockery comes with it.
- 2:247 "‘How can he be king over us when we have a greater right to rule than he? He does not even have great wealth.’" the assumption that rule follows wealth, stated by those who hold it, then overruled.
- 2:275 "That is because they say, ‘Trade and usury are the same,’" / 2:276 "but God has allowed trade and forbidden usury ... God blights usury, but blesses charitable deeds with multiple increase". the difference between honest exchange and extraction is the difference the text insists on; systems "that want to keep us paying for them" are the usury side.
- 2:279 "You shall have your capital if you repent, and without suffering loss or causing others to suffer loss". the fair settlement is your principal back and nobody harmed either way.
- 2:280 "If the debtor is in difficulty, then delay things until matters become easier for him; still, if you were to write it off as an act of charity, that would be better for you, if only you knew". delay is required and forgiveness is better; the creditor's best move is to write it off.
- 2:281 "every soul will be paid in full for what it has earned, and no one will be wronged". the ledger closes without error.
- 2:282 "this way is more equitable in God’s eyes". the written record is called more equitable; fairness is a property of documentation.
- 3:18 "He upholds justice". justice sits in the creed itself.
- 3:21 "who unjustifiably kill prophets, who kill those who command that justice is done". those who call for justice are listed beside prophets as targets.
- 3:108 "God does not will injustice for His creatures". a sentence for the manifesto's floor.
- 3:130 "do not consume usurious interest, doubled and redoubled". compounding extraction named by its shape.
- 3:180 "Whatever they meanly withhold will be hung around their necks on the Day of Resurrection". hoarding is not neutral; what is withheld becomes a weight on the hoarder.
- 3:181 "‘So God is poor, while we are rich’". the sneer of the wealthy is recorded verbatim.

## 6. agency and self-change

- 2:9 "They seek to deceive God and the believers but they only deceive themselves, though they do not realize it". deception rebounds on the deceiver.
- 2:48 "no soul will stand in place of another, no intercession will be accepted for it, nor any ransom". no proxy on the day of accounting.
- 2:57 "It was not Us they wronged; they wronged themselves". harm is self-inflicted first.
- 2:134 "That community passed away. What they earned belongs to them, and what you earn belongs to you: you will not be answerable for their deeds". repeated word for word at 2:141. inherited belief and inherited guilt are both cut; each generation earns its own.
- 2:139 "Our deeds belong to us, and yours to you". the separation of accounts.
- 2:158 "Anyone who does good of his own accord will be rewarded". initiative beyond the required is rewarded; the phrase repeats at 2:184.
- 2:166-167 "When those who have been followed disown their followers ... ‘If only we had one last chance, we would disown them as they now disown us.’" following someone does not transfer your responsibility; the leaders disown you in the end.
- 2:195 "do not contribute to your destruction with your own hands". self-harm by inaction is named; the maker's "rebel against myself" has a verse.
- 2:202 "They will have the share they have worked for". the share is proportional to the work.
- 2:272 "Whatever charity you give benefits your own soul". giving is self-change first.
- 2:286 "God does not burden any soul with more than it can bear: each gains whatever good it has done, and suffers its bad". the load is sized to the soul, and the account is personal.
- 3:117 "It was not God who wronged them; they wronged themselves". repeated diagnosis.
- 3:145 "If anyone strives for the rewards of this world, We will give him some of them. If anyone strives for the rewards of the Hereafter, We will give him some of them". striving is answered on both tracks; what you strive for shapes what you get.
- 3:165 "[Prophet], say, ‘You brought it upon yourselves.’" after a defeat the first cause named is your own conduct.
- 3:182 "That is on account of what you stored up for yourselves with your own hands". hands and earning again; agency runs through the whole chunk.

## 7. time and mortality

- 2:36 "On earth you will have a place to stay and livelihood for a time." the whole human condition in one clause: a place, a livelihood, and a term.
- 2:96 "Any of them would wish to be given a life of a thousand years, though even such a long life would not save them from the torment". length of life is not safety.
- 2:126 "I will grant them enjoyment for a short while’". even enjoyment is time-boxed.
- 2:189 "They ask you [Prophet] about crescent moons. Say, ‘They show the times appointed for people’". the moon is a calendar; time is given so that people can keep appointments.
- 2:254 "give from what We have provided for you, before the Day comes when there is no bargaining, no friendship, and no intercession". act before the window closes; the deadline is the reason to give now.
- 2:259 "‘How long did you stay like that?’ He answered, ‘A day, or part of a day.’ God said, ‘No, you stayed like that for a hundred years.’" the sleeper cannot tell a day from a century; subjective time is unreliable.
- 3:133 "Hurry towards your Lord’s forgiveness". urgency as an imperative verb.
- 3:145 "No soul may die except with God’s permission at a predestined time". the hour is fixed and unknown; the maker's "the exact amount unknown only to God" is here.
- 3:178 "when We give them more time they become more sinful". extra time is not a gift by itself; what is done with it decides.
- 3:185 "Every soul will taste death and you will be paid in full only on the Day of Resurrection ... The present world is only an illusory pleasure". the finite span stated plainly, with payment deferred to the end.
- 3:196-197 "do not be deceived by the disbelievers’ [lucrative] trading to and fro in the land this is only a brief enjoyment". busy profitable movement is not the same as lasting work.
- 3:102 "make sure you devote yourselves to Him, to your dying moment". the term of devotion is the whole span, no retirement.

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- 2:11-12 "When it is said to them, ‘Do not cause corruption in the land,’ they say, ‘We are only putting things right,’ but really they are causing corruption, though they do not realize it". the false reformer speaks the reformer's own words; the manifesto must know that its sentence "we repair" is also the sentence of the corrupter.
- 2:13 "‘Should we believe as the fools do?’ but they are the fools, though they do not know it". contempt for ordinary people is the mark of the fool.
- 2:16 "They have bought error in exchange for guidance, so their trade reaps no profit". corruption described as a bad trade.
- 2:27 "who sever the bonds that God has commanded to be joined, who spread corruption on the earth- these are the losers". severing bonds is a form of corruption; isolation is decay.
- 2:34 "But not Iblis, who refused and was arrogant". arrogance is the first sin recorded.
- 2:60 "‘Eat and drink the sustenance God has provided and do not cause corruption in the land.’" abundance comes with a single condition.
- 2:74 "your hearts became as hard as rocks, or even harder, for there are rocks from which streams spring out". rocks can still give water; a hardened heart is worse than stone.
- 2:87 "whenever a messenger brings you something you do not like, you become arrogant, calling some impostors and killing others". the pattern of the incumbent: dismiss, then destroy.
- 2:102 "They learned what harmed them, not what benefited them, knowing full well that whoever gained [this knowledge] would lose any share in the Hereafter". knowledge is not neutral; there is a knowledge that harms its holder, chosen knowingly. this speaks to ai used for slop and control.
- 2:171 "Calling to disbelieversis like a herdsman calling to things that hear nothing but a shout and a cry: they are deaf, dumb, and blind". the closed system that hears only noise.
- 2:205 "he sets out to spread corruption in the land, destroying crops and live-stock- God does not like corruption". corruption is measured in destroyed crops and livestock, real things that feed people.
- 2:206 "his arrogance leads him to sin. Hell is enough for him". arrogance again as the driver.
- 2:220 "God knows those who spoil things and those who improve them". the two verbs of the workshop's whole argument: spoil or improve.
- 2:251 "If God did not drive some back by means of others the earth would be completely corrupt, but God is bountiful to all". pushing back is the mechanism that keeps the earth from full corruption; "push back against harmful defaults" is described here as a law of how the world is kept.
- 3:7 "The perverse at heart eagerly pursue the ambiguities in their attempt to make trouble and to pin down a specific meaning of their own". manufacturing trouble from ambiguity is a named corruption.
- 3:11 "just as Pharaoh’s people and their predecessors denied Our revelations". pharaoh appears as the type of the denier.
- 3:63 "God is well aware of anyone who causes corruption". the sentence stands alone at the end of an argument.
- 3:99 "why do you turn the believers away from God’s path and try to make it crooked". making the path crooked is the work of those who should be witnesses to it.

## 9. community and mutual aid

- 2:62 "all those who believe in God and the Last Day and do good- will have their rewards with their Lord. No fear for them, nor will they grieve". the community of reward is wider than one label; muslims, jews, christians and sabians are listed together.
- 2:83 "be good to your parents and kinsfolk, to orphans and the poor; speak good words to all people". "all people" is the scope of kind speech.
- 2:143 "We have made you [believers] into a just community, so that you may bear witness [to the truth] before others". the community is made for witness.
- 2:148 "Each community has its own direction to which it turns: race to do good deeds and wherever you are, God will bring you together". difference of direction is accepted; what unites is the race to do good.
- 2:213 "Mankind was a single community, then God sent prophets ... It was only those to whom it was given who disagreed about it after clear signs had come to them, because of rivalry between them". the whole started as one; rivalry among the knowledgeable split it.
- 2:233 "If, by mutual consent and consultation, the couple wish to wean [the child], they will not be blamed". consultation appears at the smallest scale, two parents deciding when to wean.
- 2:249 "‘How often a small force has defeated a large army with God’s permission! God is with those who are steadfast.’" the small group's verse.
- 3:52 "‘Who will help me in God’s cause?’ The disciples said, ‘We will be God’s helpers’". the call and the answer; a collective forms by answering.
- 3:64 "‘People of the Book, let us arrive at a statement that is common to us all’". reaching a common statement across difference is offered as method.
- 3:103 "Hold fast to God’s rope all together; do not split into factions. Remember God’s favour to you: you were enemies and then He brought your hearts together and you became brothers by His grace". the rope is held together; former enemies made brothers is the founding memory.
- 3:104 "Be a community that calls for what is good, urges what is right, and forbids what is wrong: those who do this are the successful ones". the community's three verbs.
- 3:112 "unless they hold fast to a lifeline from God and from mankind". a lifeline from people is named beside the one from God; mutual aid is a lifeline.
- 3:113 "But they are not all alike." a correction against lumping a group together.
- 3:159 "By an act of mercy from God, you [Prophet] were gentle in your dealings with them- had you been harsh, or hard-hearted, they would have dispersed and left you- so pardon them and ask forgiveness for them. Consult with them about matters". the cousin of 42:38 in this chunk: consultation, and gentleness as the reason a community stays.

## 10. humility, moderation and warmth

- 2:45 "Seek help with steadfastness and prayer- though this is hard indeed for anyone but the humble". humility makes the hard thing possible.
- 2:58 "enter its gate humbly and say, “Relieve us!”". entering a city with a bowed head.
- 2:109 "Forgive and forbear until God gives his command". the response to envy is forgiveness and patience, not retaliation.
- 2:173 "if anyone is forced to eat such things by hunger, rather than desire or excess, he commits no sin: God is most merciful and forgiving". need is excused; desire and excess are the line.
- 2:185 "God wants ease for you, not hardship". the manifesto's warmth in six words.
- 2:186 "if My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I respond to those who call Me". nearness stated in the first person.
- 2:190 "do not overstep the limits: God does not love those who overstep the limits". even in a fight, limits.
- 2:214 "Truly, God’s help is near". placed right after "‘When will God’s help arrive?’"; the answer to despair.
- 2:219 "‘There is great sin in both, and some benefit for people: the sin is greater than the benefit.’" the text weighs; it admits benefit even in what it forbids. a model for honest tradeoff writing.
- 2:225 "He will not call you to account for oaths you have uttered unintentionally, but He will call you to account for what you mean in your hearts". intention outweighs slips.
- 2:237 "Waiving [your right] is nearer to godliness, so do not forget to be generous towards one another". giving up what you are owed is nearer to God.
- 2:263 "A kind word and forgiveness is better than a charitable deed followed by hurtful [words]". a kind word outranks a gift with strings.
- 2:286 "Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or make mistakes’". the closing prayer of the longest surah asks pardon for forgetting and for error.
- 3:134 "who restrain their anger and pardon people". restraint is listed before pardon; both are required of those who do good.
- 3:139 "Do not lose heart or despair- if you are true believers you have the upper hand". after a defeat, the first instruction is not to despair.
- 3:159 "had you been harsh, or hard-hearted, they would have dispersed and left you". harshness disperses people; gentleness is a structural requirement for any collective.
- 3:199 "humbling themselves before God, they would never sell God’s revelation for a small price". humility and refusal to sell knowledge are paired.

## 11. wonder, play and nature

- 2:22 "who spread out the earth for you and built the sky; who sent water down from it and with that water produced things for your sustenance". earth spread, sky built, water sent; creation described as making.
- 2:26 "God does not shy from drawing comparisons even with something as small as a gnat, or larger". the text is not embarrassed by a small image; a manifesto need not be either.
- 2:74 "there are rocks from which streams spring out, and some from which water comes when they split open, and others which fall down in awe of God". rocks that give water, split, or fall in awe; the stone is more alive than the hard heart.
- 2:138 "‘[Our life] takes its colour from God, and who gives a better colour than God?’". colour as the image of belief; playful and strange.
- 2:164 "in the alternation of night and day; in the ships that sail the seas with goods for people; in the water which God sends down ... in the changing of the winds and clouds that run their appointed courses". a catalogue of signs that includes cargo ships alongside rain and wind.
- 2:189 "They ask you [Prophet] about crescent moons". the moon as a question people ask and a tool for keeping time.
- 2:259 "Look at your food and drink: they have not gone bad. Look at your donkey ... look at the bones: see how We bring them together and clothe them with flesh!’" three "look" commands; the sign is shown, not argued.
- 2:260 "‘Take four birds and train them to come back to you. Then place them on separate hilltops, call them back, and they will come flying to you’". a training experiment with birds, given to a prophet who wanted his heart at rest.
- 2:261 "like grains of corn that produce seven ears, each bearing a hundred grains". compounding shown as a plant.
- 2:265 "like a garden on a hill: heavy rain falls and it produces double its normal yield; even if no heavy rain falls, it will still be watered by the dew". the garden that works in both weathers; resilience without the word.
- 3:6 "it is He who shapes you all in the womb as He pleases". shaping is the verb of creation.
- 3:27 "You merge night into day and day into night; You bring the living out of the dead and the dead out of the living". a gradient, not a switch.
- 3:49 "I will make the shape of a bird for you out of clay, then breathe into it’". play with clay as the first sign of a messenger.
- 3:191 "‘Our Lord! You have not created all this without purpose’". the reflecting person's conclusion: nothing here is pointless.

## 12. building and repairing stories

- 2:30 "‘I am putting a successor on earth’". the appointment of a builder who will also break things.
- 2:60 "‘Strike the rock with your staff.’ Twelve springs gushed out, and each group knew its drinking place". one strike, twelve outlets, each group with its own access point; a picture of distributed infrastructure.
- 2:125 "We made the House a resort and a sanctuary for people ... ‘Purify My House for those who walk round it, those who stay there, and those who bow and prostrate themselves’". a built thing serving three kinds of user: the passer, the stayer, the worshipper.
- 2:127 "As Abraham and Ishmael built up the foundations of the House [they prayed], ‘Our Lord, accept [this] from us’". builders praying over foundations.
- 2:129 "make a messenger of their own rise up from among them, to recite Your revelations to them, teach them the Scripture and wisdom’". the builders ask for a teacher from within the community, not sent from outside.
- 2:182 "if anyone knows that the testator has made a mistake, or done wrong, and so puts things right between the parties, he will incur no sin". repairing a flawed record is permitted and blameless when done knowingly and fairly.
- 2:220 "‘It is good to set things right for them’". the verb of repair applied to orphans' affairs.
- 2:248 "‘The sign of his authority will be that the Ark [of the Covenant] will come to you. In it there will be [the gift of] tranquillity from your Lord and relics’". the ark carries calm and an archive; the legitimate leader is the one who keeps the record.
- 2:249 "Anyone who drinks from it will not belong with me, but anyone who refrains from tasting it will belong with me; if he scoops up just one handful [he will be excused].’" the river test: restraint is the filter, and one handful is allowed. moderation with an escape hatch.
- 2:251 "David killed Goliath, and God gave him sovereignty and wisdom and taught him what He pleased". the builder-king receives wisdom and teaching after the fight, not before.
- 2:259 "Or take the one who passed by a ruined town. He said, ‘How will God give this life when it has died?’". the ruin is the starting point of the story; revival is shown on a body, not a building.
- 3:37 "Whenever Zachariah went in to see her in her sanctuary, he found her supplied with provisions". a guardian who finds his ward already provided for; custody without control.
- 3:49 "I will tell you what you may eat and what you may store up in your houses’". storing is a taught skill; joseph's storehouses have a cousin here.
- 3:96 "The first House [of worship] to be established for people was the one at Mecca. It is a blessed place; a source of guidance for all people". the first built thing is "for people" and "for all people".

## 13. freedom and no compulsion

- 2:6 "As for those who disbelieve, it makes no difference whether you warn them or not: they will not believe". the warner is released from the outcome in the sixth verse of the book.
- 2:38 "when guidance comes from Me, as it certainly will, there will be no fear for those who follow My guidance nor will they grieve". guidance comes; following it is the reader's act.
- 2:119 "You will not be responsible for the inhabitants of the Blaze". responsibility for other people’s choices is explicitly removed.
- 2:148 "Each community has its own direction to which it turns". difference is accepted as given.
- 2:256 "There is no compulsion in religion: true guidance has become distinct from error, so whoever rejects false gods and believes in God has grasped the firmest hand-hold, one that will never break". the reason no compulsion is needed is that the difference is already distinct; clarity replaces force. a manifesto that makes the choice clear has done its whole job.
- 2:257 "He brings them out of the depths of darkness and into the light. As for the disbelievers, their allies are false gods who take them from the light into the depths of darkness". allies pull in one direction or the other; choose your allies.
- 2:272 "It is not for you [Prophet] to guide them; it is God who guides whoever He will". even the messenger does not own the outcome.
- 3:20 "if they turn away, your only duty is to convey the message". the whole duty is to convey; the rest is not yours.
- 3:79 "No person to whom God had given the Scripture, wisdom, and prophethood would ever say to people, ‘Be my servants, not God’s.’" no true teacher asks for followers of himself; a warning against building a cult around a maker.
- 3:83 "Everyone in the heavens and earth submits to Him, willingly or unwillingly". the two modes are named; the text prefers willing.
- 3:128 "Whether God relents towards them or punishes them is not for you [Prophet] to decide". judgement of others is withheld from the messenger.

## 14. clarity and plain speech

- 2:2 "This is the Scripture in which there is no doubt". the book opens by claiming clarity.
- 2:68-71 "‘Call on your Lord for us, to show us what sort of cow it should be.’ ... all cows are more or less alike to us’". the cow story: each request for more specification is a way of not doing the thing. over-specification as avoidance.
- 2:99 "We have sent down clear messages to you and only those who defy [God] would refuse to believe them". clear messages; refusal is defiance, not confusion.
- 2:104 "do not say [to the Prophet], ‘Raina,’ but say, ‘Unzurna,’ and listen". a word that can be twisted is replaced with one that cannot; choose words that cannot be misheard.
- 2:185 "clear messages giving guidance and distinguishing between right and wrong". clarity and criterion in one line.
- 2:187 "In this way God makes His messages clear to people, that they may guard themselves against doing wrong". clarity has a purpose: so that people can act.
- 2:219 "In this way, God makes His messages clear to you, so that you may reflect". the same refrain with a different purpose: reflection.
- 2:230 "These are God’s bounds, which He makes clear for those who know". bounds are made clear, not hidden; a system's limits should be readable.
- 2:242 "In this way God makes His revelations clear to you, so that you may grow in understanding". the third purpose: growth.
- 2:266 "In this way God makes His messages clear to you, so that you may reflect on them". the parable is followed by the explanation of why it was told.
- 3:3 "Step by step, He has sent the Scripture down to you". delivery in steps; radical clarity is incremental.
- 3:7 "Some of its verses are definite in meaning- these are the cornerstone of the Scripture- and others are ambiguous". the text tells the reader which parts are load-bearing; a manifesto could mark its own cornerstones.
- 3:58 "We relate to you [Muhammad] this revelation, a decisive statement". decisive statement as a name for the form.
- 3:64 "let us arrive at a statement that is common to us all’". a shared plain sentence as the goal of dialogue.
- 3:138 "This is a clear lesson to people, and guidance and teaching for those who are mindful of God". clear lesson, guidance, teaching: three levels of the same text.
- 3:184 "even though they came with clear evidence, books of wisdom and enlightening scripture". three kinds of clear thing a messenger brings.

## 15. livelihood and trade

- 2:16 "They have bought error in exchange for guidance, so their trade reaps no profit". the first trade image in the book is a bad one.
- 2:41 "do not sell My messages for a small price". the recurring phrase "small price" marks the sale of knowledge as always underpriced.
- 2:164 "in the ships that sail the seas with goods for people". commerce is listed as a sign.
- 2:168 "People, eat what is good and lawful from the earth". livelihood from the earth is addressed to all people, not only believers.
- 2:198 "but it is no offence to seek some bounty from your Lord". seeking bounty during pilgrimage is allowed; making a living is not a compromise of worship. the maker's "business in a very loose way" has its permission here.
- 2:245 "Who will give God a good loan, which He will increase for him many times over?" giving described as a loan with return.
- 2:261 "Those who spend their wealth in God’s cause are like grains of corn that produce seven ears, each bearing a hundred grains". compounding of what is given away.
- 2:262 "Those who spend their wealth in God’s cause, and do not follow their spending with reminders of their benevolence or hurtful words, will have their rewards with their Lord". no reminders; the gift ends when it is given.
- 2:264 "do not cancel out your charitable deeds with reminders and hurtful words, like someone who spends his wealth only to be seen by people ... like a rock with earth on it: heavy rain falls and leaves it completely bare". a reminder cancels the gift; the performative giver is bare rock after rain. a release that comes with a demand for credit is cancelled.
- 2:265 "those who spend their wealth in order to gain God’s approval, and as an affirmation of their own faith, are like a garden on a hill". giving as self-affirmation, not display.
- 2:267 "give charitably from the good things you have acquired and that We have produced for you from the earth". give the good things; do not open-source your leftovers.
- 2:271 "if you keep it secret and give to the needy in private, that is better for you". secret giving is better; anonymous release has a verse.
- 2:273 "The unknowing might think them rich because of their self-restraint, but you will recognize them by their characteristic of not begging persistently". the deserving recipient is the one who does not beg; funding goes to the quiet ones, and the text asks the giver to learn to see them.
- 2:274 "Those who give, out of their own possessions, by night and by day, in private and in public, will have their reward". all modes of giving are counted.
- 2:276 "God has allowed trade and forbidden usury". the single line that separates honest business from extraction.
- 2:282 "Have witnesses present whenever you trade with one another". witnesses are for ordinary trade, not only for debt.
- 2:283 "something should be handed over as security, but if you decide to trust one another, then let the one who is trusted fulfil his trust". security or trust; both are honest ways to trade.
- 3:14 "gold and silver treasures piled up high, horses with fine markings, livestock, and farmland- these may be the joys of this life". the catalogue of what is made alluring; a description of what the attention economy sells.
- 3:92 "None of you [believers] will attain true piety unless you give out of what you cherish: whatever you give, God knows about it very well". giving what you cherish, not what you can spare. the workshop's best work is what it gives away.
- 3:134 "who give, both in prosperity and adversity". giving is not conditional on having a surplus.
- 3:180 "Those who are miserly with what God has granted them out of His grace should not think that it is good for them; on the contrary, it is bad for them". hoarding a grant is bad for the hoarder.

## 16. warnings for the manifesto's own posture

- 2:11-12 "‘We are only putting things right,’ but really they are causing corruption, though they do not realize it". the reformer who does not realise it; the manifesto should say this about itself somewhere.
- 2:13 "‘Should we believe as the fools do?’ but they are the fools, though they do not know it". contempt for "ordinary people" is the mark of the corrupter; a manifesto for ordinary people must not look down on them.
- 2:44 "How can you tell people to do what is right and forget to do it yourselves, even though you recite the Scripture? Have you no sense". the ancestor of 61:2-3, addressed to people who recite. saying without doing, with the book in hand.
- 2:85 "So do you believe in some parts of the Scripture and not in others?" selective adoption of principles; the manifesto cannot pick only the convenient verses.
- 2:94 "‘If the last home with God is to be for you alone and no one else, then you should long for death, if your claim is true.’" a claim is tested by whether you act as if it were true.
- 2:113 "The Jews say, ‘The Christians have no ground whatsoever to stand on,’ and the Christians say, ‘The Jews have no ground whatsoever to stand on,’ though they both read the Scripture". sects reading the same source and denying each other's ground.
- 2:176 "those who pursue differences in the Scripture are deeply entrenched in opposition". pursuing differences for their own sake is a trap.
- 2:190 "Fight in God’s cause against those who fight you, but do not overstep the limits: God does not love those who overstep the limits". rebellion has limits; overstepping them is not loved.
- 2:194 "So if anyone commits aggression against you, attack him as he attacked you, but be mindful of God". proportion; not more than what was done to you.
- 2:204-205 "There is [a kind of] man whose views on the life of this world may please you [Prophet], he even calls on God to witness what is in his heart, yet he is the bitterest of opponents / When he leaves, he sets out to spread corruption". the persuasive speaker who invokes God and then destroys; charisma is not a credential.
- 2:213 "It was only those to whom it was given who disagreed about it after clear signs had come to them, because of rivalry between them". those who received the knowledge are the ones who split it, out of rivalry. the warning is for the knowledgeable.
- 2:229 "These are the bounds set by God: do not overstep them". bounds again; a maker who breaks defaults must know which lines are not defaults.
- 2:231 "Do not hold on to them with intent to harm them and commit aggression: anyone who does this wrongs himself. Do not make a mockery of God’s revelations". holding on in order to harm is aggression; mockery of the sacred is named beside it.
- 2:264 "like someone who spends his wealth only to be seen by people". release for visibility cancels the release.
- 3:7 "The perverse at heart eagerly pursue the ambiguities in their attempt to make trouble and to pin down a specific meaning of their own: only God knows the true meaning". a direct warning to anyone "deriving my own" from the text: the ambiguous parts are not for pinning down; stand on the cornerstones.
- 3:19 "Those who were given the Scripture disagreed out of rivalry, only after they had been given knowledge". knowledge came first, then rivalry; the order is the warning.
- 3:105 "Do not be like those who, after they have been given clear revelation, split into factions and fall into disputes". sect-making after clarity.
- 3:152 "you faltered, disputed the order, and disobeyed, once He had brought you within sight of your goal- some of you desire the gains of this world". the failure at uhud came within sight of the goal, from wanting the spoils; a workshop that starts winning is at its most dangerous.
- 3:154 "‘Do we get a say in any of this?’ [Prophet], tell them, ‘Everything to do with this affair is in God’s hands.’" the complaint of those who wanted control over outcomes; agency is over your own conduct, not over results.
- 3:161 "It is inconceivable that a prophet would ever dishonestly take something from the battle gains". skimming from shared gains.
- 3:167 "They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts". the tongue outrunning the heart.
- 3:188 "Do not think [Prophet] that those who exult in what they have done and seek praise for things they have not done will escape the torment". exulting and seeking praise for undone things; the two failures of a loud manifesto.
- 3:135 "and who never knowingly persist in doing wrong". the definition of the good includes not persisting once you know.

## images and parables

- the fire that lights and is taken away: 2:17 "They are like people who [labour to] kindle a fire: when it lights up everything around them, God takes away all their light, leaving them in utter darkness". effort that produces light and then loses it; the hype cycle in one image.
- fingers in the ears: 2:19 "put their fingers into their ears to keep out the thunderclaps for fear of death". refusing to hear the warning while standing in the storm.
- the gnat: 2:26 "even with something as small as a gnat". permission to use small, odd images.
- the sealed heart: 2:7 "God has sealed their hearts and their ears, and their eyes are covered". the closed interface.
- rocks with streams: 2:74 "there are rocks from which streams spring out, and some from which water comes when they split open". stone that gives water.
- twelve springs: 2:60 "Twelve springs gushed out, and each group knew its drinking place". one source, many outlets, each community knowing its own.
- the book thrown over the shoulder: 2:101 "threw the Book of God over their shoulders as if they had no knowledge" and 3:187 "they tossed the pledge over their shoulders". the gesture of discarding what you were given to keep.
- colour: 2:138 "‘[Our life] takes its colour from God’". a dye rather than a badge.
- the herdsman and the herd: 2:171 "like a herdsman calling to things that hear nothing but a shout and a cry". broadcasting to what cannot listen.
- white thread and black thread: 2:187 "until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct from the black". the moment of distinction as a measure of time.
- the back door: 2:189 "Goodness does not consist of entering houses by the back [door] ... So enter your houses by their [main] doors". the front-door principle: do things in the open.
- the river test: 2:249 "‘God will test you with a river. Anyone who drinks from it will not belong with me, but anyone who refrains from tasting it will belong with me; if he scoops up just one handful [he will be excused].’" the discipline of the small group, with one handful allowed.
- the small force: 2:249 "‘How often a small force has defeated a large army with God’s permission!’".
- the ark: 2:248 "In it there will be [the gift of] tranquillity from your Lord and relics". the box that holds calm and memory.
- the firmest hand-hold: 2:256 "has grasped the firmest hand-hold, one that will never break".
- out of darkness into light: 2:257 "He brings them out of the depths of darkness and into the light".
- sunrise from the west: 2:258 "‘God brings the sun from the east; so bring it from the west.’" a proof no power can fake.
- the hundred-year sleep: 2:259 "Look at your food and drink: they have not gone bad. Look at your donkey’". the preserved provisions beside the bones.
- the four birds: 2:260 "‘Take four birds and train them to come back to you. Then place them on separate hilltops, call them back’". trained return.
- seven ears, a hundred grains: 2:261.
- bare rock after rain: 2:264 "like a rock with earth on it: heavy rain falls and leaves it completely bare". the performative gift washed off.
- the garden on the hill, watered by dew: 2:265 "even if no heavy rain falls, it will still be watered by the dew". the work that yields in lean weather.
- the garden burnt in old age: 2:266 "when you are afflicted with old age and feeble offspring, is struck by a fiery whirlwind and burnt down". what you built, lost when you can no longer rebuild.
- the scribe and the two witnesses: 2:282. a record, a writer who may not refuse, witnesses who may not refuse, and a due date.
- the cornerstone: 3:7 "these are the cornerstone of the Scripture". the load-bearing verses.
- the bird of clay: 3:49.
- the rope and the pit: 3:103 "Hold fast to God’s rope all together ... you were about to fall into a pit of Fire and He saved you from it".
- the frosty wind: 3:117 "a frosty wind strikes and destroys the harvest of people who have wronged themselves".
- the lifeline: 3:112 "a lifeline from God and from mankind".
- hung around their necks: 3:180 "Whatever they meanly withhold will be hung around their necks". the hoard as a collar.
- night merging into day: 3:27 "You merge night into day and day into night".
- the pen and the hand: 2:79 "Woe to them for what their hands have written!" the pen is present as the hand that forges.

## how the text itself speaks

- the opening surah is seven lines in the first person plural: 1:5 "It is You we worship; it is You we ask for help" and 1:6 "Guide us to the straight path". the book begins as a prayer spoken by a "we"; a manifesto that opens in "we" is on old ground.
- unexplained letters: 2:1 "Alif Lam Mim" and 3:1 "Alif Lam Mim". the text begins with a mark it never explains; strangeness is allowed at the door.
- direct address by group: "You who believe" (2:153, 2:172, 2:178, 2:183, 2:208, 2:254, 2:264, 2:267, 2:278, 2:282, 3:100, 3:102, 3:118, 3:130, 3:149, 3:156, 3:200); "People" (2:21, 2:168); "Children of Israel" (2:40, 2:47, 2:122); "People of the Book" (3:64, 3:65, 3:70, 3:71, 3:98, 3:99). the reader is always named before being told anything.
- the "Remember when" chain: from 2:49 "Remember when We saved you from Pharaoh’s people" through 2:93, a run of shared memory addressed to a people. a manifesto can recite the collective's own history back to it.
- questions to the reader: 2:44 "Have you no sense"; 2:28 "How can you ignore God when you were lifeless and He gave you life"; 2:140 "‘Who knows better: you or God?’"; 3:65 "Do you not understand"; 3:118 "will you not use your reason"; 2:214 "Do you suppose that you will enter the Garden without first having suffered like those before you?"; 3:142 "Did you think you would enter the Garden without God first proving which of you would struggle".
- the "They ask you ... Say" pattern: 2:189, 2:215, 2:217, 2:219, 2:220, 2:222. a question-and-answer form, where the community's actual questions are quoted and answered in order. a manifesto could carry a faq in this shape.
- the "Say" imperative: 2:80, 2:91, 2:94, 2:97, 2:111, 2:135, 2:139, 3:12, 3:15, 3:20, 3:26, 3:29, 3:31, 3:32, 3:64, 3:84, 3:95, 3:98, 3:99. the speaker is handed lines to say; the text scripts its own spokesman.
- refrains: "no fear for them, nor will they grieve" (2:38, 2:62, 2:112, 2:262, 2:274, 2:277, 3:170); "In this way God makes His messages clear" (2:187, 2:219, 2:221, 2:242, 2:266, 3:103); "though they do not realize it" (2:9, 2:12, 3:69); "if only they knew" / "if only you knew" (2:102, 2:103, 2:184, 2:280); "God sees everything you do" and "God is not unaware of what you do" closing many verses (2:74, 2:85, 2:110, 2:144, 2:149, 2:233, 2:237, 2:265, 3:156, 3:163). the refrain is how the text keeps its promises in the reader's ear.
- "small price": 2:41, 2:79, 2:174, 3:77, 3:187, 3:199. the same two words for every sale of truth.
- "consider": 2:243 "[Prophet], consider those people who abandoned their homeland", 2:246 "consider the leaders of the Children of Israel", 2:258 "have you not thought about the man who disputed with Abraham". the text presents cases and asks the reader to think about them; case studies.
- parables marked with "like": 2:17, 2:19, 2:171, 2:261, 2:264, 2:265, 2:266 (with "Would any of you like to have a garden"), 3:117. each parable is followed by its purpose ("so that you may reflect").
- promise paired with warning: 2:24 "beware of the Fire" then 2:25 "give those who believe and do good the news that they will have Gardens"; 2:81 then 2:82; 3:14 then 3:15 "‘Would you like me to tell you of things that are better than all of these?’". the pairing is systematic; the text never warns without promising in the next breath.
- the numbered list of duties: 2:83 "‘Worship none but God; be good to your parents and kinsfolk, to orphans and the poor; speak good words to all people; keep up the prayer and pay the prescribed alms.’"; 2:177 defines goodness as a list running from belief through giving, prayer, alms, pledges and steadfastness; 3:17 "those who are steadfast, truthful, truly devout, who give [in God’s cause] and pray before dawn"; 3:134-135. a manifesto's list of commitments has this form.
- two kinds of person set side by side: 2:204-207 "There is [a kind of] man ... But there is also a kind of man who gives his life away to please God". contrast by portrait.
- law with mercy clauses: throughout 2:178-2:241, hard rules are followed by "you will not be blamed" (2:234, 2:235, 2:236, 2:240) and "there is no blame" (2:203, 2:229, 2:230, 2:233). rules stated with their exceptions in the same breath.
- the closing prayer: 2:286 "Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or make mistakes’", 3:8-9, 3:191-194 "‘Our Lord!’" four times in a row. surahs end by turning from instruction to petition; a manifesto can end with what its makers ask for rather than what they promise.
- "Step by step" (3:3): the text describes its own delivery as incremental.
- oaths: none in this chunk (the oath form belongs to the shorter meccan surahs in later chunks). noting the absence honestly.

## name and mark candidates

- furqan (criterion): 2:53 "We gave Moses the Scripture, and the means to distinguish [right and wrong]"; 2:185 "clear messages giving guidance and distinguishing between right and wrong"; 3:4 "He has sent down the distinction [between right and wrong]". haleem renders it three ways; the idea is a tool that separates.
- burhan (proof) and its family: 2:111 "‘Produce your evidence, if you are telling the truth.’"; 2:145 "even if you brought every proof"; 2:159 "those who hide the proofs and guidance"; 3:86 "after they have been shown clear proof"; 2:209 "after clear proof has come to you".
- bayan (clear): the refrain "makes His messages clear" (2:187, 2:219, 2:221, 2:242, 2:266); 2:99 "clear messages"; 3:138 "a clear lesson"; 3:58 "a decisive statement"; 2:256 "true guidance has become distinct from error".
- hikma (wisdom): 2:129, 2:151, 2:231 "the Scripture and wisdom He sent to teach you", 2:251 "sovereignty and wisdom", 2:269 "Whoever is given wisdom has truly been given much good", 3:48, 3:79, 3:81, 3:164, 3:184 "books of wisdom".
- nur (light): 2:17 "God takes away all their light"; 2:257 "out of the depths of darkness and into the light". light appears only as something taken or given, never as a possession.
- sabil (way/path): 1:6 "Guide us to the straight path"; 2:108 "strayed far from the right path"; 2:213 "a straight path"; 3:51 "that is a straight path"; 3:99 "turn the believers away from God’s path and try to make it crooked"; 3:101 "guided to the straight path".
- rope, hand-hold, lifeline: 2:256 "the firmest hand-hold, one that will never break"; 3:103 "God’s rope"; 3:112 "a lifeline from God and from mankind". three names for the thing you hold.
- witness (shahid): 2:143 "bear witness [to the truth] before others"; 2:282 "Call in two men as witnesses"; 3:18 "God bears witness ... as do the angels and those who have knowledge"; 3:53 "record us among those who bear witness"; 3:64 "‘Witness our devotion to Him.’"; 3:81 "‘Then bear witness and I too will bear witness.’"
- pen and record: 2:79 "what their hands have written"; 2:282 "have a scribe write it down justly"; 3:181 "We shall record everything they say"; 3:53 "record us".
- read and recite: 2:44 "you recite the Scripture"; 2:129 "to recite Your revelations to them"; 3:93 "read out [the relevant passage]"; 3:113 "recite God’s revelations during the night".
- amana (trust): 2:283 "let the one who is trusted fulfil his trust"; 3:75 "if you [Prophet] entrust them with a heap of gold". no other form in the chunk.
- mizan (balance): the word does not appear in this chunk. its work is done by "fair" and "equitable": 2:178 "fair retribution", 2:282 "more equitable in God’s eyes", 2:228 "according to what is recognized to be fair".
- fitra: does not appear in this chunk. the nearest idea is 2:138 "‘[Our life] takes its colour from God’" (sibghah) and 2:30 "successor".
- sanad (chain): 2:87 "We sent messengers after him in succession"; 3:33-34 "God chose Adam, Noah, Abraham’s family, and the family of Imran ... in one line of descent". succession and descent as chain of transmission.
- time marks: 2:189 "crescent moons ... ‘They show the times appointed for people’"; 2:259 "a hundred years"; 3:27 "You merge night into day and day into night"; 2:187 "the white thread of dawn". no hourglass; the moon and the thread of dawn are the time-pieces.
- other candidate words from this chunk: "cornerstone" (3:7); "successor" (2:30); "the House" (2:125, 3:96, a built sanctuary "for people"); "the Opener" (surah 1's name); "Step by step" (3:3); "twelve springs" (2:60); "the firmest hand-hold" (2:256); "the small force" (2:249); "seven ears" (2:261).

## surprises

- 2:251 "If God did not drive some back by means of others the earth would be completely corrupt". pushing back is not rebellion against the order; it is the order. the maker's whole mission is described as the mechanism by which the earth is kept from total corruption.
- 3:187 "‘Make it known to people; do not conceal it’". being given knowledge creates a pledge to publish it; open publishing is not generosity but the discharge of a debt.
- 2:247 the objection to talut is "He does not even have great wealth" and the answer is "great knowledge and stature". the book says out loud that people assume wealth qualifies for leadership, and overrules it.
- 2:282 protects the scribe and the witness: "let no harm be done to either scribe or witness, for if you did cause them harm, it would be a crime on your part". the recorder is a protected role. a tool that keeps the owner's record stands in the scribe's place.
- 2:282 "No scribe should refuse to write". the writer's refusal is forbidden; a record-keeper that goes silent is in breach.
- 2:181-182 alteration of a bequest is guilt, but repair of a mistaken one is blameless. the text distinguishes tampering from fixing; the difference is knowledge and fairness.
- 2:104 raina/unzurna: a word is replaced because it can be twisted by hostile listeners. the text itself practises defensive wording.
- 2:68-71 the cow: every extra question about the cow's age, colour and condition is a delay, and the closing note is "they slaughtered it, though they almost failed to do so". over-specification is a way of not doing the thing.
- 2:273 the needy who do not beg: "The unknowing might think them rich because of their self-restraint". the text asks givers to learn to see the quiet ones; a funding principle.
- 2:280 "if you were to write it off as an act of charity, that would be better for you". the creditor's best outcome is to forgive the debt.
- 2:102 "They learned what harmed them, not what benefited them, knowing full well". harmful knowledge exists and is chosen; this is the verse for ai used to generate slop and control.
- 2:106 "Any revelation We cause to be superseded or forgotten, We replace with something better or similar". the text describes its own versioning; supersession is not failure.
- 2:138 colour as the image of a way of life: "who gives a better colour than God?". a playful and strange image for identity, nothing to do with badges or labels.
- 2:26 the gnat: the text defends its own right to small comparisons against those who ask "‘What does God mean by such a comparison?’". the objection to a weird image is itself a mark of the rebel.
- 3:7 tells the reader which verses are load-bearing ("cornerstone") and which are not, and warns against pinning private meanings on the ambiguous ones. a maker who derives his own from the core message is exactly the reader 3:7 addresses, both as permission (stand on the cornerstones) and as warning (do not pin the rest).
- 3:159 gives the mechanism of a collective's survival: "had you been harsh, or hard-hearted, they would have dispersed and left you". warmth is not decoration; it is what keeps people in the room.
- 3:154 "‘Do we get a say in any of this?’" is quoted as the complaint of those who wanted control over outcomes; the text separates agency over conduct from control over results.
- 3:195 "whether you are male or female, each is like the other [in rewards]". stated in a war passage, as the answer to a prayer.
- 2:60 twelve springs from one rock, "each group knew its drinking place". one strike, twelve access points, no gatekeeper.
- 2:189 "Goodness does not consist of entering houses by the back [door]". a front-door principle for how a workshop operates.
- 2:36 "On earth you will have a place to stay and livelihood for a time." the terms of the human lease.
- 2:249 the river test allows "just one handful". discipline with a stated tolerance.
- 2:143 the community is made "just" in order to "bear witness". the word haleem gives as "just" (wasat) is the middle; a middle community whose purpose is testimony.
- 3:14 the list of alluring things ends with "farmland", and 3:15 answers "‘Would you like me to tell you of things that are better than all of these?’". the text competes with desire by offering something better, not by scolding.
- 2:213 and 3:19 both say the split came "out of rivalry" among those who had been given the knowledge. gatekeeping and factionalism are diseases of the knowledgeable, not of the ignorant.
- 2:259 the sleeper's food "have not gone bad" while the donkey is bones. what was provided is preserved; what carried him is remade. the image of a long pause after which the work is still good.

## coverage

first verse read: 1:1 "In the name of God, the Lord of Mercy, the Giver of Mercy". last verse read: 3:200 "You who believe, be steadfast, more steadfast than others; be ready; always be mindful of God, so that you may prosper". verses in the chunk: 493 (surah 1: 7, surah 2: 286, surah 3: 200), read in full, every line. stretches where i found little for the manifesto: 2:196 (the detailed pilgrimage compensation rules), 2:226-2:241 (the divorce, waiting-period and widow provisions, except 2:228 "put things right", 2:231, 2:233 consultation, 2:237 waiving), 3:33-3:47 (the birth narratives of mary, john and jesus, except 3:37 custody and 3:44 "beyond your knowledge"), and 3:121-3:127 and 3:140-3:158 (the badr and uhud battle narrative, except 3:152-3:154 and 3:159). the long legal middle of surah 2 and the battle passages of surah 3 are honest gaps; they carry the book's tone of bounds and mercy clauses more than material for the themes.
