# notes 7 of 8: surahs 36 to 47 (ya sin to muhammad), abdel haleem

reader 7. every quote below is from `quran/chunks/chunk-7.txt` unless it is labelled with another translation, in which case it came from `verse.py`. the chunk is almost all meccan (36 to 46) and ends with the first medinan surah of the set (47). that shape matters: this stretch is argument with people who will not listen, so it is thick on proof, on inherited belief, on the tyrant's rhetoric, on the duty to deliver and then let go. it is thin on law.

one axiom of the tool sits in this chunk: 47:24. what sits around it that the maker has not used is noted under theme 1 and in the surprises.

## 1. knowledge and reading

- 39:9 "Say, ‘How can those who know be equal to those who do not know?’ Only those who have understanding will take heed" (the rank of knowing is asserted by the text itself, but note the first half of the verse: the one who knows is the one "who worships devoutly during the night", so knowing is a practice kept up in the dark, not a credential).
- 39:18 "who listen to what is said and follow what is best. These are the ones God has guided; these are the people of understanding" (a reading method in eleven words: take in what is said, all of it, then pick the best; this is how a research shop should consume the world, including its critics).
- 47:24 "Will they not contemplate the Quran? Do they have locks on their hearts" (already an axiom of the tool; the two verses that frame it are unused, see the next two entries).
- 47:16 "once they leave your presence, they sneer at those who have been given knowledge, saying, ‘What was that he just said?’ These are the ones whose hearts God has sealed, those who follow their own desires" (the sneer at knowledge, made once you are out of the room, is named as the mark of the sealed heart; the manifesto can name the sneer directly).
- 47:18 "Its signs are already here, but once the Hour has actually arrived, what use will it be then to take heed" (the signs are available now; reading late is not reading).
- 43:22 "They say, ‘We saw our fathers following this tradition; we are guided by their footsteps.’" and 43:23 "those corrupted by wealth said, in the same way, ‘We saw our fathers following this tradition; we are only following in their footsteps.’" (the second verse is the sharper one: the defenders of the inherited default are named as the affluent; the default is not neutral, it is kept by those it pays).
- 43:24 "‘Even though I bring you a truer religion than what you saw your fathers following?’ and they replied, ‘But we do not believe the message you bring.’" (the refusal is not an argument; it is a refusal, and the text lets it stand bare so the reader can see that).
- 37:69 "They found their forefathers astray" and 37:70 "and rushed to follow in their footsteps" (the verb is rushed; inherited defaults are adopted at speed, without inspection).
- 38:7 "We did not hear any such claim in the last religion: it is all an invention" (the new is rejected for being new; a workshop that ships new things will hear this sentence).
- 36:62 "Did you not use your reason" and 36:68 "If We extend anyone’s life, We reverse his development. Do they not use their reason" (reason is asked for as a duty, not offered as a luxury).
- 40:58 "The blind and the sighted are not equal, just as those who believe and do good works and those who do evil are not equal: how seldom you reflect" (seeing and doing are set side by side as the same kind of inequality).
- 38:29 "This is a blessed Scripture which We sent down to you [Muhammad], for people to think about its messages, and for those with understanding to take heed" (the book states its own purpose as being thought about; a publication that does not want to be thought about is not in this tradition).
- 41:53 "We shall show them Our signs in every region of the earth and in themselves, until it becomes clear to them that this is the Truth." (research points outward, to the regions of the earth, and inward, to the self, in one sentence).
- 45:5 "and in His shifting of the winds there are signs for those who use their reason" (weather as a reasoning exercise; observation is worship).
- 45:18 "Do not follow the desires of those who lack [true] knowledge" and 45:24 "They have no knowledge of this; they only follow guesswork." and 43:20 "they do not know that- they are only guessing" (guesswork is named three times as the opposite of knowledge; the manifesto's word for slop could be guesswork).
- 38:69 "I have no knowledge of what those on high discuss" (the messenger states the limit of what he knows; humility about knowing is put in the mouth of the one with the most authority).
- 46:9 "‘I am nothing new among God’s messengers. I do not know what will be done with me or you; I only follow what is revealed to me; I only warn plainly.’" (three limits stated in one breath: not new, not knowing the outcome, only plain).
- 41:26 "The disbelievers say, ‘Do not listen to this Quran; drown it in frivolous talk: you may gain the upper hand.’" (concealment by noise: the strategy of drowning a signal in chatter is described as a deliberate tactic to gain the upper hand; this is the oldest description of slop I know of).
- 46:29 "When they heard it, they said to one another, ‘Be quiet!’ Then when it was finished they turned to their community and gave them warning" (a listening protocol: silence, hear to the end, then carry it to your people; the jinn are the model readers in this chunk).
- 43:18 "‘Someone who is brought up amongst trinkets, who cannot put together a clear argument?’" (a clear argument is treated as the thing that matters; the verse is the idolaters' contempt for daughters, turned against them).
- 40:83 "When messengers came to them with clear signs, they revelled in the knowledge they had, and so they were engulfed by the very punishment they mocked" (knowledge one revels in is the knowledge that blinds; a warning to a knowledge shop).
- 39:49 "he says, ‘All this has been given to me because of my knowledge’- it is only a test, though most of them do not know it" (crediting your own knowledge for your fortune is named as the test you fail).
- 42:14 "They divided, out of rivalry, only after knowledge had come to them" and 45:17 "They differed among themselves out of mutual rivalry, only after knowledge came to them" (the division came after the knowledge, not before; knowing does not protect a community from splitting, rivalry does the splitting).
- 44:58 "We have made this Quran easy to understand- in your own language [Prophet]- so that they may take heed" (accessibility is stated as design intent; the gate is meant to be low).
- 36:70 "This is a revelation, an illuminating Quran to warn anyone who is truly alive" (the audience is defined as whoever is alive enough to hear).

## 2. proof and truth

- 37:154 "What is the matter with you? How do you form your judgements" 37:155 "Do you not reflect" 37:156 "Do you perhaps have clear authority" 37:157 "Bring your scriptures, if you are telling the truth" (four questions in a row that move from method to reflection to authority to evidence; a checklist for any claim).
- 46:4 "‘Consider those you pray to other than God: show me which part of the earth they created or which share of the heavens they own; bring me a previous scripture or some vestige of divine knowledge––if what you say is true.’" (the demand for proof accepts three kinds: a work, a prior record, or a vestige of knowledge; the manifesto can adopt the same three).
- 40:28 "‘How can you kill a man just for saying, “My Lord is God”? He has brought you clear signs from your Lord- if he is a liar, on his own head be it- and if he is truthful, then at least some of what he has threatened will happen to you." (the secret believer inside pharaoh's court proposes a test: let the claim be judged by its consequences, and do not kill the claimant; this is a defence of dissent and of falsifiability at once).
- 40:5 "every community schemed to destroy its messenger and strove to refute truth with falsehood" (refuting truth with falsehood is the standard move of the incumbent).
- 41:40 "Those who distort the meaning of Our message are not hidden from Us." (distortion is a named offence, distinct from rejection).
- 41:42 "which falsehood cannot touch from any angle" (the standard for a text: falsehood cannot reach it from any side).
- 47:31 "We shall test you to see which of you strive your hardest and are steadfast; We shall test the sincerity of your assertions" (the claim-versus-deed test, seven verses after the axiom 47:24 and unused; assertions get tested for sincerity).
- 47:30 "but you will know them anyway by the tone of their speech" (the tone of speech is evidence; hype has a tone).
- 47:3 "In this way God shows people their true type" (type is shown by what people follow, not by what they say about themselves).
- 36:12 "We record what they send ahead of them as well as what they leave behind: We keep an account of everything in a clear Record" (a ledger of both the outputs and the residue; the record is described as clear).
- 45:28 "Every community will be summoned to its record" and 45:29 "Here is Our record that tells the truth about you: We have been recording everything you do.’" (a record that tells the truth about you, kept continuously; the model for an audit log).
- 43:19 "Did they witness their creation? Their claim will be put on record and they will be questioned about it" (a claim about something you did not witness is still recorded and still answered for).
- 43:80 "Our messengers are at their sides, recording everything" (recording is done at the side of the actor, in real time).
- 39:69 "the Record of Deeds will be laid open; the prophets and witnesses will be brought in. Fair judgement will be given between them: they will not be wronged" (open record, witnesses, fair judgement: the three parts of a verifiable process).
- 36:65 "On that Day We shall seal up their mouths, but their hands will speak to Us, and their feet bear witness to everything they have done" (the hands and feet testify when the mouth is sealed; what was done outlasts what was said).
- 41:21 "‘God, who gave speech to everything, has given us speech" and 41:22 "You thought that God did not know about much of what you were doing" (the skin as witness; the body is a log you cannot silence).
- 40:51 "on the Day when witnesses arise" (witnesses are the mechanism of the final account).
- 46:8 "He is sufficient as a witness between me and you" (the messenger appeals to a witness rather than to his own credibility).
- 43:86 "unlike those who bore witness to the truth and recognized it" (bearing witness and recognizing are paired; you must know a truth to witness it).
- 39:33 "It is the one who brings the truth and the one who accepts it as true who are mindful of God" (truth needs a bringer and an acceptor; both are named as doing the work).
- 47:14 "Can those who follow clear proof from their Lord be compared to those whose foul deeds are made to seem alluring to them" (proof versus allure; the manifesto's enemy is the alluring, not the false alone).
- 38:86 "‘I ask no reward from you for this, nor do I claim to be what I am not" (no fee and no inflated claim, in one line).
- 44:14 "they turned their backs on him, saying, ‘He is tutored! He is possessed!’" (the two dismissals: he was coached, or he is mad; neither engages the claim).
- 43:52 "Am I not better than this contemptible wretch who can scarcely express himself" 43:53 "Why has he not been given any gold bracelets? Why have no angels come to accompany him?’" (pharaoh's test for truth is eloquence and jewellery; status and polish stand in for proof).
- 46:11 "‘If there were any good in this Quran, they would not have believed in it before we did,’" (the elite reject a thing because the ordinary adopted it first; gatekeeping by precedence).
- 39:32 "So who could be more wrong than the person who invents a lie about God and rejects the truth when it comes to him?" (two faults paired: inventing and rejecting).

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship (khalifa), custody

- 38:26 "‘David, We have given you mastery over the land. Judge fairly between people. Do not follow your desires, lest they divert you from God’s path" (haleem hides the word; asad has "We have made thee a [prophet and, thus, Our] vicegerent on earth" and sahih has "We have made you a successor upon the earth", both from verse.py; the arabic is khalifa. this is the one place in the chunk where stewardship is handed to a named person, and the first duty attached is to judge fairly and the first danger is one's own desire).
- 38:24 "[Then] David realized that We had been testing him, so he asked his Lord for forgiveness, fell down on his knees, and repented" (the steward is tested while judging and does not know it until after; custody includes the readiness to find you were wrong).
- 38:39 "‘This is Our gift, so give or withhold as you wish without account.’" (solomon is given discretion over the gift; a trust with freedom in it).
- 43:13 "‘Glory be to Him who has given us control over this; we could not have done it by ourselves" (the rider's prayer over a mount or a ship; the maker's honest posture over any tool that carries him).
- 45:13 "He has subjected all that is in the heavens and the earth for your benefit, as a gift from Him. There truly are signs in this for those who reflect" (everything usable is a gift, and a gift is held in trust).
- 36:35 "It was not their own hands that made all this. How can they not give thanks" (the limits of authorship; the maker did not make the ground he builds on).
- 44:18 "‘Hand the servants of God over to me! I am a faithful messenger who has been sent to you" (the word is amin, faithful, trustworthy; the messenger's claim to be heard rests on trustworthiness).
- 44:25 "Many a garden and spring they left behind" 44:26 "many a cornfield and noble building" 44:27 "many a thing in which they had delighted" 44:28 "We gave these to another people to inherit" (custody passes; nothing built is owned past the term).
- 47:38 "Whoever is grudging is so only towards himself: God is the source of wealth and you are the needy ones. He will substitute other people for you if you turn away, and they will not be like you" (the mission is not owed to any one carrier; if the steward turns away, others are brought in, and they will be different).
- 43:32 "We are the ones who give them their share of livelihood in this world and We have raised some of them above others in rank, so that some may take others into service: your Lord’s grace is better than anything they accumulate" (rank is for service between people, not for accumulation).
- 36:17 "Our duty is only to deliver the message to you,’" and 42:48 "We have not sent you [Prophet] to be their guardian: your only duty is to deliver the message." (the scope of the trust is delivery, not control; a release is a delivery).
- 37:164 "‘Every single one of us has his appointed place" 37:165 "we are ranged in ranks" (custody is distributed; each holds a post).

## 4. craft and excellence

- 39:74 "How excellent is the reward of those who labour" (the last word on paradise in surah 39 is labour).
- 39:35 "God will absolve them even of their worst deeds and will reward them according to their best" and 46:16 "We accept from such people the best of what they do and We overlook their bad deeds." (graded by the best of the work, not the average; permission to ship imperfect things and keep going).
- 46:15 "‘Lord, help me to be truly grateful for Your favours to me and to my parents; help me to do good work that pleases You; make my offspring good." (the prayer at forty asks for good work by name).
- 38:17 "Remember Our servant David, a man of strength who always turned to Us" and 38:20 "We strengthened his kingdom; We gave him wisdom and a decisive way of speaking" (the craftsman-king is given wisdom and decisive speech as his tools).
- 38:32 "he kept saying, ‘My love of fine things is part of my remembering my Lord!’ until [the horses] disappeared from sight" (solomon's love of fine horses is remembrance, not vanity; beauty in craft is allowed to point back to its source).
- 38:37 "and the jinn––every kind of builder and diver" (solomon's workforce is described by craft: builders and divers).
- 37:95 "‘How can you worship things you carve with your own hands" 37:96 "when it is God who has created you and all your handiwork?’" (handiwork is real and is God's; the fault is worshipping it, not making it).
- 37:102 "When the boy was old enough to work with his father, Abraham said, ‘My son, I have seen myself sacrificing you in a dream. What do you think?’" (craft is passed by working alongside, and even a command from a dream is put to the apprentice as a question).
- 40:64 "He shaped you, formed you well, and provided you with good things." (the maker of makers formed well; forming well is the standard).
- 41:10 "measured out its varied provisions for all who seek them" and 41:12 "Such is the design of the Almighty, the All Knowing" (measured provision and design, named as design).
- 44:38 "We were not playing a pointless game when We created the heavens and earth and everything in between" 44:39 "We created them for a true purpose, but most people do not comprehend" (creation is not pointless play; a playful workshop must still have a purpose it can state).
- 46:33 "God, who created the heavens and earth and did not tire in doing so" (endurance in making).
- 37:80 "This is how We reward those who do good" (the refrain of surah 37, repeated at 37:105, 37:110, 37:121, 37:131; the word is muhsinin, those who do ihsan; each prophet's story closes on it).
- 41:33 "Who speaks better than someone who calls people to God, does what is right, and says, ‘I am one of those devoted to God’" (speech is ranked by whether it is joined to doing right).
- 47:33 "believers, obey God and the Messenger: do not let your deeds go to waste" (waste of work is the thing to avoid; the whole of surah 47 keeps returning to deeds coming to nothing).
- 36:41 "Another sign for them is that We carried their seed in the laden Ark" 36:42 "and We have made similar things for them to ride in" (the ark is the model for every later vessel; a good build becomes a pattern).
- 38:44 "‘Take a small bunch of grass in your hand, and strike [her] with that so as not to break your oath.’" (job is shown the gentlest way to keep a hard promise; craft in fulfilment, not just in making).

## 5. justice and balance

- 42:17 "It is God who has sent down the Scripture with Truth and the Balance. How can you tell? The Last Hour may well be near" (the balance is sent down with the book; asad glosses it "a balance [wherewith to weigh right and wrong]"; the manifesto can pair truth and balance as one gift).
- 38:22 "‘Do not be afraid. We are two litigants, one of whom has wronged the other: judge between us fairly- do not be unjust- and guide us to the right path" (a case brought with a request: judge fairly, do not be unjust, guide).
- 38:23 "This is my brother. He had ninety-nine ewes and I just the one, and he said, “Let me take charge of her,” and overpowered me with his words.’" (the parable of consolidation: the one who has ninety-nine wants the one; and the means was words, "overpowered me with his words"; rhetoric as the tool of taking).
- 38:24 "Many partners treat each other unfairly. Those who sincerely believe and do good deeds do not do this, but these are very few." (partnership fairness is rare and named as rare).
- 38:28 "but would We treat those who believe and do good deeds and those who spread corruption on earth as equal?" (the refusal to treat builders and corrupters as equal; false balance is itself an injustice).
- 45:21 "How badly they judge" (a two-word verdict on those who expect the same fate for doing good and doing evil).
- 43:32 "Are they the ones who share out your Lord’s grace?" (the question to anyone who thinks they control distribution).
- 42:27 "If God were to grant His plentiful provision to [all] His creatures, they would act insolently on earth, but He sends down in due measure whatever He will" (abundance breeds insolence; due measure is the design, so more is not the aim).
- 39:47 "If the evildoers possessed the earth’s assets twice over they would offer them to ransom themselves" (all the assets of the earth twice over cannot buy the thing that matters).
- 42:15 "I am commanded to bring justice between you." (the messenger's commission, stated in the first person).
- 42:40 "Let harm be requited by an equal harm, though anyone who forgives and puts things right will have his reward from God Himself––He does not like those who do wrong" 42:41 "There is no cause to act against anyone who defends himself after being wronged" 42:42 "but there is cause to act against those who oppress people and transgress in the land against all justice" (the ceiling on response is equal harm, the better path is forgive and repair, self-defence is protected, and oppression is what one acts against; a complete ethic of pushback in three verses).
- 36:54 "‘Today, no soul will be wronged in the least: you will only be repaid for your deeds" and 40:17 "today no injustice will be done. God is swift in reckoning.’" (the final account is exact).
- 46:19 "Everyone will be ranked according to their deeds and God will repay them in full for what they have done: they will not be wronged" (rank by deeds, paid in full).
- 39:67 "These people have no grasp of God’s true measure." (failing to measure rightly is the root error).
- 43:33 "houses with roofs of silver, sweeping staircases to ascend" 43:34 "massive gates, couches to sit on" 43:35 "and golden ornaments. All of these are mere enjoyments of this life" (the text withholds these from the disbelievers only so that people do not all become one nation of disbelievers; wealth as a sorting error).
- 36:47 "‘Why should we feed those that God could feed if He wanted? You must be deeply misguided.’" (the excuse for not sharing, quoted so the reader can hear how it sounds).
- 41:7 "who do not pay the prescribed alms and refuse to believe in the world to come" (withholding alms is listed with disbelief).

## 6. agency and self-change

- 39:7 "No soul will bear another’s burden. You will return to your Lord in the end and He will inform you of what you have done" (each carries only their own; you will be told what you did, not what was done to you).
- 39:41 "Whoever follows the guidance does so for his own benefit, whoever strays away from it does so at his own peril: you are not in charge of them" (benefit and peril belong to the one who chooses; nobody is in charge of another's choice).
- 41:46 "Whoever does good does it for his own soul and whoever does evil does it against his own soul: your Lord is never unjust to His creatures" and 45:15 "Whoever does good benefits himself, and whoever does evil harms himself" (the same sentence twice in two surahs; the return on good is to the doer first).
- 42:30 "Whatever misfortune befalls you [people], it is because of what your own hands have done- God forgives much" (misfortune traced to one's own hands, and forgiveness in the same breath; ownership without despair).
- 39:53 "My servants who have harmed yourselves by your own excess, do not despair of God’s mercy. God forgives all sins" (the harm is by your own excess, and the door is still open; change starts from where you are).
- 39:15 "‘The true losers are the ones who will lose themselves and their people on the Day of Resurrection: that is the most obvious loss" (the worst loss is loss of self).
- 36:19 "‘The evil omen is within yourselves." (what people blame on the messenger is in themselves).
- 39:39 "‘My people, do whatever is in your power- and so will I. You will find out" (the messenger's challenge: act within your power, I will act within mine, results will show).
- 36:20 "Then, from the furthest part of the city, a man came running. He said, ‘My people, follow the messengers" 36:21 "Follow them: they are not asking you to reward them and they are rightly guided" (one person from the edge of the city runs in and speaks; his test of the messengers is that they ask no fee; change from the periphery).
- 40:38 "‘My people, follow me! I will guide you to the right path" and 40:44 "so I commit my case to God: God is well aware of His servants.’" (the secret believer speaks up in the court and then lets go of the outcome).
- 37:61 "Everyone should strive to attain this" (striving is universal, not for the elect).
- 43:26 "‘I renounce what you worship" 43:27 "I worship only Him who created me, and it is He who will guide me,’" 43:28 "and he bequeathed these words to his descendants so that they might return [to God]" (abraham's self-change is bequeathed as words, a fork others can pick up).
- 37:30 "we had no power over you- and you were already exceeding all limits" 37:32 "We led you astray as we ourselves were astray.’" (the misleaders disown power over the misled; you cannot outsource your ruin to your influencers).
- 40:47 "‘We were your followers, so can you now relieve us from some share of the Fire?’" 40:48 "but they will say, ‘We are all in this together." (following does not transfer responsibility; the haughty and the weak share the outcome).
- 41:23 "so it was the thoughts you entertained about your Lord that led to your ruin" (the thoughts entertained are the cause; what you assume shapes what you do).
- 42:39 "and defend themselves when they are oppressed" (self-defence is listed among the virtues of the believers, beside consultation and giving).

## 7. time and mortality

- 39:30 "You [Prophet] will certainly die, and so will they" (the messenger's own death is stated flatly; nobody is exempt from the term).
- 41:47 "Knowledge of the Hour belongs solely to Him and no crop comes out of its sheath, nor does any female conceive or give birth, without His knowledge." (the hour is unknown to all, but the smallest event, a crop leaving its sheath, is known; the unknown span and the fully known detail in one verse).
- 46:23 "‘Only God knows when it will come: I simply convey to you the message I am sent with" (the messenger disclaims the timetable and keeps to the delivery).
- 46:3 "It was for a true purpose and a specific term that We created heaven and earth and everything in between" (purpose and term go together; a finite span is part of the design).
- 46:35 "it will seem to them that they lingered no more than a single hour of a single day [in this life]" (the whole life compressed to an hour of a day in hindsight).
- 40:67 "then He let you grow old- though some of you die sooner- and reach your appointed term so that you may reflect" (the stages of life end in "so that you may reflect"; the point of the span is reflection).
- 36:68 "If We extend anyone’s life, We reverse his development. Do they not use their reason" (a long life is a reversal, not a gain; urgency without panic).
- 36:50 "They will have no time to make bequests, nor will they have the chance to return to their own people" (the end comes before the bequest can be made; write the will now).
- 39:42 "God takes the souls of the dead and the souls of the living while they sleep- He keeps hold of those whose death He has ordained and sends the others back until their appointed time- there truly are signs in this for those who reflect" (each night is a rehearsal of the end and a return; each morning is a reprieve).
- 47:18 "Its signs are already here, but once the Hour has actually arrived, what use will it be then to take heed" (the signs are present tense).
- 36:40 "The sun cannot overtake the moon, nor can the night outrun the day: each floats in [its own] orbit" and 40:61 "It is God who has given you the night in which to rest and the day in which to see." (time has a rhythm built in: rest and sight, orbit and orbit).
- 45:24 "‘There is only our life in this world: we die, we live, nothing but time destroys us.’" (the creed of the refusers: time as the only destroyer; the manifesto's opposite is a finite span held in trust).
- 47:36 "the life of this world is only a game, a pastime, but if you believe and are mindful of God, He will recompense you. He does not ask you to give up [all] your posses-sions" (this world as a game, and yet possessions are not demanded; hold it lightly, not contemptuously).
- 43:83 "Leave them to wade in deeper and play about, until they face the Day they have been promised" (play about, here, is the refusers' waste of the span; the manifesto's playfulness must be the other kind, purposeful).
- 44:59 "So wait; the disbelievers too are waiting" (both sides wait; the difference is what for).
- 36:44 "Only by Our mercy could they be reprieved to enjoy life for a while" (the span is a reprieve, and it is for a while).

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- 40:26 "‘Leave me to kill Moses- let him call upon his Lord!- for I fear he may cause you to change your religion, or spread disorder in the land.’" (pharaoh calls the reformer the corrupter; "disorder in the land" is the word the text uses for fasad, and the tyrant claims it for his own side; expect to be called the disorder).
- 40:29 "But Pharaoh said, ‘I have told you what I think; I am guiding you along the right path.’" (the tyrant's certainty in the first person; he does not argue, he announces).
- 40:36 "‘Haman, build me a tall tower so that I may reach the ropes that lead" 40:37 "to the heavens to look for this God of Moses. I am convinced that he is lying.’ In this way the evil of Pharaoh’s deed was made alluring to him and he was barred from the right path- his scheming led only to ruin" (the tower is built to disprove, not to learn; engineering in service of a foregone conclusion).
- 43:51 "‘My people, is the Kingdom of Egypt not mine? And these rivers that flow at my feet, are they not mine? Do you not see" 43:54 "In this way he moved his people to accept and they obeyed him- they were perverse people" (ownership of the rivers as the argument; the people are moved by the claim of ownership, and that is the decay).
- 44:31 "he was a tyrant who exceeded all bounds" (the definition of tyranny is exceeding bounds).
- 40:35 "In this way God seals up the heart of every arrogant tyrant" and 40:56 "there is nothing in their hearts but a thirst for a greatness they will never attain" (the tyrant's inner state is a thirst for greatness; the sealed heart is his).
- 41:15 "The people of 'Ad behaved arrogantly throughout the land without any right, saying, “Who could be stronger than us?”" (strength as the only claim).
- 40:82 "They were more numerous than them, stronger than them, and made a more impressive mark on the land, yet what they achieved was of no use to them at all" (scale, strength and footprint did not save them; the impressive mark on the land is the decay's own monument).
- 38:74 "but not Iblis, who was too proud. He became a rebel" 38:76 "‘I am better than him: You made me from fire, and him from clay.’" (the first rebel rebels out of superiority; this is the rebellion the manifesto must not be).
- 38:2 "Yet the disbelievers are steeped in arrogance and hostility" (arrogance and hostility together; the pair that makes rebellion cruel).
- 47:22 "‘If you turn away now, could it be that you will go on to spread corruption all over the land and break your ties of kinship?’" (corruption and broken kinship are one movement).
- 47:29 "Do the corrupt at heart assume that God will not expose their malice" (exposure is promised; the maker's word exposes has a root here).

- 45:8 "who hears God’s revelations being recited to him, yet persists in his arrogance as if he had never heard them" 45:9 "who, if he knows anything about Our revelations, ridicules it!" (hearing and then persisting; knowing a little and ridiculing).

- 39:3 "‘We only worship them because they bring us nearer to God,’" (intermediaries justified as bringing you closer; every platform says this, and so could a workshop that positions itself between people and knowledge).
- 41:26 "drown it in frivolous talk" (the workshop's own output could become the frivolous talk that drowns the signal; publish less than you could).
- 43:83 "Leave them to wade in deeper and play about" and 44:38 "We were not playing a pointless game" (play about is the refusers' pastime; the manifesto that calls itself playful must show the purpose so that its play is not pointless).

- the cloud they thought was rain (46:24): the sign misread by those who wanted it to be something else.
- the jinn who said "Be quiet!" and went home to warn (46:29-31): the model readers.
- locks on the hearts (47:24): already in use.
- rivers of water, milk, wine, honey (47:15): pleasure without shame.
- known "by the tone of their speech" (47:30): tone as evidence.
- the gourd tree over jonah on the barren shore (37:146): shelter after failure.
- ruins passed by "morning" and "and night" (37:137-138): history in plain sight, unread.
- abraham looking up to the stars before he acts (37:88).
- "a single hour of a single day" (46:35): the whole life, in hindsight.

## how the text itself speaks

- sudden reversal marked by a refrain: "lo and behold!" at 36:37, 36:51, 36:53, 36:77, 36:80. the phrase turns a description into a jolt; a manifesto can use one small recurring phrase to mark each turn.
- questions to the reader that do not wait for an answer: 36:62 "Did you not use your reason", 36:68 "Do they not use their reason", 36:73 "Will they not give thanks", 40:58 "how seldom you reflect", 45:23 "Will you [people] not take heed", 47:24 "Will they not contemplate the Quran? Do they have locks on their hearts". the question is the argument.
- a litany that closes each story on the same two lines: 37:80 "This is how We reward those who do good" and 37:81 "he was truly one of Our faithful servants", repeated for noah, abraham, moses and aaron, elijah, each with a "Peace be upon" line (37:79, 37:109, 37:120, 37:130). the roll call is the form; each name gets the same honour in the same words.
- a pivot line that separates the two groups: 37:40 "Not so God’s true servants", 37:74 "Not so the true servants of God", 37:128 "not so the true servants of God". a single sentence that swings the passage from warning to promise.
- history as evidence, asked as a travel question: 40:21 "Have they not travelled through the land and seen how those who lived before them met their end?", again at 40:82 and 47:10. the reader is sent out to look, not told.
- the first-person creed as a chain of "Say": 39:11 to 39:15, each beginning "Say," and stating one commitment. a manifesto can be written as a chain of first-person sayings.
- case comparison as a series: 39:9 "What about someone who worships devoutly during the night", 39:19 "What about the one who has been sentenced to punishment?", 39:22 "What about the one whose heart God has opened in devotion to Him", 39:24 "What about the one who will only have his bare face to protect him". cases laid side by side, judged by contrast.
- starting from common ground: 39:38 "If you [Prophet] ask them, ‘Who created the heavens and earth?’ they are sure to answer, ‘God,’", again at 43:9 and 43:87. the argument begins from what the opponent already grants.
- promise paired with warning in matched pictures: 39:71 the throngs led to hell and its gate, 39:73 the throngs led to the garden and its gate "wide open"; 47:15 the rivers of the garden set against "boiling water to drink that tears their bowels". the text always shows both doors.
- lists of duties as a portrait: 42:36-39, a single sentence naming what the community does, from shunning sins to forgiving when angry to consulting to giving to defending. the list defines the group.
- oaths as openings: 36:2 "By the wise Quran", 37:1 "By those [angels] ranged in rows", 38:1 "By the Quran with its reminding", 43:2 and 44:2 "By the Scripture that makes things clear". the oath commits the speaker before the argument begins.
- direct address to named groups: 36:60 "Children of Adam", 39:16 and 39:53 and 43:68 "My servants", 47:7 "You who believe!", 47:33 "believers". the second person plural is the default register.
- the messenger told his limits in the same words across surahs: 39:41 "you are not in charge of them", 42:6 "you are not responsible for them", 42:48 "your only duty is to deliver the message". repetition across chapters makes a rule.
- afterlife courtroom dialogues where the losers argue among themselves: 37:27-32, 38:59-64, 40:47-50, 41:21-23. the text lets the wrong side speak in full and lose by its own words.
- the tyrant quoted verbatim so that the reader condemns him without a gloss: 40:26, 40:29, 43:51-53. no adjective is added; the quotation does the work.
- the parable announced as a parable: 39:27 "We have put forward all kinds of illustration", 39:29 "God puts forward this illustration". the text tells you when it is drawing a comparison.
- the hypothetical put to the doubter: 41:52 "‘Have you ever thought, what if this revelation really is from God and you still reject it?", 46:10 "‘Have you thought: what if this Quran really is from God and you reject it?". the question invites the reader to run the scenario.
- a question that opens instead of closing: 42:17 "How can you tell? The Last Hour may well be near".
- the one-word answer: 36:58 "“Peace,”", 43:89 "say, ‘Peace’".
- shared openings that make a series: surahs 40 to 46 all begin "Ha Mim"; a house style for a run of publications.
- the shortest sentence in the chunk carrying the largest claim: 36:82 "“Be”- and it is", 40:68 "He says only ‘Be’ and it is".

## name and mark candidates

- mizan, the balance: 42:17 "It is God who has sent down the Scripture with Truth and the Balance." the only explicit balance in the chunk, and it is delivered together with the book. the strongest single candidate here.
- mubin, that makes clear: 43:2 and 44:2 "By the Scripture that makes things clear", 37:117 "the Scripture that makes things clear". al-kitab al-mubin. a mark could be the clear book, or that which makes clear.
- fussilat, made distinct: 41:3 "a Scripture whose verses are made distinct" and 44:4 "a night when every matter of wisdom was made distinct". distinctness as a name for a research method.
- hikma, wisdom: 38:20 "We gave him wisdom and a decisive way of speaking", 43:4 "full of wisdom", 43:63 "I have brought you wisdom", 45:16 "scripture, wisdom, and prophethood". 
- fasl, decision: 44:40 "The Day of Decision", 37:21 "This is the Day of Decision", and 38:20 "a decisive way of speaking" (fasl al-khitab). the criterion that settles; nearest thing to furqan in this chunk.
- nur, light: 39:22 "walks in light from his Lord", 39:69 "The earth will shine with the light of its Lord", 42:52 "We made it a light", 41:12 "beautifully illuminated", 36:70 "an illuminating Quran".
- basa'ir, insights: 45:20 "a means of insight for people" (itani: "an illumination for mankind", from verse.py).
- sabil and sirat, the way and the path: 36:4 "on a straight path", 43:43 "you truly are on the right path", 42:52 "the straight path" and 42:53 "the path of God", 47:1 "the way of God", and the concrete version at 43:10 "traced out routes on it for you to find your way".
- burhan and sultan, proof and authority: 37:156 "clear authority", 44:19 "clear authority", 47:14 "clear proof", 45:17 "clear proof".
- the record: 36:12 "a clear Record", 45:29 "Our record that tells the truth about you", 39:69 "the Record of Deeds".
- witness: 39:69 "the prophets and witnesses will be brought in", 40:51 "the Day when witnesses arise", 46:8 "sufficient as a witness", 43:86 "bore witness to the truth".
- amin, faithful: 44:18 "a faithful messenger".
- shura, consultation: 42:38 "mutual consultation"; the surah is named The Consultation.
- dhikr, reminder: 38:1 "By the Quran with its reminding", 43:44 "a reminder for you and your people".
- the hour: 41:47 "Knowledge of the Hour belongs solely to Him", 43:61 "This [Quran] gives knowledge of the Hour", 46:35 "a single hour of a single day". the hourglass idea is here without the glass.
- the ark and the ship: 36:41 "the laden Ark", 42:32 "ships, sailing like floating mountains", 45:12 "ships sail on it by His command so that you can seek His bounty".
- the letter names as marks: "Ya Sin" (36:1), "Sad" (38:1), "Ha Mim" (40:1 through 46:1), "Ayn Sin Qaf" (42:2). short, strange, unexplained; the text is comfortable with a mark that is not a word.
- qalam, iqra, fitra, sanad: not present in this chunk. the nearest to fitra is 41:11 "‘We come willingly’", the created order consenting to its own making.

## surprises

- 40:26: pharaoh's word for moses is fasad. "I fear he may cause you to change your religion, or spread disorder in the land." the incumbent calls reform corruption, in the same vocabulary the text uses for real corruption. a manifesto that expects to be called the disorder can say so in advance.
- 43:23: the people who say "We saw our fathers following this tradition" are "those corrupted by wealth". the inherited default is kept by the affluent. this is sharper than the famous 43:22 and sits right after it.
- 41:26: "drown it in frivolous talk: you may gain the upper hand." noise as a deliberate suppression tactic, described from the mouth of those using it. slop is a strategy, and it is old.
- 46:11: "If there were any good in this Quran, they would not have believed in it before we did," the elite reject a thing because ordinary people got there first. gatekeeping by precedence, named.
- 43:52-53: pharaoh's objection to moses is that he "can scarcely express himself" and has no "gold bracelets". polish and status as substitutes for proof, and the text answers with "clear authority" (44:19), not with eloquence.
- 38:23-24: the ninety-nine ewes and the one, taken by a man who "overpowered me with his words". consolidation by rhetoric. and david, judging it, finds "We had been testing him". the one who exposes unfairness is being tested by the same case.
- 38:32: "My love of fine things is part of my remembering my Lord!" craft and beauty are not vanity when they point back. a prophet says it about horses.
- 39:35 and 46:16: rewarded "according to their best"; "We accept from such people the best of what they do and We overlook their bad deeds." graded on the best work, not the average. permission to release imperfect things and keep iterating.
- 47:31: "We shall test the sincerity of your assertions", seven verses after the axiom 47:24. the claim-versus-deed test sits next to the verse the tool already uses and has not been used.
- 47:38: "He will substitute other people for you if you turn away, and they will not be like you" the mission is not owed to its current carriers, and the replacements will be different. an antidote to indispensability.
- 40:28: the secret believer in pharaoh's own family proposes a test for moses's claim, "if he is a liar, on his own head be it- and if he is truthful, then at least some of what he has threatened will happen to you", and defends his right to speak. dissent protected from inside the establishment, on evidential grounds.
- 36:20: "from the furthest part of the city, a man came running." the one who speaks up comes from the edge, and his argument for the messengers is that "they are not asking you to reward them" (36:21). trust the ones who do not charge for the message.
- 46:29-31: the jinn hear the recitation, say "Be quiet!", listen to the end, then go home and warn their people. a complete listening protocol in three verses.
- 42:27: "If God were to grant His plentiful provision to [all] His creatures, they would act insolently on earth," abundance is not the aim; due measure is. a business that wants enough, not everything, has a text.
- 43:13: "we could not have done it by ourselves" is the prayer said on a mount or in a ship. the honest posture of a maker over a tool that carries him.
- 40:4: "do not be dazzled by their movements back and forth across the land". busyness and motion are not evidence of anything.
- 37:164 "‘Every single one of us has his appointed place" and 37:165 "we are ranged in ranks". the collective as a set of posts, each held.
- 41:11: the heavens and earth are told "Come into being, willingly or not" and answer "We come willingly". consent is built into the making of the world.
- 39:29: the man with "several partners at odds with each other" as masters. a portrait of divided attention, and of dependence on many services that do not agree.
- 39:12: "I have been commanded to be the first to submit." the leader is the first adopter of the thing he asks others to adopt.
- 37:102: abraham puts a command from a dream to his son as "What do you think?" consultation even where the order is not his to change.
- 38:44: job is shown how to keep an oath with "a small bunch of grass". the gentlest lawful way to honour a hard promise is the one given.
- 44:38 against 47:36: "We were not playing a pointless game" and "the life of this world is only a game, a pastime". play appears with two edges. the maker's playfulness needs a stated purpose so it is the first kind and not the second.
- 47:4 is in this chunk and is a battle verse ("strike them in the neck"), softened by "later you can release them by grace or by ransom". noted so that the manifesto does not draw on surah 47 without knowing what else is there.
