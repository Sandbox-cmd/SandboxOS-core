# notes 4 of 8 · surahs 11 to 17 (hud, joseph, thunder, abraham, al-hijr, the bee, al-isra) · abdel haleem

reader 4. every verse from 11:1 to 17:111 was read in order. quotations are verbatim from chunk-4.txt; anything from outside the chunk is labelled with its translation and came from verse.py.

a note on the maker's ten axioms first. none of the ten (54:17, 96:6-7, 33:72, 21:22, 55:7-9, 2:222, 47:24, 61:2-3, 4:135, 21:47) falls inside surahs 11 to 17. but this chunk holds the working-out of several of them, and those neighbours are what the maker has not used yet:

- 55:7-9 (haleem: "He has set the balance / so that you may not exceed in the balance / weigh with justice and do not fall short in the balance") has its street-level form here in 11:84-85 and 17:35, its cosmic form in 15:19 "made everything grow there in due balance" and 13:8 "everything has its measure with Him".
- 61:2-3 (haleem: "why do you say things and then do not do them") has its positive mirror in 11:88, where shu'ayb says "I do not want to do what I am forbidding you to do, I only want to put things right as far as I can".
- 33:72 (haleem: "We offered the Trust ... mankind undertook it") is lived out in joseph, who is given "our trust and favour" (12:54) and asks for the storehouses (12:55), and in the pledge verses 13:20, 16:91, 16:95, 17:34.
- 4:135 (haleem: "uphold justice and bear witness to God, even if it is against yourselves") has its narrative twin in 12:79-82, where joseph refuses to take a substitute prisoner and the brothers give a witness statement limited to what they saw.
- 21:47 (haleem: "We will set up scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection") has its personal ledger in 17:13-14, "Read your record. Today your own soul is enough to calculate your account."
- 96:6-7 (haleem: "man exceeds all bounds / when he thinks he is self-sufficient") is illustrated by 11:10 "He becomes exultant and boastful", 17:37 on strutting, and 17:83.
- 47:24 (haleem: "Do they have locks on their hearts") has its cousins 16:108 "hearts, hearing, and sight have been closed off" and 17:46 "covers on their hearts".
- 21:22 (haleem: "If there had been in the heavens or earth any gods but Him, both heavens and earth would be in ruins") is restated in 17:42.
- 54:17 (the quran made easy to remember) has 12:2, 14:4, 17:41, 17:89, 17:106 as its explanation of method: own language, many examples, little by little.

## 1. knowledge and reading

- 11:14 "then you will all know that it is sent down containing knowledge from God" · the book describes itself as a container of knowledge, and offers a test (11:13) rather than asking for deference.
- 11:24 "These two groups are like the blind and the deaf as compared with those who can see and hear well: can they be alike? How can you not take heed" · knowing is treated as a sense, like sight, not a possession.
- 11:46 "Do not ask Me for things you know nothing about. I am warning you not to be foolish." and 11:47 "My Lord, I take refuge with You from asking for things I know nothing about." · noah, the builder who was right about the flood, is corrected for speaking one step past his knowledge, and he accepts it; the maker's humility is modelled by the maker of the ark.
- 11:49 "These accounts are part of what was beyond your knowledge [Muhammad]. We revealed them to you. Neither you nor your people knew them before now" · knowledge is received, not owned; repeated at 12:102.
- 11:51 "I ask no reward from you, my people; my reward comes only from Him who created me. Why do you not use your reason" · reason and the unpaid message sit in one breath; the honest teacher asks for nothing and asks you to think.
- 11:62 "Will you forbid us to worship what our fathers worshipped?" with 11:87 and 11:109 "it is merely what their fathers worshipped before them" · in this chunk the inherited default is the standing objection raised against every messenger; the appeal to what the fathers did is the shape of the harmful default.
- 12:7 "There are lessons in the story of Joseph and his brothers for all who seek them" · lessons are for seekers; the story does not force itself.
- 12:22 "When he reached maturity, We gave him judgement and knowledge: this is how We reward those who do good" · knowledge comes as the reward of doing good, not as the entry ticket to it.
- 12:40 "mere names you and your forefathers have invented, names for which God has sent down no sanction" · inherited names without warrant; a definition of a default nobody checked.
- 12:44 "These are confusing dreams and we are not skilled at dream-interpretation" · the court's experts admit they cannot; the knowledge is with a prisoner. gated expertise fails in public.
- 12:76 "We raise the rank of whoever We will. Above everyone who has knowledge there is the One who is all knowing" · rank comes from knowledge, and every knower has a ceiling above them; the two halves of the maker's stance on expertise.
- 12:86 "I have knowledge from God that you do not have" and 12:94-95 "You may think I am senile but I can smell Joseph," / "You are still lost in that old illusion of yours!" · jacob's knowing is called senility by the crowd until the shirt arrives; a knower may look like a fool for years.
- 12:105 "there are many signs in the heavens and the earth that they pass by and give no heed to" · the signs are public; attention is the scarce thing.
- 12:109 "Have the [disbelievers] not travelled through the land and seen the end of those who went before them? ... Do you [people] not use your reason" · travel and history as data; see also 16:36.
- 12:111 "There is a lesson in the stories of such people for those who understand" · the closing line of joseph's surah gives the story to the understanding.
- 13:3 "There truly are signs in this for people who reflect" and 13:4 "there truly are signs in this for people who reason" · the refrain that ends nature verses names the faculty each time.
- 13:19 "Can someone who knows that the revelation from your Lord is the Truth be equal to someone who is blind? Only those with understanding will take it to heart" · knowledge and blindness set as opposites again.
- 13:37 "If you were to follow their desires, after the knowledge that has come to you, you would have no one to guard you" · knowledge obliges; you cannot unknow.
- 14:52 "so that those who have minds may take heed" · the message is addressed to minds.
- 15:53 "We bring you good news of a son who will have great knowledge." · the promised gift to abraham is described by its knowledge, not its power.
- 16:25 "they will bear the full weight of their own burden, as well as some of the burden of those they misled with no true knowledge" · misleading people without knowledge is a debt you carry; a warning for anyone publishing.
- 16:43 "you [people] can ask those who have knowledge if you do not know" · the rank of those who know is a rank to be consulted; then 16:44 "so that you can explain to people what was sent for them, so that they may reflect" closes the chain from knower to explainer to reflecting reader.
- 16:70 "after having knowledge, they will know nothing at all" · knowledge is lent to a body that ages; urgency without vanity.
- 16:74 "So do not make up images about God: God knows and you do not" · against invention dressed as knowledge.
- 16:78 "It is God who brought you out of your mothers' wombs knowing nothing, and gave you hearing and sight and minds, so that you might be thankful" · everyone starts at zero with the same instruments; the ground of an open workshop.
- 17:12 "for you to seek your Lord's bounty and to know how to count the years and calculate. We have explained everything in detail" · counting and calculation named as purposes of day and night.
- 17:14 "Read your record. Today your own soul is enough to calculate your account." · iqra turned on one's own ledger.
- 17:36 "Do not follow blindly what you do not know to be true: ears, eyes, and heart, you will be questioned about all these" · the epistemic duty stated as an audit of three instruments; the anti-slop verse.
- 17:85 "You have only been given a little knowledge." · the answer to a question about the spirit is a limit on knowing.
- 17:107 "those who were given knowledge earlier fall down on their faces when it is recited to them" and 17:109 "[the Quran] increases their humility" · knowledge here produces humility, not pride; the test of real knowing.

## 2. proof and truth

- 11:13 "Then produce ten invented suras like it, and call in whoever you can beside God, if you are truthful." · the book invites a reproduction challenge; it does not ask to be believed on authority. (17:88 repeats the challenge for the whole.)
- 11:17 "those who have clear proof from their Lord, recited by a witness from Him" · proof and witness together.
- 11:18 "the witnesses will say, 'These are the ones that lied about their Lord.'" · liars are named by witnesses, not by mobs.
- 11:53 "you have not brought us any clear evidence. We will not forsake our gods on the strength of your word alone" · the demand for evidence is spoken by the opponents and the text does not mock it; a messenger must expect it.
- 11:54 "I call God to witness, and you too are my witnesses, that I disown those you set up as partners with God" · a public declaration with named witnesses, including hostile ones.
- 11:88 "What if I am acting on clear evidence from my Lord?" · shu'ayb's reform rests on evidence, said as a question.
- 11:93 "Soon you will know who will receive a disgraceful punishment and who is a liar. Watch out, and so will I." · time is the judge; both sides watch.
- 12:18 "they showed him his shirt, deceptively stained with blood. He cried, 'No! Your souls have prompted you to do wrong!" · forged evidence, and a reader who is not fooled by a bloody artifact.
- 12:26 "If his shirt is torn at the front, then it is she who is telling the truth and he who is lying" and 12:27 "but if it is torn at the back, then she is lying and he is telling the truth." · a he-said she-said settled by a physical trace; forensics in scripture.
- 12:50 "Go back to your master and ask him about what happened to those women who cut their hands" and 12:51 "Now the truth is out: it was I who tried to seduce him- he is an honest man." · joseph will not leave prison until the record is cleared; clear the audit before taking the post.
- 12:52 "for my master to know that I did not betray him behind his back: God does not guide the mischief of the treacherous" · the point of the proof is the trust of the one who trusted him.
- 12:81 "We can only tell you what we saw. How could we guard against the unforeseen" and 12:82 "Ask in the town where we have been; ask the people of the caravan we travelled with: we are telling the truth." · a witness statement limited to what was seen, with two independent sources to verify; the shape of an honest report.
- 12:108 "This is my way: based on clear evidence, I, and all who follow me, call [people] to God" · the way is defined by evidence, and it is shared by all who follow; a movement stated in one line.
- 12:111 "This revelation is no fabrication: it is a confirmation of the truth of what was sent before it; an explanation of everything" · continuity with what came before is offered as a mark of truth.
- 13:33 "Say, 'Name them,' or, 'Can you tell Him about something on the earth He does not know to exist, or is this just a display of words?'" · name it, or admit it is only words; a test for hype.
- 13:43 "God is sufficient witness between me and you: all knowledge of the Scripture comes from Him." · the final witness when human ones fail (also 17:96).
- 14:10 "Bring us clear proof then, [if you can]." and 14:11 "True, we are only men like you ... We cannot bring you any proof unless God permits it" · messengers admit the limit of the proof they can bring; honesty about what you cannot show.
- 15:14 "even if We opened a gateway into Heaven for them and they rose through it, higher and higher" and 15:15 "they would still say, 'Our eyes are hallucinating. We are bewitched.'" · no evidence convinces a will set on denial; know when proof is not the missing piece.
- 15:75 "There truly is a sign in this for those who can learn" and 15:76 "it is still there on the highway" and 15:79 "both are still there on the highway, plain for all to see" · the ruins are public evidence you can go and check; open data on the roadside.
- 16:39 "in order to make clear for them what they have differed about and so that the disbelievers may realize that what they said was false" · the resurrection is framed as the final clarification of disputes.
- 16:56 "By God! You will be questioned about your false inventions" · inventions are audited.
- 16:105 "Falsehood is fabricated only by those who do not believe in God's revelation: they are the liars" · fabrication defined as the mark of the liar.
- 16:116 "Do not say falsely, 'This is lawful and that is forbidden,' inventing a lie about God: those who invent lies about God will not prosper" · against issuing rules you cannot back.
- 17:13 "We shall bring out a record for each of them, which they will find spread wide open" · the record is opened, not summarised.
- 17:59 "We gave the people of Thamud the she-camel as a clear sign, yet they maltreated it." · a sign given and abused.
- 17:80 "My Lord, make me go in truthfully, and come out truthfully, and grant me supporting authority from You." and 17:81 "The truth has come, and falsehood has passed away: falsehood is bound to pass away." · truthful entry and exit as a prayer; falsehood as a thing that expires.
- 17:102 "You know very well that only the Lord of the heavens and earth could have sent these signs as clear proof." · moses tells pharaoh he already knows; denial in the face of known proof.
- 17:105 "We sent down the Quran with the truth, and with the truth it has come down" · truth in the sending and in the arrival.

## 3. trust (amana), stewardship (khalifa), custody

- 11:61 "It was He who brought you into being from the earth and made you inhabit it" · the earth is given to inhabit; stewardship stated to the thamud before their fall.
- 11:64 "this camel belongs to God, a sign for you, so leave it to pasture on God's earth and do not harm it, or you will soon be punished." · a living thing in custody; harming it undoes a whole people (11:65-68).
- 11:86 "I am not your keeper." · shu'ayb refuses custody over other adults; the reformer is not a warden.
- 12:11 "Why do you not trust us with Joseph? We wish him well" and 12:64 "Am I to entrust him to you as I did his brother before? God is the best guardian" · a broken trust is remembered at the next request; trust has a history.
- 12:21 "Look after him well! He may be useful to us, or we may adopt him as a son." · custody of a person begins with a promise of care.
- 12:23 "My master has been good to me; wrongdoers never prosper." · joseph guards his master's trust when no one is watching.
- 12:54 "From now on you will have our trust and favour." and 12:55 "Put me in charge of the nation's storehouses: I shall manage them prudently and carefully." · trust is granted first, then the steward asks for a specific custody and states his standard of care; the sequence matters.
- 12:66 "Then, when they had given him their pledge, he said, 'Our words are entrusted to God.'" · a pledge is deposited somewhere above both parties.
- 13:20 "those who fulfil the agreements they make in God's name and do not break their pledges" · keeping agreements is the first mark in the list of those who understand.
- 13:25 "those who break their confirmed agreements made in God's name, who break apart what God has commanded to be joined and who spread corruption on earth" · breaking agreements and spreading fasad are named together.
- 15:21 "There is not a thing whose storehouses are not with Us. We send it down only according to a well-defined measure" · the storehouses belong to God; what we hold is released on measure. joseph's storehouses (12:55) are a copy of these.
- 15:22 "you do not control its sources" · humility of the custodian.
- 15:23 "it is We who inherit [everything]" · every holding returns.
- 16:91 "Fulfil any pledge you make in God's name and do not break oaths after you have sworn them, for you have made God your surety" · God as the guarantor of your word.
- 16:95 "Do not sell for a small price any pledge made in God's name: what God has [to give] is better for you" · a pledge is not for sale; against selling out.
- 17:2 "Entrust yourselves to no one but Me" · the only unconditional trust.
- 17:34 "Do not go near the orphan's property, except with the best [intentions], until he reaches the age of maturity. Honour your pledges: you will be questioned about your pledges" · custody of what is not yours lasts only until its owner can hold it; then it is returned.

## 4. craft and excellence

- 11:7 "so as to test which of you does best" · creation is a test of who does best, not who does most.
- 11:37 "Build the Ark under Our [watchful] eyes and with Our inspiration." · the craft is done in sight and with inspiration; work that God sees, in the imperative.
- 11:38 "So he began to build the Ark, and whenever leaders of his people passed by, they laughed at him. He said, 'You may scorn us now, but we will come to scorn you" · the mockery comes from the leaders, and the builder keeps building; the maker's answer to laughter is the ark.
- 11:41 "Board the Ark. In the name of God it shall sail and anchor." · the launch is named; sailing and anchoring both.
- 11:88 "He Himself has given me good provision: I do not want to do what I am forbidding you to do, I only want to put things right as far as I can. I cannot succeed without God's help" · the repairer's creed: put things right as far as I can, earn honestly, do not exempt yourself.
- 11:115 "Be steadfast: God does not let the rewards of those who do good go to waste" · good work is not wasted even when it looks lost.
- 12:47 "You will sow for seven consecutive years as usual. Store all that you reap, left in the ear, apart from the little you eat" · a technical instruction inside scripture: leave the grain in the ear so it keeps.
- 12:59 "Have you not seen me giving generous measure and being the best of hosts" · the steward's excellence is measured in generosity and hospitality.
- 12:90 "God does not deny anyone who is mindful of God and steadfast in adversity the rewards of those who do good." · ihsan under adversity.
- 13:4 "all watered with the same water, yet We make some of them taste better than others: there truly are signs in this for people who reason" · same inputs, different quality; craft is the difference.
- 13:17 "like the froth that appears when people melt metals in the fire to make ornaments and tools: in this way God illustrates truth and falsehood- the froth disappears, but what is of benefit to man stays behind" · the smith's fire as the picture of truth; what is of benefit stays.
- 15:82 "They carved out dwellings in the mountains, and lived in security" and 15:84 "What they had gained was of no use to them" · skill alone does not save; craft without justice is rock that falls.
- 16:6 "you find beauty in them when you bring them home to rest and when you drive them out to pasture" · beauty noticed in working animals at the end of the day.
- 16:14 "bring out jewellery to wear; you see the ships cutting through its waves so that you may go in search of His bounty" · ornament and vessel both given.
- 16:68 "And your Lord inspired the bee, saying, 'Build yourselves houses in the mountains and trees and what people construct" · the small builder is given inspiration and told to build, including inside human structures.
- 16:80 "from the skins of animals made you homes that you find light [to handle] when you travel and when you set up camp; furnishings and comfort for a while from their wool, fur, and hair" and 16:81 "garments to protect you from the heat, and garments to protect you in your wars. In this way He perfects His blessings on you" · materials, tents, garments; making is counted among perfected blessings.
- 16:90 "God commands justice, doing good, and generosity towards relatives" · ihsan is a command, listed second after justice.
- 16:96 "We shall certainly reward those who remain steadfast according to the best of their actions" and 16:97 "reward them according to the best of their actions" · reward is scaled to one's best, said twice in two verses.
- 17:19 "if anyone desires the life to come and strives after it as he should, as a true believer, his striving will be thanked" · striving thanked, not only rewarded.
- 17:55 "We gave David a book [of Psalms]" · david's gift in this chunk is a book; craft and text together.
- 17:106 "it is a recitation that We have revealed in parts, so that you can recite it to people at intervals; We have sent it down little by little" · a release cadence: in parts, at intervals, little by little.

## 5. justice and balance

- 11:84 "Do not give short measure nor short weight. I see you are prospering, but I fear you will have torment on an overwhelming Day" · prosperity and short measure appear together; the profitable default is the one being questioned.
- 11:85 "My people, in fairness, give full measure and weight. Do not withhold from people things that are rightly theirs, and do not spread corruption in the land" · corruption is defined here as withholding what is rightly others'.
- 11:87 "refrain from doing whatever we please with our own property? Indeed you are a tolerant and sensible man." · the short-weighers' defence is property rights, and they flatter the reformer while refusing him.
- 11:111 "Your Lord will give everyone full due for whatever they have done" · full due as the divine standard for measure.
- 11:116 "while the unjust pursued the enjoyment of plenty, and persisted in sin" · plenty pursued at others' cost is the pattern of ruin.
- 12:20 "and then sold him for a small price, for a few pieces of silver: so little did they value him" · a person priced as goods; the extraction economy in one line.
- 12:79 "God forbid that we should take anyone other than the person on whom we found our property: that would be unjust of us." · joseph refuses a convenient substitute even when the accused offer it themselves.
- 12:88 "We have brought only a little merchandise, but give us full measure. Be charitable to us: God rewards the charitable." · the poor ask for full measure and charity together.
- 13:8 "everything has its measure with Him" · measure as a divine attribute.
- 13:17 "He sends water from the sky that fills riverbeds to overflowing, each according to its measure." · abundance with measure.
- 14:31 "before a Day comes when there will be no trading or friendship" · a day when the market closes.
- 15:19 "made everything grow there in due balance" · mizan in the earth itself.
- 16:71 "Those who have been given more are unwilling to pass their provision on to the slaves they possess so that they become their equals. How can they refuse to acknowledge God's blessings" · the refusal to make equals is named as ingratitude; the gatekeeper's sin.
- 16:76 "can he be considered equal to one who commands justice and is on the straight path" · justice commanded as the mark of the able man.
- 16:90 "God commands justice, doing good, and generosity towards relatives and He forbids what is shameful, blameworthy, and oppressive. He teaches you, so that you may take heed" · the three commanded and the three forbidden; a charter verse.
- 16:92 "just because one party may be more numerous than another" · do not break a trust because the other side is bigger.
- 16:111 "every soul will be paid in full for all its actions- they will not be wronged" · the final full measure.
- 16:126 "If you [believers] have to respond to an attack, make your response proportionate, but it is best to stand fast" · proportion in response; the constructive limit on rebellion.
- 17:16 "We command those corrupted by wealth [to reform], but they [persist in their] disobedience" · wealth-corruption is the named cause of a town's end.
- 17:26 "Give relatives their due, and the needy, and travellers- do not squander your wealth wastefully" · due before waste.
- 17:29 "Do not be tight-fisted, nor so open-handed that you end up blamed and overwhelmed with regret" · balance between hoarding and giving away.
- 17:33 "he should not be excessive in taking life, for he is already aided [by God]" · even the wronged party is bound by proportion.
- 17:35 "Give full measure when you measure, and weigh with accurate scales: that is better and fairer in the end" · the scales verse of this chunk, with "in the end" as the horizon.
- 17:100 "If you possessed the very stores of my Lord's bounty, you would hold them back in your fear of spending: man is ever grudging." · hoarding is diagnosed as fear.

## 6. agency and self-change

- 11:35 "If I have made this up, I am responsible for my own crime, but I am innocent of the crimes you commit." · own your own errors and no one else's.
- 11:93 "My people, do whatever is within your power, and I will do likewise. Soon you will know" · parallel effort, judged by time.
- 11:101 "We did not wrong them; they wronged themselves." · repeated at 16:33 and 16:118; the cause of ruin is placed inside.
- 11:121 "Do whatever you can: we too are doing what we can," and 11:122 "and 'Wait: we too are waiting.'" · the calm answer to opponents: we are also working.
- 12:53 "I do not pretend to be blameless, for man's very soul incites him to evil unless my Lord shows mercy" · joseph, vindicated, still refuses to claim purity.
- 12:67 "But I cannot help you against the will of God: all power is in God's hands. I trust in Him; let everyone put their trust in Him," · jacob takes a precaution (different gates) and also names its limit; act and trust together.
- 13:11 "God does not change the condition of a people [for the worse] unless they change what is in themselves" · in haleem's reading, with the bracket, this is a warning: a people's condition does not worsen until they change inside. the manifesto can still use it, but honestly: the change starts inside, in both directions.
- 14:22 "I had no power over you except to call you, and you responded to my call, so do not blame me; blame yourselves." · the tempter has only a voice; the responding was ours. this is the algorithm's defence and the user's agency in one sentence.
- 14:44 "Did you [disbelievers] not swear in the past that your power would have no end" · the illusion of permanent power.
- 15:42 "you will have no power over My servants, only over the ones who go astray and follow you" · power over you requires your following.
- 16:93 "You will be questioned about your deeds" · deeds, not intentions or affiliations.
- 16:110 "those who leave their homes after persecution, then strive and remain steadfast" · leaving, striving, staying: three verbs of agency.
- 17:7 "whether you do good or evil it is to your own souls" · consequence lands on the actor.
- 17:13 "We have bound each human being's destiny to his neck." and 17:15 "Whoever accepts guidance does so for his own good; whoever strays does so at his own peril. No soul will bear another's burden, nor do We punish until We have sent a messenger" · the burden is personal, and no one is judged before being told.
- 17:64 "Rouse whichever of them you can with your voice, muster your cavalry and infantry against them, share their wealth and their children with them, and make promises to them- Satan promises them nothing but delusion" and 17:65 "but you will have no authority over My [true] servants" · the whole apparatus of pressure is voice, force, money and promises, and it has no authority over the steady.
- 17:84 "Say, 'Everyone does things their own way, but your Lord is fully aware of who follows the best-guided path.'" · each acts by disposition; the judgment of which way is best is not ours to make.

## 7. time and mortality

- 11:3 "He will grant you wholesome enjoyment until an appointed time" · enjoyment is real and bounded.
- 11:104 "We are delaying it only for a specified period" · the delay is not indefinite.
- 12:107 "or that the Last Hour will not come upon them suddenly when they least expect it" · the unknown hour.
- 13:2 "He has subjected the sun and the moon each to pursue its course for an appointed time" · even the sun has a term.
- 13:26 "though they may revel in the life of this world, it is but a fleeting comfort compared with the Life to come" · fleeting is the word for comfort.
- 13:38 "There was a Scripture for every age" · each age gets what it needs.
- 15:4 "Never have We destroyed a community that did not have a set time" and 15:5 "no community can bring its time forward, nor delay it" · every community has a span; also 16:61.
- 15:85 "the Hour will certainly come, so [Prophet] bear with them graciously" · the certainty of the end produces graciousness, not panic; the exact temperament the maker asked for.
- 15:99 "worship your Lord until what is certain comes to you" · death is called "what is certain".
- 16:1 "God's Judgement is coming, so do not ask to bring it on sooner." · urgency without haste.
- 16:70 "Some of you will be reduced, in old age, to a most abject state, so that, after having knowledge, they will know nothing at all" · the window for knowing closes before the window for living.
- 16:77 "The coming of the Hour of Judgement is like the blink of an eye, or even quicker" · the hour as a blink.
- 16:96 "What you have runs out but what God has endures" · the finite against the durable.
- 17:11 "man is ever hasty" · haste is the human fault, not urgency.
- 17:12 "to know how to count the years and calculate" · time given to be counted.
- 17:51 "Say, 'It may well be very soon" and 17:52 "you think you have stayed [on earth] only a little while.'" · on the last day, life will seem short.

## 8. corruption and decay (fasad)

- 11:10 "He becomes exultant and boastful" · comfort after harm turns quickly to boasting.
- 11:19 "who hinder others from God's path, trying to make it crooked" · making the path crooked is the act of corruption; repeated at 14:3.
- 11:27 "it is clear to see that only the vilest among us follow you. We cannot see how you are any better than we are." · the elite's argument against noah is class contempt.
- 11:59 "followed the command of every obstinate tyrant" · a people falls by following, not only by leading.
- 11:91 "we find you very weak in our midst. But for your family, we would have stoned you, for you have no great status among us." · status is the corrupt society's only measure of a speaker.
- 11:97 "they followed Pharaoh's orders, and Pharaoh's orders were misguided" · the plainest statement of institutional obedience.

## how the text itself speaks

- the sign refrain. eight nature passages end by naming a faculty: "for people who reflect" (13:3), "for people who reason" (13:4), "for those who reflect" (16:11), "for those who use their reason" (16:12), "for those who take it to heart" (16:13), "for people who listen" (16:65), "for those who think" (16:69), "for those who believe" (16:79). a manifesto could end its sections the same way, each time naming what the reader is being asked to do.
- the "say" line. 13:16 gives the speaker five "Say" commands in one verse; 17:88-96 is a run of them. the text scripts a voice rather than describing a position.
- questions to the reader. "How can you not take heed" (11:24); "Why do you not use your reason" (11:51); "Do you [people] not use your reason" (12:109); "Can He who creates be compared to one who cannot create? Why do you not take heed" (16:17); "Are the blind equal to those who can see? And are the depths of darkness equal to the light?" (13:16).
- oaths. "By your life [Prophet]" (15:72); "by your Lord, We will question them all" (15:92); and characters swearing "By God!" (12:73, 12:85, 12:91, 12:95).
- the challenge. "produce ten invented suras like it" (11:13); "Even if all mankind and jinn came together to produce something like this Quran, they could not produce anything like it, however much they helped each other." (17:88). an invitation to test rather than a demand to trust.
- parallel doing. "Do whatever you can: we too are doing what we can," (11:121) "and 'Wait: we too are waiting.'" (11:122); "do whatever is within your power, and I will do likewise ... Watch out, and so will I." (11:93). a calm two-line answer to opposition.
- refrains of ruin. "as though they had never lived and flourished there" (11:68) and "as if they had never lived and flourished there" (11:95); "so away with the Ad" (11:60), "so away with the Thamud" (11:68), "away with the people of Midian" (11:95). the same sentence closes each story, so the pattern is heard.
- the duty list. 17:22-39 is a numbered block of commands: no other god, parents, kin and needy, no waste, moderation, no killing children for fear of poverty, no adultery, no wrongful killing, orphans' property, pledges, full measure, do not follow what you do not know, do not strut. it opens with "Set up no other god" and closes with "this is some of the wisdom your Lord has revealed to you" (17:39). a manifesto could carry a block like this.
- the portrait by clauses. 13:20-22 describes the understanding as a series of "who ..." lines: fulfil agreements, join what should be joined, are in awe, remain steadfast, keep up prayer, give secretly and openly, repel evil with good.
- promise paired with warning. "If you are thankful, I will give you more, but if you are thankless, My punishment is terrible indeed." (14:7); "tell My servants that I am the Forgiving, the Merciful" (15:49) "but My torment is the truly painful one" (15:50); 17:9-10.
- "God presents this illustration" (16:75), "God presents another illustration" (16:76), "God presents the example of a town" (16:112). the text labels its parables as parables.
- the text explains its own method: "do you not see how God makes comparisons?" (14:24); "We have explained things in various ways" (17:41); "revealed in parts ... little by little" (17:106).
- one long story. surah 12 is a single narrative with dialogue, dramatic irony ("You will tell them of all this [at a time] when they do not realize [who you are]!" 12:15), reversal, and a closing moral (12:111). it shows that a founding text can carry one story end to end.
- brackets of address: "[Prophet]", "[People]" (17:66), "[Muhammad]". the reader is addressed as a single person even inside a general text.
- purpose stated to the messenger: "We have told you the stories of the prophets to make your heart firm" (11:120). the stories are for steadiness, not information.

## surprises

- 13:11 in haleem reads "God does not change the condition of a people [for the worse] unless they change what is in themselves". the bracket flips the popular reading; here it is a warning that decline begins inside. a manifesto that quotes it as a self-help promise would be quoting against this translation. it still holds that change starts inside, which is the point that matters.
- 11:116 is the quiet mission statement: the societies that fell lacked "people with a rem-nant of good sense, to forbid corruption on the earth", and the cause of the fall was that "the unjust pursued the enjoyment of plenty". a small remnant of sense that says no to corruption is what the text says was missing.
- 11:87: shu'ayb's opponents call him "a tolerant and sensible man" and defend short weight with "refrain from doing whatever we please with our own property?" the text puts the property-rights argument in the mouth of the cheats and lets the reader hear it.
- 11:88: "I do not want to do what I am forbidding you to do, I only want to put things right as far as I can." the positive form of 61:2-3, spoken by a trader-prophet. the maker has the negative axiom but not this one.
- 12:44: the king's counsellors admit "we are not skilled at dream-interpretation"; the answer is with a prisoner. the gated expertise fails in public and the outsider holds the knowledge.
- 12:50: joseph will not walk out of prison on a pardon; he sends the messenger back to reopen the case of the women. the audit before the appointment.
- 12:55: joseph names his own job and its standard: "I shall manage them prudently and carefully." a steward may ask for custody and say how he will hold it.
- 12:62: joseph puts the brothers' payment back in their bags without telling them. a picture of a tool that gives back what you paid, and brings you back by kindness rather than lock-in.
- 12:81-82: the brothers' report is a model witness statement: only what was seen, an admission of the unforeseen, and two independent places to check.
- 13:17: froth on the flood and on molten metal. "the froth disappears, but what is of benefit to man stays behind". the exact image for generated slop against durable work, in a verse about truth and falsehood.
- 14:22: the tempter's defence at the end: "I had no power over you except to call you, and you responded to my call, so do not blame me; blame yourselves." 17:64 lists his tools: voice, cavalry, infantry, shared wealth, promises. this is the recommendation engine described from the inside, and the responsibility placed back on the one who clicked.
- 14:37: abraham founds his line in "an uncultivated valley" and prays "Make people's hearts turn to them". starting in barren ground and asking for an audience is scriptural.
- 15:88: "lower your wings over the believers". a leader's posture is a bird covering its own, not a standard raised.
- 16:43-44: "ask those who have knowledge if you do not know" is followed at once by the messenger's duty to "explain to people what was sent for them, so that they may reflect". knowledge is meant to be asked for, then explained, then reflected on; a three-step pipeline.
- 16:68: the bee is told to build "in the mountains and trees and what people construct". an inspired maker is allowed to build inside other people's structures.
- 16:71: those given more "are unwilling to pass their provision on to the slaves they possess so that they become their equals". the refusal to make equals of those below you is named as a refusal of God's blessing. this is the gatekeeper verse.
- 16:92: breaking a trust "just because one party may be more numerous than another" is unpicking your own spun thread. against following the bigger side.
- 16:106: speech under compulsion does not count; "their hearts remain firm in faith". what a system forces you to say is not what you hold.
- 16:96-97: reward is "according to the best of their actions", twice. you are judged by your best work, not your average.
- 16:126: "make your response proportionate, but it is best to stand fast". the limit on rebellion stated as a rule of engagement.
- 17:14: "Read your record. Today your own soul is enough to calculate your account." the first word of revelation applied to one's own log; the tool's approve ledger is the picture.
- 17:36: the duty of knowledge is audited by three instruments, "ears, eyes, and heart", and the command is not to follow what you do not know to be true. this is the anti-slop rule in the middle of a household law list.
- 17:73-74: even the prophet "would almost have inclined a little towards them" when tempted to soften the message for friendship. the pull to compromise for acceptance is treated as real, not as weakness of lesser people.
- 17:106: the book describes its own release method: "in parts", "at intervals", "little by little". iteration is how the text says it was made.
- 17:110: the middle way applied to volume of voice. a rule for tone.
- 15:16: the sky was "made ... beautiful for all to see". beauty for everyone is a stated purpose of creation, not a luxury.
