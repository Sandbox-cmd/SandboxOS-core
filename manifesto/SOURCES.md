# sources: the reading under the manifesto

the maker asked for the whole quran to be read before a word of the manifesto was written. this
page says how that was done, what it found, theme by theme, and where the human traditions and the
form of the manifesto itself were studied. every quotation from the text is the abdel haleem
translation unless marked, and every one was machine-checked against the file it came from.

## how the reading was done

- four english translations were fetched as complete texts: abdel haleem (primary), muhammad asad,
  talal itani, and sahih international. all four hold 6,236 verses. a helper prints any verse in
  all four so a rendering could be cross-read.
- the primary text was split on surah boundaries into eight chunks of about 18,000 words each.
  eight readers each read one chunk line by line, with a shared brief naming sixteen themes drawn
  from the maker's own words, four further sections (images, the text's own devices, name words,
  surprises), and a coverage line.
- every reader reported the first and last verse read, the verse count, and the stretches that gave
  nothing to the themes, and those honest gaps are recorded in each reader's notes under
  `research/`. in total, 6,236 verses were read. roughly one verse in twelve gave nothing to the
  sixteen headings, which is a finding and never a failure.
- the readers quoted only from their chunk files, verbatim, and their quotations were checked by
  script against the source. where a reader wanted a verse outside its chunk, it ran the helper and
  labelled the translation it used, so the label travels with the quote.
- the ten axioms the tool already stands on (54:17, 96:6-7, 33:72, 21:22, 55:7-9, 2:222, 47:24,
  61:2-3, 4:135, 21:47) were each located, and each reader noted what sits around them unused.

the full notes are in `research/quran/notes-1.md` through `notes-8.md` (about 110,000 words), and
a compressed pick list by chunk is in `research/quran/picks-by-chunk.md`. what follows is the
digest that fed the manifesto.

## the digest, by theme

each line: the verse, the words, and what it gave the page.

### knowledge, reading, and the inherited default

- 96:1-5 "Read! ... who taught by [means of] the pen who taught man what he did not know". the
  first command is read; knowledge is taught, and so owed onward.
- 2:170, 5:104, 7:28, 43:22-23 the argument from the forefathers, refused every time, and 43:23
  names who keeps it: "those corrupted by wealth". the inherited default is kept by those it pays.
- 20:63 the reformer accused of ending "your time-honoured way of life". a default defends itself
  as tradition.
- 17:36 "Do not follow blindly what you do not know to be true: ears, eyes, and heart, you will be
  questioned about all these". the audit of three instruments.
- 6:116 "If you obeyed most of those on earth ... They follow nothing but speculation". the
  majority is no evidence; the average is a guess.
- 39:18 "who listen to what is said and follow what is best". a reading method.
- 12:76 "Above everyone who has knowledge there is the One who is all knowing". every knower has a
  ceiling; 18:22 "stick to what is clear"; 21:109 "I do not know".
- 20:114 "do not rush to recite before the revelation is fully complete but say, ‘Lord, increase me
  in knowledge!’". finish the reading before you publish.
- 34:46 "stand before God, in pairs or singly, and think". a research method in nine words.
- 62:5 "like asses carrying books". an archive that never ships, which is the fate this whole page
  exists to avoid.

### proof, truth, and the record

- 2:111 "‘Produce your evidence, if you are telling the truth.’" the four words for the wall.
- 61:2-3 "why do you say things and then do not do them". the axiom, and 47:31 seven verses after
  another axiom: "We shall test the sincerity of your assertions".
- 2:282 the debt written by a scribe "justly", with witnesses called in and "the time it falls
  due", because "this way is more equitable in God’s eyes, more reliable as testimony, and more
  likely to prevent doubts". the record with witnesses and a due date.
- 2:282 again: "No scribe should refuse to write" and "let no harm be done to either scribe or
  witness". the recorder is a protected role.
- 2:181-182 tampering with a bequest is guilt; repairing a mistaken one is blameless.
- 5:106-108 the witness protocol, with its reason written in: perjured witnesses replaced by two
  from the harmed side, "or fear that their oaths might be refuted by others afterwards".
- 49:6 "check it first, in case you wrong others unwittingly"; 24:11-16 the rumour protocol: each
  repeater "charged with the sin he has earned"; 27:27-28 solomon tests the hoopoe's report.
- 7:116-118 fakery works "on people’s eyes, striking fear into them"; the staff "devoured their
  fakery". a working demonstration beats a narrative.
- 6:93 "‘I too can reveal something equal to God’s revelation’". the generated must never claim to
  equal the revealed.
- 17:14 "Read your record. Today your own soul is enough to calculate your account."

### trust, custody, and permission

- 33:72 the trust offered and undertaken; 4:58 "return things entrusted to you to their rightful
  owners"; 6:89 a failed custodian's trust is "entrusted to others". custody can be forked.
- 24:27-28 "do not enter other people’s houses until you have asked permission ... If you find no
  one in, do not enter ... If you are told, ‘Go away’, then do so". the owner's key as household
  law; an empty house is a no.
- 7:123, 20:71, 26:49 "‘How dare you believe in Him before I have given you permission?’". the
  gatekeeper's voice, three times, always pharaoh's.
- 12:54-55 joseph: trust granted, then "Put me in charge of the nation’s storehouses: I shall
  manage them prudently and carefully"; 12:62 he returns the brothers' payment in secret.
- 28:26-28 "a strong, trustworthy man is the best to hire"; a stated term; "if you complete ten,
  it will be of your own free will"; "I do not intend to make things difficult for you"; "God is
  witness to what we say". the hiring standard and the fair contract.
- 33:70 "speak in a direct fashion and to good purpose", two verses before the trust.

### craft, and work seen in the act

- 10:61 "whatever work you [people] are doing, We witness you when you are engaged in it"; 9:105
  "Take action! God will see your actions". the ground of craft as worship.
- 34:10-11 "We softened iron for him / saying, ‘Make coats of chain mail and measure the links
  well.’ ‘Do good, all of you, for I see everything you do.’"; 34:13 "Work thankfully".
- 21:80 "We taught him how to make coats of mail for the benefit of you [people], to protect
  you". knowledge is armour, and armour is for the many.
- 67:2 "which of you does best"; 39:35, 46:16, 16:96-97, 29:7 rewarded "according to the best of
  their actions". graded on the best work, which is permission to ship and iterate.
- 13:17 "the froth disappears, but what is of benefit to man stays behind". slop against durable
  work, in the smith's fire.
- 11:37-48 the ark: built "under Our [watchful] eyes", laughed at by leaders, "it shall sail and
  anchor"; 54:13 "a vessel of planks and nails"; 36:41-42 the ark, then "similar things for them
  to ride in". a build that becomes a class.
- 38:32 solomon: "‘My love of fine things is part of my remembering my Lord!’"; 7:32 adornment
  defended, extravagance the one limit.
- 67:3-4 "Look again! ... And again!". making that survives inspection.
- 18:94-98 dhul-qarnayn refuses tribute, "if you lend me your strength", runs the forge, tests the
  wall against scaling and piercing, and says at completion it will be razed.
- 2:267 "bad things that you yourself would only accept with your eyes closed". the quality bar
  for anything released.

### justice, measure, and extraction

- 55:7-9 the balance; 57:25 "the Scripture and the Balance ... We also sent iron, with its mighty
  strength and many uses". text, measure, and tooling in one shipment.
- 7:85-86 "Give full measure and weight ... do not cause corruption in the land after it has been
  set in order" then "Do not sit in every pathway, threatening and barring". gatekeeping charged
  beside short measure, by the prophet of merchants.
- 83:1-3 short measure: full for me, less for you.
- 2:276 "God has allowed trade and forbidden usury"; 30:39 gain "through other people’s wealth
  will not increase". the line between a business and an extraction.
- 59:7 "so that they do not just circulate among those of you who are rich"; 16:71 those given more
  "are unwilling to pass their provision on ... so that they become their equals".
- 6:138 "‘These cattle and crops are reserved, and only those we allow may eat them’- so they
  claim!". gatekeeping in the text's own sarcasm; 35:2 "No one can withhold the blessing God opens
  up for people".
- 34:14 the workers who kept labouring for a dead king. systems that keep you paying.
- 26:41-42 "‘Shall we be rewarded if we win?’ / ‘Yes, and you will join my inner court.’". expertise
  paid in access; against five prophets, "I ask no reward of you".
- 31:6 "pays for distracting tales, intending, without any knowledge, to lead others from God’s way,
  and to hold it up to ridicule". the slop economy in one verse; 41:26 "drown it in frivolous talk:
  you may gain the upper hand". noise as a strategy.
- 54:28 a commons: "each one should drink in turn"; 68:17-32 the garden ruined by one plan, "‘Make
  sure no poor person enters the garden today!’", and repaired by hope.
- 4:49, 4:53, 4:77, 4:124 wrong measured in the husk, groove, fibre, and dip of a date stone.

### agency, and the first rebellion

- 13:11 in haleem: "God does not change the condition of a people [for the worse] unless they
  change what is in themselves". quoted honestly, it is a warning as much as a promise; 8:53 is
  the favour side. the change starts inside, both ways.
- 2:195 "do not contribute to your destruction with your own hands"; 25:18 "pleasures in this life,
  until they forgot Your Reminder and were ruined". comfort, then forgetting.
- 14:22 "I had no power over you except to call you, and you responded to my call, so do not blame
  me; blame yourselves"; 17:64 the tools: voice, cavalry, infantry, shared wealth, promises. the
  algorithm from the inside.
- 4:97 "‘was God’s earth not spacious enough for you to migrate to some other place?’", with 4:98
  exempting the truly helpless; 9:25 "the earth seemed to close in on you despite its spaciousness".
- 5:23 two men: "‘Go in to them through the gate’" while the many say "we will stay here".
- 19:25 mary must shake the palm though the stream is given. grace provides, agency collects.
- 53:39 "man will only have what he has worked towards"; 92:4-10 the way is smoothed in the
  direction taken.
- 34:31-33 the oppressed and the oppressors, and neither is let off.

### time

- 2:36 "On earth you will have a place to stay and livelihood for a time"; 6:2 "a term [for you]
  and another fixed time, known only to Him"; 31:34 "no soul knows in what land it will die".
- 103:1-3 "By the declining day man is [deep] in loss except for those who believe, do good deeds,
  urge one another to the truth, and urge one another to steadfastness".
- 25:45-46 the sundial: "the sun its indicator ... little by little". the text's hourglass.
- 73:20 "He knows that you will not be able to keep a measure of it and He has relented towards
  all of you". the anti-burnout verse; 78:9-11 sleep, night as cover, day for livelihood.
- 17:11 "man is ever hasty"; 21:37 "created hasty". the text praises speed toward good and names
  haste as the human fault, so fast is fine and hasty is the thing to watch.
- 18:98 the builder names the wall's expiry at completion.
- 15:85 "bear with them graciously". the temperament the certain hour produces.

### the shape of corruption

- 2:11-12 "‘We are only putting things right,’ but really they are causing corruption, though they
  do not realize it". the false reformer speaks the reformer's own words; 18:104 efforts "misguided,
  even when they think they are doing good work"; 35:8 the corrupt who think themselves good.
- 40:26 pharaoh calls moses the one who will "spread disorder in the land". expect to be called the
  disorder.
- 7:148, 20:88-89 the calf: "a mere shape that made sounds like a cow ... it did not speak to them
  or guide them in any way", cast from the people's own jewellery.
- 29:41 the spider's house, "the frailest of all houses".
- 36:8-9 iron collars up to the chin and barriers before and behind. the posture of the stuck.
- 59:19 "those who forget God, so God causes them to forget their own souls: they are the rebellious
  ones". the other kind of rebellion.
- 96:6-7 "man exceeds all bounds when he thinks he is self-sufficient", bracketed by reading and by
  96:9-10, "the man who forbids [Our] servant to pray".
- 28:4 pharaoh "divided the people into different groups". divide, then oppress.
- 114:4-5 "the slinking whisperer". a name for an algorithm.

### community, and the stranger at the door

- 2:251 "If God did not drive some back by means of others the earth would be completely corrupt";
  22:39-40 pushback keeps "monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques" standing. pushing back is
  the order, and it is plural.
- 11:116 "If only there had been ... people with a rem-nant of good sense, to forbid corruption on
  the earth! ... while the unjust pursued the enjoyment of plenty". the mission verse.
- 3:187 "‘Make it known to people; do not conceal it’"; 2:146 the concealers "know it as well as
  they know their own sons". the pledge to publish, and a portrait of who breaks it: the ones who
  know best.
- 9:122 a group goes out "to gain understanding ... so that they can teach their people when they
  return". a research collective.
- 26:111-114 noah: "‘I will not drive believers away’", and "What knowledge do I have of what they
  used to do". the door stays open, the past unvetted.
- 5:48 "We have assigned a law and a path to each of you ... so race to do good". plurality by
  design, a verse for forks; 42:13 "do not divide into factions".
- 3:159 "had you been harsh, or hard-hearted, they would have dispersed and left you ... Consult
  with them". gentleness is structural.
- 49:11-13 forbids jeering ("who may after all be better than them"), nicknames, spying and
  backbiting, and says the tribes were made "so that you should recognize one another".
- 58:11 the rank of knowledge announced inside "make room for one another in your assemblies".
- 8:26 "Remember when you were few".
- 2:60 "Twelve springs gushed out, and each group knew its drinking place". one strike, many
  outlets, and no toll.

### warmth, and the manner of rebellion

- 16:126 "make your response proportionate, but it is best to stand fast"; 22:60 "merely with its

- 5:2 "Do not let your hatred for the people who barred you from the Sacred Mosque induce you to

- 19:14, 19:32 john and jesus praised as "not domineering or rebellious", "not ... domineering or

- 38:74-76, 7:12 the first rebel: "‘I am better than him’".
- 9:14-15 "remove the rage from their hearts". a rebellion that keeps its rage is unfinished.
- 20:44 "Speak to him gently", to pharaoh; 19:47 abraham's "Peace be with you" to a father who
  threatened stoning; 25:63 "walk humbly on the earth ... reply, ‘Peace’".
- 15:88, 17:24, 26:215 "lower your wing"; 31:18-19 "Go at a moderate pace and lower your voice".
- 7:154 "When Moses’ anger abated, he picked up the Tablets".
- 93:6-11 the one who was found in need and made self-sufficient is told: "So do not be harsh with

- 80:1-10 the frown: the messenger himself rebuked for courting "the self-satisfied one" and

### play, and its counterfeit

- 21:16-17 "We did not create the heavens and the earth and everything between them playfully";

- 9:64-65 "‘We were just chatting, just amusing ourselves.’" the joke as cover; 83:29-33 winking
  and in-jokes as the manners of the wicked.
- 2:26 "God does not shy from drawing comparisons even with something as small as a gnat". small

- 20:18 the staff: "‘I lean on it; restrain my sheep with it; I also have other uses for it.’"
- 27:19 solomon smiles at the ant; 27:44 the hall paved with glass that changes a queen's mind.
- 21:58-63 abraham leaves the biggest idol standing: "‘Ask them, if they can talk.’" the text's own

- 52:40, 68:46 "Do you [Prophet] demand a payment from them that would burden them with debt". no

## the text's severe side, named

a manifesto that leans on the book's mercy should say where it is choosing its emphasis. the
readers found, and this page records: 9:5, 9:29, 9:73 and 47:4 are fighting verses tied in the
text to broken oaths and first aggression, and bounded by 8:61 ("if they incline towards peace,
you [Prophet] must also incline towards it"), 9:6 (safe passage to hear), 9:4-7 and 60:8 (justice
to anyone who has not fought you). 24:2 and 66:9 carry a stern register, and 111 curses an enemy
by name once. 48:29 pairs in-group compassion with out-group harshness, and 33:36 limits
"freedom of choice" on a matter already decided. 71:26 has noah, after every method failed, pray his refusers
away. the manifesto's "never cruel" is therefore argued from 41:34, 42:40, 16:126, 22:60, 5:2 and
60:8, and never by pretending the other verses are absent. the maker's own reading is the one that
governs here, as the maker asked; this page only refuses to hide the material.

two counter-notes the readers flagged for the maker's words in particular: 19:14 and 59:19 use
"rebellious" for the wrong side, so the manifesto names the object of its rebellion in every
tenet where the word appears; and 21:16, 6:32 and 47:36 use "play" and "game" for what the
manifesto refuses, so play is defined on the page as trying, uncounted, and earnest.

## the human traditions

the full study is `research/traditions-study.md` (about 7,000 words, from memory, with every
quotation marked verbatim, paraphrase, or uncertain). what the manifesto took:

- **constructive anarchism.** kropotkin's mutual aid as an empirical claim; colin ward's anarchy as
  "a seed beneath the snow" (verbatim), already present in how people cooperate; landauer's state
  as a relationship replaced by other relationships (paraphrase); james c. scott's legibility and
  metis; elinor ostrom's eight principles for a governed commons, above all that those affected
  may change the rules. how it differs from the destructive, the punk and the libertarian right:
  the alternative already exists in embryo, and the work is to build, document and federate it.
- **convivial tools.** ivan illich's convivial tool (anyone can use it, as often as they wish, for
  a purpose they choose), his two watersheds, and radical monopoly as the precise name for a
  harmful default; schumacher's intermediate technology; mumford's democratic technics; winner's
  artifacts with politics; ursula franklin's whole-process against prescriptive technologies, which
  "breed a culture of compliance"; postman's questions to ask of any new tool.
- **access to tools.** the whole earth catalog's subtitle (verbatim) and listing criteria; ted
  nelson's "You can and must understand computers NOW" (verbatim); engelbart's augmentation and
  bootstrapping; papert's child as epistemologist and the microworld; bret victor's immediate
  connection; the four freedoms and copyleft; "Cypherpunks write code." (verbatim); aaron swartz's
  "Information is power. But like all power, there are those who want to keep it for themselves."
  (verbatim); local-first software's seven ideals.
- **craft as devotion.** ruskin's roughness as the workman's freedom; morris's three hopes and
  "Have nothing in your houses that you do not know to be useful, or believe to be beautiful."
  (verbatim); yanagi's unknown craftsman; christopher alexander's pattern language as the form a
  blueprint takes; sennett's craft as doing a job well for its own sake; ihsan (16:90, 55:60,
  67:2) and God's own itqan (27:88) as the scriptural warrant, with the two most quoted work-ethic
  lines marked as hadith.
- **the islamic knowledge tradition.** the translation movement of eighth to tenth century baghdad
  as a two-century, multi-faith research culture sustained by many small patrons (with the
  house-of-wisdom academy marked as thin in the sources); ibn al-haytham's seeker after truth who
  suspects the ancients and submits to demonstration (paraphrase); the ikhwan al-safa, an anonymous
  tenth-century collective publishing an encyclopaedia under one name; ibn khaldun's asabiyya and
  the decay cycle; al-jazari's book of ingenious devices as a blueprint set meant to be rebuilt;
  isnad and ijaza as a chain of custody for claims, which is what a receipt is; waqf as a locked
  principal funding a public good past its founder; sadaqa jariya (hadith) as knowledge that keeps
  benefiting after the giver is gone, which is what a blueprint that forks is; timbuktu's family
  libraries and scholar networks as a distributed institution with no campus.
- **the diagnosis.** wu, zuboff, doctorow (enshittification, adversarial interoperability), lanier,
  carr; five publishers with more than half of indexed papers (2015 study), open access and its own
  capture by processing charges; the ai moment as a skills divide before it is a money divide, with
  the harness layer where the gate is being built.
- **the palantir question, in plain words.** an ontology layer is a machine-readable map of what an
  organisation has, how things relate, and what actions are allowed on them; a harness is the loop,
  tools, memory, permissions, logs and tests around a model. they concentrate power through
  switching cost, encoded policy, and vendor-held expertise, and none of that is a technical
  necessity. the public version, from the precedents (schema.org, wikidata, openstreetmap, the
  semantic web's failure, open harnesses and the model context protocol): readable files,
  local-first, a receipt on every object and link, a licence to fork, permissions in plain
  language, and patterns a small group can adopt a part of. the honest counter is ostrom's: the
  integration labour is a commons problem before it is a code problem.

the three ideas that clash with the maker's values, named so the manifesto refuses them: "we are
as gods" (the manifesto's posture is a servant who works well); autonomy as exit (the manifesto's
autonomy is kropotkin's, grounded in mutual aid); and the unknown, unpaid craftsman (the manifesto
feeds its maker, puts receipts on work, and says it is a business).

## the form

the full study is `research/genre-study.md` (about 4,500 words, twenty-three manifestos). the
rules the draft was held against: declarative present tense; "we" with a visible "i"; each tenet
in three parts (what it refuses, what it does instead, what that looks like in a tool); every
claim breakable in practice, with a table of what a stranger would point at; concrete nouns; one
enemy named as a mechanism and then left alone; a line that fits on a wall; an ending on the
practice or the call and never on the founder; the source of the faith named once, as origin and
never as argument; a licence stated in one sentence; a rule on who may amend the page (ostrom);
"community", "family", "tribe" and "movement" kept off the page. the study's length target is 500
to 900 words, so the manifesto ships in two layers: `WALL.md` at under two hundred words, and
`MANIFESTO.md` as the page, at about 2,000 words of prose plus the reading table.
